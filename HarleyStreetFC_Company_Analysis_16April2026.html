Executive Summary

A 24-year-old specialist fertility clinic — not a football club — incorporated in 2002, now 90% owned by APTIS Holdings Ltd, generating MUR 67.3M in revenue with strong gross margins and backed by one of the world's most experienced fertility physicians; a highly specialised medical entity requiring an adapted EOS approach that respects clinical governance.

Important Clarification — Entity Name and Nature
The company searched as "Harley Street FC" (File No. C39923) is Harley Street Fertility Centre (Mauritius) Ltd — a private fertility clinic offering IVF, ICSI, IUI, and reproductive medicine services. The "FC" abbreviation stands for "Fertility Centre", not "Football Club." No football club or sports entity was found under the "Harley Street" search on MNS. Harley Street Fertility Centre (Mauritius) Ltd was incorporated on 4 February 2002 and operates as a specialist infertility diagnosis and treatment centre at Georges Guilbert Avenue, Floréal, Curepipe, Mauritius. With Dr Rajat Goswamy FRCOG as Medical Director — a physician with 43+ years of fertility expertise who helped establish IVF centres at King's College London and trained under IVF pioneer Dr Patrick Steptoe at Bourn Hall Clinic — the centre brings world-class reproductive medicine credentials to the Mauritius healthcare market. Treatments include IVF, ICSI, IUI, laser-assisted hatching, PGD, laparoscopic surgery, and embryo freezing.

Ownership structure (FY2024): APTIS Holdings Ltd holds 123,426 shares (90.0%) and The Harley Trust holds 13,714 shares (10.0%), totalling 137,140 ordinary shares (stated capital MUR 13,714,000). The board comprises four directors: Sabbi Appanna Dev Anand and Jacques Vaulbert de Chantilly (both appointed 25 August 2021), Dr Rajat Krishan Goswamy (since 11 August 2003), and Anne Régine de Ravel de l'Argentière (since 7 November 2023). Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Rising infertility rates globally and in Mauritius — delayed parenthood, lifestyle factors, and increased awareness of fertility treatment options are expanding the addressable patient population for clinics like HSFC.
  • Medical tourism growth in the Indian Ocean region — Mauritius is increasingly positioning itself as a medical tourism destination; HSFC's international-quality clinical team and English/French bilingual service capability positions it well for regional patient attraction.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Medical entities require adapted EOS application: clinical governance remains physician-led; EOS tools apply to the administrative, commercial, and management layers. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

EOS Adaptation Note for Medical Entities
EOS in a medical clinic must explicitly separate the clinical governance domain (physician authority — Dr Goswamy) from the administrative and business management domain (Accountability Chart, Scorecard, Rocks). Any EOS engagement framing that appears to subordinate clinical decisions to a business management framework will be rejected by a medical director of Dr Goswamy's stature. The correct framing is: "EOS runs the business of the clinic so the clinical team can focus entirely on patient outcomes." This distinction is critical to EOS adoption success in a healthcare entity.

The administration costs of MUR 33.5M at average Mauritius medical professional salary levels suggest approximately 30–60 staff (clinical + administrative). This places it within the EOS sweet spot. The clinical dimension is well-managed; the business management dimension is the growth constraint.

The hospitaliy-background clinic manager (Comarmond) likely brought service process discipline, but EOS-style management cadences are almost certainly absent.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

A fertility clinic dealing with deeply personal patient journeys — helping couples conceive — develops a distinctive culture of empathy, patience, and clinical excellence. Dr Goswamy's 43-year career in fertility medicine suggests a team culture built around medical excellence and patient outcomes. Decision-making at HSFC operates on two distinct tracks: clinical decisions (Dr Goswamy's exclusive domain) and business decisions (board / Aptis / clinic manager). The ambiguity between these tracks — who decides on marketing spend? who approves new services? who owns pricing? — is likely unresolved without explicit Accountability Chart clarity. Aptis's 90% ownership gives it formal authority, but Dr Goswamy's clinical authority and the trust structure's 10% holding creates a governance complexity that requires careful navigation. Overall Rating: Moderate — Viable with Healthcare-Adapted EOS Framing

HSFC is a viable EOS candidate, but the healthcare context requires a carefully adapted approach. The strongest EOS entry is through Sabbi Appanna (Aptis Group CFO and board member) and Francesca Comarmond (Clinic Manager) — not Dr Goswamy, who must be positioned as the clinical authority whose work the EOS framework supports. Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Harley Street Fertility Centre

Accountability Chart (Clinical vs. Administrative)

The most important EOS output for HSFC is a clear Accountability Chart that explicitly separates the clinical governance structure (Dr Goswamy as Medical Director, Dr Vashista as physician, clinical nursing/embryology team) from the administrative management structure (Clinic Manager, patient services, billing, marketing). This chart resolves the decision-rights ambiguity that currently exists between board, medical director, and operations.

Scorecard (Business Metrics Only)

HSFC's Scorecard should track business metrics: weekly patient consultations, treatment cycle starts, treatment completion rate, revenue per cycle, patient satisfaction score, referral source tracking, and insurance/third-party payer revenue %. Explicitly exclude clinical outcome metrics (pregnancy rates, live birth rates) from the Scorecard — these belong in clinical governance, not the business Scorecard. This distinction is critical for physician acceptance of EOS.

These are very different strategies. Jean-Pierre Mamet's accessibility mission is currently an aspiration without a documented implementation plan.
