Executive Summary

A 16-year-old professional services firm specialising in corporate secretarial, trust, accounting, and immigration services, recently acquired by Aptis Holdings and now navigating a founder-exit leadership transition under new CEO Julien Rey — a profitable, cash-generative business at a pivotal inflection point.

Navitas Corporate Services Ltd was incorporated on 16 September 2008 and operates from Navitas House, Robinson Road, Floréal, Mauritius. With approximately 99 employees and a 15+ year track record serving domestic and international clients — from SMEs to large listed firms — Navitas is one of the established corporate services providers in Mauritius. Services span company formation, secretarial administration, accounting, trust administration, immigration, and global business (offshore). The company is licensed by the Financial Services Commission of Mauritius.

Ownership is held entirely by Navitas Holdings Ltd (10,000 shares, 100%), which is itself the parent structure through which the Aptis Group has consolidated its stake. The acquisition process was formalised in 2023 when Aptis and founder Julien Hoareau reached a strategic agreement, with Hoareau stepping down in early 2025. Julien Rey — co-founder and former COO — was appointed CEO in March/April 2025, providing continuity while the new ownership drives growth. Beatrice (Natalie) Gallet remains as Director and Partner/Executive Director. Sabbi Appanna Dev Anand (Aptis Group CFO) joined as Director on 6 April 2023, representing the Aptis ownership interest. BDO audits the company.

Financially, FY2024 shows a healthy professional services business: revenue of MUR 28.3M (zero cost of sales — pure fee income), administration costs of MUR 17.3M (61% of revenue), and net profit of MUR 8.9M (31.6% net margin). Total assets MUR 10.0M, total equity MUR 1.5M (reflecting a high-payout or low-capital model typical of professional services firms), and long-term provisions of MUR 4.7M (employee obligations). No charges registered. EOS suitability scores 6.0/10 — a viable candidate post-transition, with Julien Rey as the primary EOS conversation partner.

Key Finding — Aptis Acquisition Transition Creates EOS Window
Navitas is in the classic post-acquisition leadership transition phase: founder departed, new CEO installed, majority shareholder (Aptis) with growth ambitions for offshore expansion. This is precisely the moment when EOS delivers maximum value — a new CEO needing to establish authority and operational cadence, a business that has operated on informal founder-centric systems for 16 years, and a strategic owner (Jean-Pierre Mamet) who expects systematic performance improvement. The EOS conversation should be positioned as the "operating system installation" that Julien Rey can make his own — differentiating his leadership from his predecessor's personal model.

Structural Competitive Advantages

15+ years of client relationships: In corporate services, client stickiness is very high — companies rarely switch secretarial providers due to the complexity of migrating statutory records and relationships. Navitas's 15-year client book represents significant recurring revenue with low churn risk.

FSC licensure and regulatory standing: The FSC licence is a significant barrier to entry for competitors. Navitas's clean regulatory track record and established FSC relationship is a genuine competitive advantage that new entrants cannot replicate quickly.

Captive Aptis Group client base: Since the acquisition, Navitas serves as company secretary to multiple Aptis entities (TNS Consumer, Harley Street FC, and likely others). This captive base provides revenue floor certainty and cross-entity cost efficiency — a structural advantage unavailable to independent competitors.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Africa-focused investment gateway growth — Mauritius's double taxation agreement network with African nations continues to attract foreign investors channelling capital into Africa through Mauritius vehicles, driving demand for global business services.
  • Increased compliance complexity — FATF requirements, beneficial ownership registers, and enhanced due diligence obligations are increasing the workload and expertise required for corporate services — benefiting established, experienced providers like Navitas over smaller competitors.
  • Technology disruption risk — Automated secretarial and accounting platforms (e.g. Complyor, Diligent) are commoditising basic company secretarial work. Navitas must move up the value chain toward advisory and offshore services to protect margin.
  • Increased cross-border wealth structuring demand — African high-net-worth individuals and family offices increasingly use Mauritius for wealth structuring — creating opportunities in trust and captive insurance services where Navitas has existing capability.
  • Competition from Big-4 and regional firms — BDO, KPMG, and MCB Corporate Services compete in the premium end of the market. Navitas's mid-market positioning and personal service model differentiates it, but price pressure exists in the commodity secretarial segment.

EOS's emphasis on "document and optimise" processes should be applied immediately to client relationship documentation — ensuring every key client's situation, contacts, and ongoing obligations are captured in a format the team can manage without the founder.
