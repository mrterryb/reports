Executive Summary

Mauritius's oldest advertising agency — incorporated in 1929, operating since 1924 — holds a prestigious Ogilvy Worldwide affiliation and genuine market leadership, yet is currently technically insolvent with accumulated losses of MUR 5.75M wiping out shareholder equity.

Maurice Publicite Limited (File C443, BRN C07000443) is the operating entity behind what the market knows as "Maurice Publicité Ogilvy" or "Moris Pub Ogilvy." It is one of Mauritius's pioneer advertising agencies, tracing its origins to 1924, formally incorporated as a private domestic company on 7 May 1929. The company is headquartered at Old Reduit Road, Bon Air, Moka — having moved its registered office address in September 2025. It operates across the full spectrum of marketing communications: brand advertising, digital strategy, media buying, event management, corporate branding, and creative design. Its Ogilvy Worldwide affiliation (since 1995/1996) grants access to a global network of 500+ specialist firms and proprietary brand tools including the "360 Degree Brand Stewardship" framework.

Ownership is tightly held between two shareholders, each holding exactly 150,000 ordinary shares (50/50): Neubert Geraldine and Neubert Peter Staver. This is a family-controlled business with no external equity investors. The company's secretary is KBA Investment Ltd (appointed September 2021), a corporate services provider based in Port Louis. The company's service address for director Neubert Anne Geraldine is care of ABAX Corporate Administrators, a major Mauritius trust and corporate governance firm — indicating she may be operationally positioned through a formal governance structure.

Financially, the FY2025 results (year ended 30 June 2025) reveal a business with strong gross margins (60.6%) but dangerously thin net margins (1.4%). Revenue of MUR 35.1M is absorbed almost entirely by administration costs of MUR 22.5M — the agency's primary cost line, which at 64.3% of revenue points to a heavy staffing and overhead burden. The most critical financial finding is negative shareholder equity of MUR -1.67M caused by accumulated retained losses of MUR 5.75M. The company has technically consumed all its capital and is operating in negative equity territory. The EOS suitability score of 5.5/10 reflects real opportunity — classic SME complexity and family ownership patterns — but the financial fragility must be acknowledged in any conversation.

Key Finding — Competition Law Breach & Reputational Risk
In August 2023, the Mauritius Competition Commission found Maurice Publicité Limited, alongside Circus Advertising Company Ltd and P&P Link Co. Ltd, guilty of collusive conduct under Section 41 of the Competition Act 2007. The three agencies, through their jointly-controlled media buying entity Touchpoint Ltd, had agreed to restrict the supply of media space to clients of competing agencies. This regulatory action is a significant reputational and legal event that signals governance vulnerabilities in the company. It is also a direct signal of the kind of market-control instinct that EOS Accountability Charts and defined Roles & Responsibilities are designed to channel productively rather than destructively.

Charge Register

One charge is registered against Maurice Publicite Limited: Volume CH1447/18, dated 16 August 1996, filed 27 September 1996. Nature: Floating charge over movable and immovable property (MOV/IMMOV). Amount: MUR 1,500,000. Currency: Mauritius Rupee. This charge is approximately 30 years old and predates the company's Ogilvy affiliation (1995–1996). The floating nature means it covers a class of assets rather than specific property, and it has not been discharged from the CBRIS record.

Charge Register — Interpretation
The 1996 floating charge of MUR 1.5M remains on record and has never been formally discharged. While charges of this vintage may simply reflect historical bank facilities that were rolled into successor arrangements, an undischarged charge from 30 years ago is a governance housekeeping issue. The charge predates the Ogilvy affiliation and was likely related to initial agency expansion financing. Combined with current long-term borrowings of MUR 4.7M, the company has sustained a debt-financed operating model throughout its modern history.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Ogilvy Worldwide Network Access: The 1995/1996 affiliation with Ogilvy gives Maurice Publicite access to proprietary brand frameworks (360 Degree Brand Stewardship), global case studies, training resources, and the Ogilvy brand's international credibility. For clients with regional or international aspirations, this affiliation is uniquely valuable and not replicable by any local-only competitor. No other Mauritius agency holds a major international network affiliation of this calibre.

2. Century-Long Institutional Relationships: Having pioneered advertising in Mauritius in 1924, Maurice Publicite holds relationships with clients and institutions that predate most of its competitors by decades. This includes established positions with major Mauritius conglomerates and familiarity with the public sector's communication procurement processes. Institutional memory and trust are genuine competitive moats in a small-market context.

3. Full-Stack Capability Under One Roof: Unlike specialist boutiques, MPO offers the complete marketing communications stack — strategy, creative, digital, media, events, packaging — with a single client relationship. For mid-to-large Mauritius businesses that want a single agency partner, this breadth is genuinely differentiated.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Digital advertising growth: Mauritius digital ad market projected to reach USD 56.3M by 2028 (6.76% CAGR 2024–2028) — a direct tailwind for any agency that builds genuine digital capabilities, but a threat to those that do not adapt.
  • AI and automation disruption: AI is reshaping content creation, media planning, and campaign optimisation in 2025–2026. Agencies without AI capability pipelines risk becoming cost-inefficient relative to nimble digital boutiques. Ogilvy network gives MPO access to AI tools their competitors lack.
  • Mauritius ICT sector growth: The broader Mauritius ICT/services economy is growing — USD 156M revenue by 2025 with 5.4% CAGR to 2030 — creating new B2B clients who need professional marketing communications as they scale.
  • Competition Act consequences: The 2023 collusion finding against MPO, Circus, and P&P Link may reshape the media buying landscape. If Touchpoint Ltd is unwound or restructured, MPO must develop independent media planning and buying capabilities — a significant operational transition.
  • Short-video and social media dominance: Mauritius consumer audiences are increasingly engaged on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and Facebook — requiring creative capabilities that traditional agency models did not need. This is an area where digital-native competitors have a head start.
  • Regional market opportunity: Mauritius agencies with credible international affiliations (particularly Ogilvy) are well-positioned for African mainland mandates as regional businesses seek sophisticated communications support. MPO's SADC and World Bank track record is an underexploited asset.
Notable Gap — No Active Digital Presence for a Digital Agency
Maurice Publicite sells digital strategy and communication services to its clients, yet its own digital presence is effectively absent. This is a credibility gap that any prospective client, competitor, or business partner will notice immediately. Addressing this is both a commercial priority and a prerequisite for any serious conversation about digital service growth.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 6 / 10

ZoomInfo and directory data indicate 11–50 employees — squarely in the EOS sweet spot of 10–250. However, at the lower end of this range, the complexity may be modest. The company runs multiple service lines (creative, digital, media, events, strategy) across a small team — creating interdependency complexity that EOS handles well. The JV arrangement with Touchpoint Ltd adds a layer of structural complexity (a separate entity to manage). Administration costs of MUR 22.5M on MUR 35.1M revenue suggest a relatively heavy overhead structure for the team size, implying inefficiency or role ambiguity rather than genuine complexity. Score reflects adequate size with some complexity — ideal EOS candidate in terms of headcount but not yet at peak complexity.

02 — Leadership Openness 5 / 10

Mixed signals. Positive: Geraldine Neubert has a documented track record of innovation — the Ogilvy affiliation in 1995, the Touchpoint JV concept, the digital transformation push, and the 90th anniversary innovation conference in 2014 all suggest an entrepreneurially open leader. Peter Neubert's role as SME Mauritius Ltd President shows willingness to engage with business improvement frameworks. Negative: the competition law finding signals a leadership culture willing to bend rules for competitive advantage — a mindset that EOS's Accountability Chart and IDS (Issues, Discuss, Solve) approach can channel, but which requires genuine openness to change. The 50/50 ownership split is the key risk — EOS requires a clear Integrator/Visionary structure, and two equal owners rarely make this easy to install without tension.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 7 / 10

Classic EOS-addressable pain points are present and measurable. The accumulated losses of MUR 5.75M, the DPO of 331 days, the DSO of 108 days, and the negative equity all signal a business that has grown in revenue scale (MUR 35M is meaningful for Mauritius) but has not built the operational discipline to capture profit from that scale. The competition law breach is a direct symptom of unclear accountability for strategic decisions — nobody owned the decision to restrict media space via Touchpoint, or if they did, it was not reviewed against documented values or guardrails. The company appears to be at the "ceiling" growth stage EOS is designed for — technically capable, brand-strong, but leaking cash and value through operational chaos.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 4 / 10

Low score reflects the evidence of weak process discipline. A DPO of 331 days means the accounts payable process is dysfunctional — either invoices are not being tracked or payments are being deliberately deferred without a formal policy. A DSO of 108 days suggests the client invoicing and collections process lacks enforcement. Administration costs at 64.3% of revenue suggest that overhead management lacks a systematic review process. The 30-year-old undischarged floating charge on CBRIS points to gaps in legal and compliance process management. The company's dormant digital presence (website, social media) for a digital agency suggests internal processes for maintaining the firm's own brand are absent or unowned.

05 — Company Culture 5 / 10

The agency has a genuine brand story — 100 years of existence, Ogilvy affiliation, and a history of industry innovation create a cultural foundation. Geraldine Neubert's public profile as an innovative agency leader likely creates some cultural energy around creativity and market leadership. However, the competition law finding raises a direct culture question: was the Touchpoint arrangement a knowing breach, or a governance failure where nobody checked? Either answer is a culture signal. Long-tenured staff at a 100-year-old agency often creates complacency alongside experience. The absence of any visible values statement, public team culture content, or structured people practices (beyond annual return compliance) suggests culture is informal rather than intentional.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 4 / 10

The 50/50 ownership structure is the defining constraint on decision-making. In a company where two shareholders must agree on everything strategic, and where neither has a casting vote, decisions are either fast (one person dominates) or paralysed (genuine disagreement). The Touchpoint JV — a three-way commercial arrangement that ended in a legal finding — suggests decisions were made without adequate legal review or documented approval processes. The absence of a clearly documented Managing Director with defined operational authority (vs. Geraldine's MD title) versus Peter's director role creates ambiguity about who owns what decisions day-to-day. Low score is appropriate given structural constraints.

07 — Vision Clarity 4 / 10

No public Vision/Traction Organizer equivalent is visible. The company's public positioning oscillates between heritage (100 years, Mauritius pioneer) and innovation (digital, Ogilvy tools, regional expansion) without a clear synthesis of where it is going and why. The competition law finding suggests the business may have been pursuing market control (via Touchpoint's media space restriction) as an implicit strategy — but this was never a legitimate documented vision. The financial trajectory — negative equity, accumulated losses, thin margins — suggests the company has been surviving rather than executing against a defined 3-year or 10-year target picture. EOS's V/TO tool would be transformative here.

08 — Meeting Culture 3 / 10

No evidence of structured meeting rhythms in any public or CBRIS material. Annual returns are filed on time — the bare minimum of governance compliance — but there is no indication of a Level 10 Meeting discipline, quarterly review process, or annual planning cycle beyond what compliance requires. The DPO of 331 days is itself evidence of absent operational review meetings — if a weekly or monthly leadership team meeting reviewed aged payables, this number would have been addressed years ago. An advertising agency's creative culture often runs on client-driven reactive meetings rather than leadership-team proactive ones. This dimension represents one of the highest-value EOS installation opportunities.

The conditions are: (1) both Neubert shareholders must genuinely agree on installing a governance framework, which the 50/50 structure makes non-trivial; (2) the competition law finding must be acknowledged as a governance failure, not a legal technicality — it is a signal that accountability structures are broken; and (3) the financial fragility (negative equity, DPO crisis) must be stabilised before EOS implementation consumes leadership bandwidth. The score would move to 7/10 if the ownership question and financial triage are resolved first.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Maurice Publicite

Accountability Chart

The most urgent EOS tool for this company. The 50/50 ownership between Geraldine and Peter Neubert creates structural ambiguity about who owns what. The Accountability Chart forces the company to define a clear Visionary/Integrator split and document every seat's five key responsibilities. This is the prerequisite for everything else.

Scorecard & Measurables

DSO of 108 days and DPO of 331 days would not persist in a company with a weekly Scorecard. Installing 10–15 weekly measurables — including receivables aging, cash position, and billable hours — creates the visibility to catch cash crises before they become structural. The most immediate financial benefit of EOS for this business.

Level 10 Meeting

An advertising agency running on client-reactive rhythms with no structured weekly leadership meeting is a business running on instinct alone. Level 10 Meetings would provide the discipline to review the Scorecard, resolve Issues, and hold people accountable. Given the evidence of absent process discipline (DPO, DSO, dormant website), this meeting rhythm would be transformative within 90 days of installation.

V/TO (Vision/Traction Organizer)

The company has a powerful heritage and an Ogilvy network but no visible 3- or 10-year documented vision. The V/TO would force Geraldine and Peter to align on Core Values, Core Focus, and a 3-Year Picture — resolving the implicit strategic ambiguity between heritage positioning and digital transformation. This alignment conversation is essential before any growth investment is made.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

The two most important immediate Rocks are clear from the data: (1) reduce DSO from 108 to 60 days within 90 days; (2) negotiate a formal creditor payment plan to bring DPO below 180 days. Installing Rock discipline ensures these financial priorities get executive ownership and weekly tracking, rather than being buried under client work.

Issues List & IDS

The competition law finding is evidence that the company's Issues were never properly surfaced, discussed, and solved through a structured process. The Touchpoint arrangement — a legally risky market-control mechanism — was apparently allowed to persist without a structured review. IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve) would create the discipline to surface and resolve structural business risks before they become regulatory events.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations in total: four financial/operational (to be implemented before or concurrent with EOS) and four EOS-specific. Sequencing principle: stabilise the cash position first, then install the operating system, then pursue growth.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Implement a 90-Day Collections Blitz: DSO Target 60 Days

Assign Geraldine Neubert (as MD) personal ownership of collections. Map every receivable over 60 days. Contact the top 5 clients by outstanding value immediately with a structured payment plan. Introduce a policy: no new work starts on a client with an invoice over 90 days outstanding. Target: reduce receivables from MUR 10.4M to MUR 5.8M within 90 days, releasing MUR 4.6M in cash. Success metric: weekly receivables aging report reviewed at Monday morning leadership meeting. This single action changes the company's financial position more than any revenue initiative.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Negotiate a Formal Creditor Payment Plan: DPO Target 180 Days

DPO of 331 days means the company owes MUR 12.5M in trade payables that are, on average, almost a year overdue. Proactively contact the top 5 creditors (likely media owners through Touchpoint) and negotiate structured 12-month repayment schedules. The cash released from Rec 01 funds this. Do not wait for creditors to demand payment — that scenario removes negotiating leverage. Target: normalise DPO to 180 days within 6 months, then 90 days within 12 months. This restores supplier relationships, removes the latent insolvency trigger, and begins rebuilding a positive credit reputation.

Immediate Priority — Within 30 Days
Recommendation 03

Administration Cost Review: Target 50% of Revenue

Administration costs at 64.3% of revenue are the root cause of the company's chronic inability to convert its strong gross margin into net profit. Commission an independent review of the cost base. Benchmark staffing ratios against comparable Ogilvy network agencies (10–15% revenue per head is the standard). Identify the top 5 cost lines within admin. Target: reduce admin costs to MUR 17.5M (50% of revenue), restoring net margin to approximately 7–8%. Owner: both directors. Timeline: 60-day analysis, 90-day implementation of identified savings. Success metric: monthly P&L review with admin costs as a tracked KPI.

Within 60 Days
Recommendation 04

Discharge the 1996 Floating Charge and Clarify Legal Position

The MNS/CBRIS record shows an undischarged floating charge (CH1447/18) from August 1996 — now 30 years old. Engage a Mauritius commercial lawyer to review whether this charge relates to an active facility or was never formally discharged when the underlying debt was repaid. If the charge is spent, file for discharge immediately. Additionally, obtain a full legal status update on the Touchpoint Competition Commission ruling: what penalties were imposed, what remedial actions are required, and what ongoing compliance obligations exist. This is essential corporate hygiene before any third-party due diligence (investors, bank, acquirer) is conducted.

Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

Maurice Publicite is Mauritius's most credentialled advertising agency — a 100-year-old Ogilvy-affiliated pioneer with genuine market leadership and institutional relationships no competitor can easily replicate. But it is also a financially fragile business running on fumes: negative equity, a 331-day payables backlog, and a competition law finding that signals governance structures are broken. The opportunity is real. The conversation should start with cash and accountability, not with growth. If Geraldine and Peter Neubert can align on the Visionary/Integrator split and commit to the financial triage, this company has the brand, the skills, and the network to be sustainably profitable within 18–24 months.
