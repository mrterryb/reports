Executive Summary

A 53-year-old family-owned printing institution generating MUR 180M in revenue — technically profitable but financially stretched, carrying a MUR 32M retained deficit and MUR 72M in debt against a thin 1.7% net margin.

Précigraph Limited is one of Mauritius's oldest and most established commercial printing companies, incorporated on 14 August 1973 and operating continuously from its Avenue St Vincent de Paul facility in Les Pailles. The company offers a comprehensive one-stop-shop for print: from concept, design, and prepress through to offset printing, digital printing (via Précisprint), binding, packing, and delivery. Its flagship Précidiary range — launched in 1981 — commands the local diary and agenda market. With approximately 115 employees, Précigraph sits squarely in the EOS sweet spot of 10–250 people. The company exports 30–40% of revenue to Réunion, Seychelles, Madagascar, Comores, and East Africa, a rare competitive differentiator in Mauritius's printing sector.

Ownership is tightly concentrated in the de Labauve d'Arifat family. France de Labauve d'Arifat and his spouse hold the overwhelming majority of both ordinary and preference shares — approximately 83% of ordinary shares and 100% of preference shares — with Société d'Argus holding the remaining 16.3% of ordinary shares. This is a multigenerational family enterprise: France de Labauve d'Arifat has been a director since the company's founding in 1973 and Chairman since 1992, while his son Vincent serves as CEO and has led operations since 1988. A third generation is now represented by Hubert de Labauve d'Arifat (Manager, Development) and a recent 2023 director appointment (Thevenau Marie Joseph Bertrand) signals possible governance evolution.

Financially, FY2024 shows MUR 180.2M in revenue with a 27.6% gross margin — adequate for the sector — but steep administration costs of MUR 44.7M (24.8% of revenue) compress the EBIT to MUR 7.6M (4.2% margin) and net profit to just MUR 3.0M (1.7%). The balance sheet reveals a more concerning picture: MUR 72.5M in total borrowings (54% short-term), a MUR 32.2M retained earnings deficit, and working capital of only MUR 4.7M. With an EOS suitability score of 6.4/10, Précigraph presents a genuine opportunity — the size is right, the pain points are visible, and the family's long track record suggests openness to structured systems, but financial tightness must be acknowledged in any EOS conversation.

Key Finding — Administration Cost Bloat Destroying Margin
Précigraph's fundamental problem is not revenue — MUR 180M from a 115-person Mauritian printing company is a strong top line. The problem is that administration costs alone consume MUR 44.7M (24.8% of revenue), turning a healthy 27.6% gross margin into a near-zero net margin of 1.7%. The company is working hard and earning well at the gross level, then giving most of it back in overhead. This is a classic EOS accountability signal: without clear role ownership and a Scorecard driving cost visibility, overhead accumulates invisibly. Any EOS engagement should open with a deep dive into what those MUR 44.7M of admin costs actually contain.

Company Overview

A family-owned commercial printing institution founded in 1973, operating across domestic and regional markets from a single Les Pailles facility with ~115 employees and a strong proprietary brand in the diary and agenda segment.

Founding and Corporate Record

Précigraph Limited was incorporated on 14 August 1973 under File No. C2223, Business Registration No. C07002223. The company is a private domestic company limited by shares, currently registered at Avenue St Vincent de Paul, Les Pailles, Mauritius (effective address since 22 November 2007). Stated capital comprises 147,897 ordinary shares (MUR 14,789,700 at par MUR 100) and 99,270 Preference B shares (MUR 9,927,000 at par MUR 100), totalling MUR 24,716,700 in share capital. The current auditor is Messr UHY & Co (appointed June 2020), replacing PricewaterhouseCoopers. Secretary is Alain Rey (since December 1992). Annual returns are current — the most recent was filed 29 January 2026 for the meeting held 22 December 2025. Financial statements are filed annually with FY end 30 June.

The company was founded with seven employees and has grown to approximately 115 staff over five decades. Key milestones include: launch of the Précidiary agenda range (1981), becoming the first Mauritius printing company to export regionally, adoption of digital printing technology (Précisprint, 2011), and launch of the Photobook.mu online platform (2011). The company received the Sappi African Digital Printer Gold Award in 2012. A 2013 Tecoma Award was received by CEO Vincent de Labauve d'Arifat, recognising innovation in printing. The shift from PwC to UHY & Co as auditor in 2020 is worth noting — it may reflect cost management, a relationship change, or both.

Products and Services Portfolio

Core Printing Services: Précigraph operates as a true one-stop print shop. Prepress services include scanning and image retouching, electronic page make-up, and digital proofing. Production capabilities cover offset printing with film lamination, hot foil stamping, and UV varnishing. Finishing services include binding (hardcover and softcover), packing, and direct delivery. The company was among the first in Mauritius to offer food-grade gold and silver plates for packaging, exported regionally.

Digital Printing — Précisprint: Launched 2011, Précisprint is the company's digital printing division, targeting short-run, variable-data, and fast-turnaround print needs. This division enables the company to serve clients who need smaller quantities without committing to offset minimum runs — widening the addressable market. The Sappi African Digital Printer Gold Award (2012) confirms technical excellence in this segment.

Proprietary Products: The Précidiary range (launched 1981) is the flagship branded product — described as "the most sought-after agendas on the local market" and positioned as 100% Mauritian. The 2026 brochure catalogue (visible at precigraph.mu/brochure) confirms active product development in the diary and agenda space. Photobook.mu (launched 2011) is an online photo album creation platform, extending the brand into consumer-facing digital services.

Published output categories: Annual reports, books (hard and soft cover), brochures, calendars, diaries, directories, folders, labels, leaflets, light packaging, magazines, posters, and postcards. This breadth positions Précigraph as a full-service partner rather than a commodity printer.

Geographic Presence

Précigraph operates from a single facility at Avenue St Vincent de Paul, Les Pailles, Mauritius — a well-established industrial-commercial corridor west of Port Louis. The domestic market is the primary revenue base. However, the company generates 30–40% of revenue from regional exports: Réunion Island, Seychelles, Madagascar, Comores, and East Africa. This makes Précigraph one of the very few Mauritius printing companies with a meaningful regional footprint — a genuine competitive differentiator that warrants attention in any EOS V/TO discussion. Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Précidiary — a durable proprietary brand in a commoditised sector. In commercial printing, where most output is undifferentiated, owning a branded product with 43 years of market presence is genuinely rare. The Précidiary range generates demand that is brand-driven rather than price-driven — insulating this revenue stream from commodity price competition in ways that commercial print jobs cannot be. No competitor in Mauritius has replicated this position.

2. Regional export infrastructure and first-mover relationships. Having established export routes to Réunion, Seychelles, Madagascar, Comores, and East Africa over multiple decades, Précigraph has built relationships, regulatory knowledge, and logistical capability that a domestic competitor cannot replicate quickly. This 30–40% revenue diversification also provides a natural hedge against domestic demand softness.

3. Full-service capability from concept to delivery. The combination of prepress, digital printing (Précisprint), offset printing, and finishing under one roof is a genuine operational differentiator. Clients seeking a single accountable partner for complex print projects — annual reports, premium magazines, corporate diaries — will pay a premium for this. Most Mauritius print operations are not genuinely full-service.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Digital disruption continuing to suppress commercial print volumes — corporate communications, annual reports, and marketing materials increasingly digital-first. Précigraph's Précisprint digital capability partially hedges against this but does not fully offset volume pressure.
  • Sustainability and ESG are driving paper and packaging premiums — corporates increasingly require FSC-certified, recycled, or low-carbon print. Précigraph's early adoption of violet laser technology (2005) and paper recycling positions it ahead of less environmentally aware competitors.
  • Regional African markets are under-served in premium print quality — as East African and Indian Ocean economies grow, demand for high-quality printed materials (banking, hospitality, government) is expanding. Précigraph's established regional presence is a genuine growth platform if capacity and quality can be maintained.
  • Short-run digital printing demand is growing — SMEs and startups require smaller print runs with faster turnaround than traditional offset allows. Précisprint addresses this directly and positions Précigraph well for the next wave of demand.
  • Premium diary and gift market faces secular decline risk — while Précidiary is currently dominant, smartphone calendar adoption among younger demographics represents a long-term demand risk for physical diaries that the company must plan for strategically.
  • Input cost volatility — paper and ink prices — global supply chain disruptions continue to affect paper pricing and availability. At 72.4% of revenue in COGS, any sustained paper price increase directly compresses the already-thin margin. Bulk purchasing (reflected in the high inventory) is a mitigation strategy but also a working capital drain.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 8 / 10

At approximately 115 employees, Précigraph sits squarely in the EOS sweet spot of 10–250 people. The company runs multiple operational clusters — prepress, digital print, offset print, finishing, sales, export logistics, and the Précidiary product line — with geographic complexity across five regional markets. This level of operational breadth across a single management layer is precisely the environment where EOS accountability structures deliver maximum value. The size and complexity score is high; the only reason it is not 9 or 10 is the absence of confirmed evidence of multiple functional silos causing visible friction.

02 — Leadership Openness 6 / 10

There are meaningful signals of openness alongside structural barriers. Positive signals: Vincent de Labauve d'Arifat won the Tecoma Award 2013 for innovation — suggesting a CEO who values external recognition and new approaches. The company was a digital printing pioneer and first regional exporter, both requiring a willingness to change. The 2023 appointment of Thevenau Marie Joseph Bertrand as an external director is particularly notable — it may indicate the family is actively seeking outside governance perspective. Barriers: this is a 53-year family business where the founder is still Chairman and the CEO is his son. Long-tenure family businesses often have deep resistance to outside accountability structures, even when the leader intellectually supports them. Score reflects genuine but unconfirmed openness.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 7 / 10

The financial data reveals textbook EOS-addressable pain points. Admin costs consuming 90% of gross profit without any visible breakdown suggests a lack of cost accountability — nobody is measuring what the MUR 44.7M contains. A MUR 32.2M accumulated retained deficit indicates years of below-potential financial performance, consistent with a business that has grown in revenue but not in management discipline. The DSO of 61 days suggests a collections process that is not being managed to a clear owner and target. The thin liquidity (quick ratio 0.60×) creates operational stress that fragments management attention. These are all classic symptoms of a business that has outgrown its informal systems and needs the structure EOS provides.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

No public evidence of formal management frameworks, OKRs, KPIs, or documented processes. The switch from PwC to UHY as auditor in 2020 may indicate financial system simplification rather than sophistication. The company's longevity (53 years) and quality awards confirm that production processes are strong — but the absence of financial cost transparency (entire overhead in one admin line) suggests management information systems are not granular enough to drive decision-making. Score of 5 reflects: strong operational processes in production, likely weak processes in finance, HR, and strategic planning.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

Several strong cultural indicators. The company's five founding pillars — quality, happy work environment, employment creation, and sustainability — suggest an intentional culture. The long tenure of key directors (some since 1989–1992) indicates low leadership turnover and institutional loyalty. The MUR 24.3M in long-term staff provisions for ~115 employees (~MUR 211K per employee) indicates long-serving staff with significant entitlements, implying high retention and a stable workforce. Environmental commitments (violet laser technology, water reduction, recycling) signal a culture that cares beyond pure profit. These are positive EOS cultural prerequisites. However, family governance without clear accountability structures can breed a culture of loyalty over performance — a pattern EOS directly addresses.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 5 / 10

In a family business where the Chairman (France) founded the company and the CEO (Vincent) has led it for 36 years, decision-making almost certainly concentrates at the top. The presence of three long-tenured directors (Rey since 1989, Pilot since 1992) who have served alongside the family for decades may not provide genuine challenge. Vincent's public articulation of the five founding pillars demonstrates that Précigraph has a sense of purpose and values. Score reflects a business with genuine core values and directional momentum, but lacking the shared, written, quarterly-reviewed vision that EOS codifies.

No public evidence of structured meeting rhythms (weekly L10s, quarterly reviews, annual planning days). The annual meeting dates filed with the Registrar are consistently in late December, suggesting year-end governance meetings only — a calendar pattern consistent with a family business doing what is legally required rather than running a proactive management calendar. With 115 employees across multiple functions and five regional export markets, the absence of a disciplined meeting structure would explain the execution drag visible in the financial ratios. This is a strong EOS entry point.

EOS Suitability

Overall Rating: Moderate-High — Qualified Positive

Précigraph is a genuine EOS candidate. The size (115 people), operational complexity (multi-segment, multi-market), and financial pain points (admin cost opacity, margin compression, liquidity stress) are all EOS-addressable. The founding family's track record of innovation and the 2023 external director appointment suggest real openness to structured thinking. The gating factor is family business inertia: 53 years of the de Labauve d'Arifat family managing without external accountability frameworks means the EOS conversation must be led with business pain (the MUR 44.7M admin problem, the thin margin) rather than framework concepts. If Vincent acknowledges the admin cost opacity as a problem he cannot solve alone, EOS has a clear entry point. If the framing is "we've always done it this way," the score drops to 4.5.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Précigraph

Accountability Chart

The most urgent tool. With five directors (two family, three long-tenured), a CEO, a Development Manager, and multiple operational functions, role clarity is almost certainly absent. The Accountability Chart would force the family to define who owns what — particularly the MUR 44.7M admin cost line, the export P&L, and the Précidiary product line — and eliminate the overlapping authority that typically drives overhead accumulation in family businesses.

Scorecard & Measurables

The financial data shows no evidence that management operates from a weekly data dashboard. DSO at 61 days and admin costs at 24.8% of revenue are problems that a Scorecard would surface and force action on immediately. Building a 10–15 metric weekly Scorecard — covering DSO, cost-per-revenue-rupee, production yield, and export revenue — would give Vincent visibility he currently lacks and create the accountability cadence the business needs.

Issues List & IDS

The admin cost opacity, the liquidity crunch, the DSO problem, and the long-term provisions liability are all long-standing issues that have not been resolved — consistent with a business that discusses problems but does not systematically Identify, Discuss, and Solve them. EOS IDS meeting rhythm would break this pattern by forcing each issue onto a visible list and through a structured resolution process in every Level 10 meeting.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

Précigraph's financial condition requires near-term operational change — DSO reduction, admin cost audit, and working capital improvement cannot wait for a five-year plan. EOS Rocks would translate the three most critical financial improvements into specific 90-day commitments with named owners and weekly check-ins. This gives the team a structured sprint methodology for the financial work that needs to happen alongside any EOS rollout.

V/TO — Vision/Traction Organiser

Précigraph has clear core values and a track record of strategic moves (digital launch, regional exports, Photobook.mu) but no evidence of a written 3-year picture or 10-year target. The V/TO would capture the de Labauve d'Arifat family's vision for the business — does Vincent want to grow regional exports to 50% of revenue? Position the company for succession to the next generation (Hubert)? Crystallising this in writing would align the entire management team and reveal where strategic disagreements exist within the family.

Level 10 Meeting Rhythm

Annual governance meetings (the only structured calendar event visible in the CBRIS filing) are insufficient for a 115-person operation across multiple market segments. A weekly 90-minute L10 meeting with a fixed agenda, Scorecard review, and IDS time would immediately improve operational communication and surface the issues that are currently left to accumulate into structural problems — like the admin cost overhang and the DSO creep. This is the simplest EOS tool to implement and the one that produces the fastest visible change.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations: four financial and operational (immediate to 60-day horizon) and four EOS-specific (to be implemented sequentially following a Focus Day). Financial recommendations must precede EOS rollout to stabilise the business and establish credibility with the leadership team.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Conduct an Emergency Administration Cost Audit

Commission UHY & Co (or an internal finance lead) to produce a full breakdown of the MUR 44,683,096 administration cost line within 30 days. The objective is to identify the top 5 cost categories by value and flag any that are discretionary, duplicated, or can be reduced without operational impact. Success looks like: a ranked list of admin cost components with a documented reduction target of minimum MUR 5M (11% reduction) and an owner assigned to each action. This single initiative, if successful, would increase net profit by approximately 160%.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Launch a 60-Day Receivables Collection Drive

Assign a named owner (Finance Manager or CFO equivalent) to reduce DSO from 61 days to 45 days within 60 days. Actions: contact top 10 debtors by balance this week; implement 7-day reminder cycle for all invoices; introduce a 2% early payment discount for clients settling within 30 days. Success looks like: MUR 7.4M in cash released from debtors, quick ratio improving from 0.60× to approximately 0.73×, and a named DSO metric added to a weekly management report. This is the fastest available lever to improve the liquidity crisis without taking on more debt.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 03

Restructure Short-Term Borrowings to Reduce Rollover Risk

With MUR 39.4M (54% of total debt) in short-term borrowings, Précigraph is operationally dependent on bank renewal decisions. Within 60 days, Vincent should approach the company's banking partners to convert a minimum of MUR 20M of short-term facilities to 3–5 year term facilities, reducing the current liability profile and the rollover concentration risk. Success: short-term borrowings fall below MUR 20M, current ratio improves from 1.08× to approximately 1.41×, and interest coverage stress is reduced by extending maturities even if the rate is marginally higher.

Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

Précigraph is a strong Mauritius printing institution — 53 years old, dominant in its branded diary niche, pioneering in regional exports, and technically proficient. It generates MUR 180M in revenue from 115 people, which is genuinely impressive. The problem is entirely structural: MUR 44.7M in administration costs that nobody has publicly accounted for is destroying the margin on a business that produces MUR 49.7M of gross profit. The retained deficit of MUR 32.2M suggests this pattern has persisted for years. EOS will not fix the cost problem — but the accountability structures EOS installs (Scorecard, Accountability Chart, IDS, Rocks) will make it impossible to ignore. That is the real pitch for Vincent: not "EOS will transform your culture" but "EOS will give you the visibility to solve the problem that is costing you MUR 40M in margin every year."
