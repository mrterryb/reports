Executive Summary

A 38-year-old Mauritius industrial business with respectable margins, but receivables and charge-heavy leverage make cash conversion the central tension.

Industrial and Hotel Equipment Manufacturers Limited (IHEML) is a live domestic private company incorporated on 3 March 1988 and operating from Junction Road, Pailles. The CBRIS record shows a metal-fabrication and industrial services footprint: welding of metal work pieces, metal furniture manufacture, job contracting, goods vehicle operation, general retail and import/export. The stated capital is MUR 1.5m across 15,000 ordinary shares.

Ownership is concentrated: BCPM Limited holds 100% of the shares. The registry also shows a relatively stable leadership set, with Andre François Gérard Couve as chairman and director, plus directors Marie Roger Loïc Bâx de Keating, Rehamtulah Alnoor Goulamaly Mandjee, Louis Marie Frédéric Merle and Simon Joseph Pougnet. UHY & Co acts as auditor and Navitas Corporate Services Ltd as secretary.

Financially, the business produced MUR 136.6m revenue and MUR 8.1m profit after tax in FY2024, with gross profit at 31.4% and EBITDA-like operating profitability still positive despite a heavy expense base. The problem is not the income statement alone; it is the balance sheet and cash cycle. Trade receivables of MUR 74.2m imply roughly 198 debtor days, while the charge register shows a dense stack of floating charges, including new 2022 and 2025 registrations. EOS is plausible here because the business already looks complex enough to benefit from disciplined accountability.

Key Finding — Cash Is the Bottleneck
Most of the company’s apparent strength is trapped in working capital. The firm looks like a real operating business with decent gross profitability, but its receivables are so large relative to sales that cash conversion, not revenue growth, should be the first management priority. In practical terms, this is the kind of business where an EOS rollout should begin with scorecard discipline, clear accountability, and a focused working-capital turnaround.

Structural Competitive Advantages

The main defensible advantages appear to be: a real industrial site at Pailles; the ability to bundle fabrication, transport, contracting and trading; and the credibility that comes from a 1988 incorporation date and a visible corporate record. In a market where buyers often want one supplier to handle multiple steps, that bundle can be more valuable than raw price alone.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Construction, industrial maintenance and metalwork demand can support steady fabrication volumes when the local economy is active.
  • Import substitution and local sourcing trends can favour Mauritius-based fabricators that can respond quickly.
  • Digital discoverability matters more each year; the lack of a clear public web footprint is a commercial disadvantage if competitors are easier to find.
  • Working-capital intensity is a structural risk in project-led fabrication businesses, especially when debtor cycles stretch out.
  • Supplier concentration and imported input exposure can squeeze margins if procurement is not tightly managed.
  • The company’s multi-line licence base can help when customers want one-stop service, but it can also dilute managerial focus if not explicitly prioritised.
Competitive Note
The business likely competes less on pure innovation and more on execution reliability, project responsiveness and breadth of service. That is a defensible market position only if management can control receivables and keep operational promises tight.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for IHEML

Scorecard & Measurables

The first priority should be a scorecard built around DSO, cash conversion, order backlog, on-time delivery, gross margin and overdue receivables. That is the fastest way to turn the cash problem into a visible weekly management conversation.

Accountability Chart

Because the company spans fabrication, contracting, logistics and trading, role clarity matters more than job titles. An EOS accountability chart would reduce overlap and make it obvious who owns sales, production, collections and purchasing.

Level 10 Meeting

The business needs a weekly operating rhythm to stop issues from sitting in inboxes and WhatsApp threads. A disciplined Level 10 meeting would surface receivables, supplier tension and production blockers early.

Rocks

Quarterly Rocks should be tied to working-capital reduction, customer follow-up discipline and operating margin improvement. Rocks will keep the business from drifting back into reaction mode.

Issues List & IDS

The company has visible problems that need structured problem-solving rather than ad hoc firefighting. An IDS process would help the leadership team attack cash collection, procurement terms and margin leakage in the right order.

V/TO

The public record does not show a clear strategic destination. A Vision / Traction Organizer would force the owner team to define what the business should become, which activities matter most, and what to stop doing.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations: four financial / operational actions first, then four EOS actions. The sequence matters — cash discipline first, then operating system.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Launch a 30-day debtor recovery sprint

Assign one owner to the top 20 overdue accounts, call weekly, and require a cash-in schedule. The target should be a measurable reduction in DSO, with at least MUR 10m of receivables converted into cash over the first cycle.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Map and renegotiate supplier terms

Segment suppliers by criticality and seek a better balance between payment terms and continuity risk. The goal is not simply to stretch payables further; it is to stabilise supply while reducing the probability of a cash cliff.

Within 30-60 Days
Recommendation 03

Separate margin reporting by business line

Break the combined filing view into fabrication, contracting, transport and trading margins. Management should know which activities create cash and which activities consume it before deciding where to grow.

Within 30-60 Days
Recommendation 04

Introduce weekly cash forecasting

Build a 13-week cash forecast with collections, payables, payroll and debt service. This should be owned by finance / operations together and reviewed every week by the leadership team.

Within 90 Days

EOS Implementation Recommendations

Recommendation 05

Run a Focus Day with the owner team

Start with the leadership team on a one-day EOS-style Focus Day to define the true issues, the core vision and the next 90 days of traction. The business is old enough and complex enough to benefit from an explicit reset.

Immediate Priority — Start Here
Recommendation 06

Build an Accountability Chart hypothesis

Create a simple draft chart for sales, operations, procurement, finance and collections. The objective is to remove overlap and clarify who owns the most cash-sensitive functions.

Within 30-60 Days
Recommendation 07

Set the first three Rocks around cash and clarity

One Rock should be receivables reduction, one should be weekly scorecard adoption, and one should be role clarity for the operations / collections chain. Make them measurable and visible to the entire leadership team.

Within 90 Days
Recommendation 08

Write the first V/TO around focus and stop-doing list

Clarify which business lines are strategic, which ones are support activities, and which low-return activities should be reduced. The business will not scale cleanly until the owner team agrees on what not to pursue.

The main obstacle is not market relevance; it is internal discipline around collections, prioritisation and accountable execution.
