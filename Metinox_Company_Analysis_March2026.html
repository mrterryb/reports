Executive Summary

Metinox is a long-standing Pailles-based stainless-equipment and metalwork business whose biggest constraint does not appear to be demand or profit generation, but working-capital discipline.

Metinox Ltd was incorporated on 6 October 1995 and is registered in Mauritius as a live domestic private company. It operates from Junction Road, Pailles, and public business listings position it in stainless steel work, kitchen equipment, and related light-engineering activity for the Mauritian market.

Ownership is clean at the immediate level: BCPM Limited owns 100% of Metinox. The director set overlaps heavily with other BCPM-linked entities, which strongly suggests Metinox sits inside a wider privately held group rather than functioning as a standalone one-owner business.

Financially, this is not a basket case. FY2024 shows revenue of Rs 16,950,646, gross margin of 48.8%, and net profit after tax of Rs 1,775,915. The ugly bit is debtor days at roughly 276, which is absurdly high for an SME of this size and likely hides a cash-conversion problem that EOS could help expose and fix.

Key Finding — Profitable, but financing its customers
Metinox looks like a commercially valid small industrial business with decent margins and no disclosed borrowing burden. The real strategic conversation is not “Can this business survive?” but “How much cash, speed and management bandwidth is being destroyed by weak operational discipline, role clarity and follow-through?”

Structural Competitive Advantages

The first real advantage is longevity: a company that has been around since 1995 and is still profitable clearly has some customer trust and execution know-how. The second is local fabrication capability, which matters in Mauritius because lead times, customisation, after-sales responsiveness and on-site adjustment often beat imported perfection. The third is group context: if Metinox can leverage a broader BCPM relationship network without inheriting group bureaucracy, that is useful. The danger, of course, is that group complexity helps sales but muddies accountability.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Mauritius hospitality refurbishment and food-service activity can support ongoing demand for stainless and kitchen-related equipment.
  • Project-led engineering businesses are vulnerable to delayed customer payments, which is already visible in Metinox’s balance sheet.
  • Imported alternatives put constant price pressure on local fabricators, so quality, speed and custom service matter more than ever.
  • Small industrial firms with weak process discipline often hit a ceiling where owner attention becomes the bottleneck; Metinox looks close to that zone.
  • Domestic fabrication remains attractive where clients need bespoke solutions, measurements, installation adjustments and short lead times.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Metinox

Scorecard & Measurables

Receivables and collection discipline jump off the balance sheet. Weekly measurables around invoices due, aged debt, quotes issued, jobs completed, and gross margin by project would likely surface reality fast.

Accountability Chart

This looks like the kind of owner-influenced SME where roles can blur between sales, projects, workshop, purchasing and collections. Clear seat ownership would probably unlock margin and speed.

Level 10 Meeting

Public data do not show any sign of formal meeting rhythm. A disciplined weekly meeting would help move the company from reaction to traction, especially around jobs, cash and priorities.

Issues List & IDS

When debtor days are this stretched, the root issue is rarely just “customers pay late”. It is usually quoting terms, project handover, billing discipline, escalation, or owner avoidance. IDS would force the truth out.

Rocks

The first 90-day Rocks should probably be operational, not inspirational: reduce DSO, tighten quote-to-cash flow, and create visibility on job profitability. One quarter of focus could move cash materially.

Vision/Traction Organiser

If the business is part of a wider BCPM orbit, it likely needs sharper strategic choices on positioning, target segments, and the relationship between group ownership and day-to-day execution. The V/TO would expose fuzziness.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations: four financial / operational and four EOS-specific. The sequencing principle is simple — fix the cash leak first, then install operating discipline so the same nonsense does not come back wearing a different shirt.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Attack receivables with a named owner and a 60-day target

Name one person accountable for collections and impose a weekly aged-debt review. Success means reducing DSO from roughly 276 days to below 90 within two quarters, with every major overdue account assigned an action and escalation date.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Tighten the quote-to-cash process

Map the full chain from quotation to delivery note to invoice to payment follow-up. The likely problem is not one broken step but a sloppy handoff between several. Success means invoices go out faster, disputes get surfaced earlier, and nobody gets to say “I thought someone else handled it.”

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 03

Measure gross margin by job, not just by company

Project and fabrication businesses lie to themselves when they only look at company-level profit. Create a simple per-job gross-margin view so unprofitable work, rework and underquoting become visible. Success means knowing which jobs create margin and which ones are vanity turnover.

Within 30–60 Days
Recommendation 04

Review deferred-tax position with the accountant

The deferred-tax balance is too meaningful to ignore. Have finance explain exactly what created it, what reverses it, and whether it reflects timing issues, asset treatment, or something structural. Success means the leadership team can explain the liability in plain English, not accountant hieroglyphics.

Within 30–60 Days

EOS Implementation Recommendations

Recommendation 05

Run a Focus Day with the real decision-makers, not the decorative ones

Bring the people who actually shape priorities across sales, projects, workshop and cash. The point is to surface the real frustrations, not to stage a polite boardroom tea party. Success means the core issues list is brutally honest by the end of the day.

During EOS Launch
Recommendation 06

Build an Accountability Chart around quote–build–bill–collect

Start with a practical operating hypothesis: Sales / Estimating, Operations / Production, Finance / Collections, and a true Integrator seat if one actually exists. Success means every critical handoff has one owner and no grey fog.

During EOS Launch
Recommendation 07

Make the first Rock a cash-conversion Rock

Do not waste the first quarter on vague culture slogans. Pick a Rock such as “Reduce overdue receivables by Rs 3m” or “Cut DSO below 120 days”. Success is numeric and weekly visible.

During EOS Launch
Recommendation 08

Use the V/TO to answer one hard strategic question

Is Metinox primarily a fabrication specialist, a kitchen-equipment business, or a broader group relationship vehicle? Until that is clarified, growth can become messy and margin can get diluted. Success means strategic focus sharper than “we do a bit of everything”.

During EOS Launch

Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

Metinox looks like a solid but probably under-managed SME, not a failing one. The opportunity is to convert existing commercial legitimacy into cleaner cash, clearer accountability and less owner drag. The obstacle is the usual one: successful people often tolerate operational mess precisely because the business is still making money.
