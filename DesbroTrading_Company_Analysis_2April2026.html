Executive Summary

Desbro Trading Ltd is a 58-year-old Sennik family-owned chemical and agro distribution business generating MUR 375M in revenue with a wafer-thin 1.1% net margin, zero bank debt, and a debtor book of MUR 83.8M that is the single greatest threat to its financial health — and the clearest EOS pain point to enter on.

Desbro Trading Limited (DTL), incorporated on 31 January 1968 as "Desbro (Trading) Limited" (File No. C1638, BRN C06001638), is a Mauritius-registered importer, stockist, and distributor of industrial chemicals, plastic raw materials, fertilizers, solvents, agrochemicals, and animal feeds. Its operational premises are at Satnam Road, Lot 23F/5, Industrial Zone, La Tour Koenig, Mauritius. The company serves over 800 clients across the Indian Ocean region and distributes more than 200 products. It achieved ISO 9001 certification in January 2024. Annual registration fees are current to 2026 and the company is fully compliant with no charges, objections, or winding-up proceedings registered.

A critical ownership correction is required against pre-CBRIS intelligence: Desbro Trading Limited is owned and controlled by the Sennik family, not the Agarwal family. Shareholders on record are Sennik Abhijeet (30%), Joya Bhandari (30%), Sennik Kapil (30%), and DRS Holdings Limited (10%). The board of directors comprises Sennik Abhijeet (director since 2006), Sennik Kapil (since 2018), Garsee Gopeechand (since 2014), and Desmarais Gaston Francois (since 2022). Anisha Sennik — who holds the role of Procurement Manager in the operational management team — is an Alternate Director, confirming direct family involvement at every level. Palash Agarwal's title of "Executive Director" on ZoomInfo does not appear in the CBRIS register and may reflect a commercial or operational role title rather than a formal directorship. The Sennik family is the governing family of record.

Financially, FY2024 (year ended 15 September 2024) shows revenue of MUR 375.5M, gross profit of MUR 56.6M (15.1% margin), and net profit after tax of MUR 4.2M (1.12% margin). The company carries zero long-term bank debt — a remarkable structural strength. However, debtor days of 81 days on a MUR 83.8M receivables book, combined with MUR 89.2M in trade payables (DPO 102 days), reveals a working capital model that is functionally financed by suppliers — a fragile arrangement. Finance costs of MUR 5.96M with no borrowings on the balance sheet suggest significant letter-of-credit or related-party charges. EOS suitability is rated 6.4/10: the business profile fits the EOS sweet spot well, but the right entry point is Sennik Abhijeet or Sennik Kapil — the family directors — not the professional GM.

Key Finding — 81-Day Debtors in a 102-Day Supplier-Financed Model
DTL's working capital structure is a tightly stretched supplier-financing model: it pays its overseas suppliers in 102 days while collecting from its 800+ Mauritius clients in 81 days — a 21-day gap that is manageable only because of the MUR 18.3M cash position and zero bank borrowings. Any deterioration in debtor collection — a single large client going slow, a bad debt write-off, or a FX-driven inventory revaluation — would trigger a cash crisis in a business with no bank credit lines as backstop. The MUR 5.96M finance cost with zero formal debt is the telltale: the company is using LC facilities and/or related-party loans (booked under "Others" in non-current liabilities at MUR 37.6M) as its primary financing mechanism. This is the most important financial risk in the business and the most compelling EOS entry conversation: "Who owns the number that says how many days your clients owe you money — and what happens to that number each week?"

Charge Register

The CBRIS charge register for Desbro Trading Limited (C1638) shows no charges registered — no floating charges, fixed charges, debentures, or mortgages of any nature. This confirms that the company's zero long-term borrowings position is genuine: no assets have been pledged to any bank or creditor. This is an exceptional balance sheet position for a MUR 375M importer/distributor and reflects 58 years of disciplined family reinvestment rather than bank leverage. The corollary risk is that the MUR 37.6M "Others" in non-current liabilities — which is the probable source of the MUR 5.96M in annual finance costs — is an unregistered related-party liability rather than a formal bank facility.

No Charges Registered — Strongest Balance Sheet Signal
A MUR 375M chemical distribution business with zero bank debt and no registered charges is highly unusual in Mauritius. The Sennik family has built this balance sheet through retained earnings alone. This gives DTL genuine strategic flexibility — it could raise significant bank facilities for growth if needed, against both its clean balance sheet and its MUR 107M in owned property. This is a strength to acknowledge in the discovery conversation.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. 58-year incumbent brand with 800+ client relationships. In a market the size of Mauritius, trust accumulated over nearly six decades is genuinely hard to displace. Industrial buyers in chemicals typically stay with known, reliable suppliers — qualification of a new supplier involves testing, regulatory compliance verification, and relationship risk. New entrants face a multi-year trust-building curve.

2. Zero bank debt provides strategic flexibility no competitor with leverage can match. DTL can self-fund growth, absorb a difficult trading year, or raise debt capital for acquisitions without existing covenants constraining it. In a rising interest rate environment, competitors with leverage are squeezed; DTL is not.

3. Group vertical integration creates captive and referral demand. STR Marketing Ltée (manufacturing pipes from PVC/PE inputs) is a captive buyer of DTL's plastic raw materials. MRC Wire Products links DTL into the broader group construction materials ecosystem. This intra-group demand provides a baseline that no independent distributor can replicate.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • ISO 9001 certification (Jan 2024) — opens government and parastatal procurement requiring certified suppliers; a direct new revenue channel DTL did not have access to before.
  • Water treatment infrastructure investment in Mauritius — ongoing government spending on water and wastewater infrastructure directly drives demand for DTL's water treatment chemical range (aluminium sulfate, ferric chloride, chlorine products, polyaluminium chloride).
  • Agro diversification tailwind — Mauritius horticulture and controlled-environment agriculture growing to reduce food import dependency; directly benefits DTL's greenhouse, drip irrigation, and agrochemical lines.
  • Mauritius manufacturing expansion — the government's 2020–2025 industrial policy targets light manufacturing growth, increasing demand for industrial chemicals and plastic raw materials across the primary DTL client base.
  • Headwind: Sugar industry structural decline. Mauritius cane sugar output has declined significantly over two decades. DTL's fertilizer business is exposed to this structural trend; active diversification into horticulture and aquaculture is essential to protect agro revenue.
  • Headwind: Supplier concentration and FX risk.
