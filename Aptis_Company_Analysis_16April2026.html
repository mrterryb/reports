Executive Summary

A 15-year-old Mauritian conglomerate that bootstrapped from tobacco distribution into a seven-subsidiary group spanning FMCG, fiberglass manufacturing, fleet management, Italian retail, fertility services, and now luxury real estate — at an inflection point where complexity has likely outgrown its operational systems.

APTIS Group is a privately held, founder-led Mauritian conglomerate incorporated in 2009 as TNS Tobacco, the exclusive BAT distributor for Mauritius. Under the vision of founder Jean-Pierre Mamet, the group aggressively diversified over 15 years, rebranding to APTIS in March 2019 to signal its transformation from a single-sector operator into a multi-cluster holding group. The group's registered holding entity, APTIS Holdings Ltd (C162483), is headquartered at Aptis House, Inova, Riche Terre Business Park, Riche Terre, Mauritius. The group operates through five activity clusters: Distribution (TNS Tobacco, TNS Consumer, Racso, L'Artigiano), Commerce (Resiglas), Services (Navitas, Harley Street Fertility Centre), Investment (Bigarade sur Mer, Dominos), and Real Estate (APTIS Property, BARNES Mauritius).

Ownership is concentrated within the Mamet family. Jean-Pierre Mamet remains Founder and President. His son Oscar Mamet (age 33) assumed the role of Group CEO in January 2024, succeeding Jean-Pierre who had held the Group Deputy CEO title. Jean-Alain Mamet serves as Managing Director of RACSO, suggesting the group is undergoing a generational leadership transition across multiple subsidiaries. The group also counts two additional live CBRIS entities — APTIS Property Company Limited (C162801) and APTIS Services Company Limited (C162742) — indicating intentional legal ring-fencing across its property and services divisions. The holding structure was selected as the primary research subject given its role as the group apex and EOS engagement entry point.

Financially, the group's dominant revenue engine is Aptis Distribution (the BAT franchise), which disclosed a turnover of MUR 6.8 billion — dwarfing all other subsidiaries and making it a low-margin, high-volume anchor. Resiglas (fiberglass) contributed MUR 148 million with a team of 167, making it the most labour-intensive entity. RACSO (fleet/fuel) recorded MUR 46.5 million with 5 employees, suggesting strong per-head productivity. BARNES Mauritius (luxury real estate, acquired May 2025) generated MUR 78 million turnover in its most recent financial year with 35 staff. EOS suitability is scored at 7.1/10 — the group shows all the classical signals of an organisation that has grown faster than its systems: multi-entity complexity, a very recent CEO transition, active M&A, and expressed strategic priorities around efficiency and innovation that have not yet been translated into operational frameworks.

Key Finding — CEO Transition + Multi-Entity Complexity = Classic EOS Entry Window
Oscar Mamet became Group CEO in January 2024 at age 33, inheriting a seven-entity conglomerate with five business clusters spanning tobacco, manufacturing, fleet, food retail, healthcare, and property. His stated priorities — "efficiency, innovation, and positioning APTIS as a leading market player" — are precisely the language of a CEO who intuitively knows his organisation needs a system but has not yet found one. The May 2025 acquisition of BARNES Mauritius (the group's largest external acquisition to date) adds a 35-person luxury sales team with a distinct culture, compounding the accountability and coordination challenge. This is a textbook EOS window: new leader, ambitious growth agenda, proven but uncoordinated subsidiaries, and no visible operating system. The conversation should open on how Oscar is currently running the group across seven subsidiaries — L10s, Rocks, accountability across entities — before EOS is ever named.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Franchise-protected monopoly in tobacco distribution. The BAT exclusivity agreement is not replicable by any competitor — it is a contractual moat that generates MUR 6.8 billion in turnover with 25 people. This cash engine funds all diversification and acquisition activity. It is the group's most valuable and most perishable asset simultaneously.

2. First-mover manufacturing heritage in Resiglas. A 65-year-old fiberglass manufacturer in a small island economy is inherently difficult to replicate — the tooling, the craft skills, the client relationships, and the local supply chain knowledge are compounded over decades. No credible local competitor exists in the fiberglass/pool/tank manufacturing segment.

3. International brand licensing in luxury real estate and premium retail. BARNES Mauritius operates under a globally recognised luxury real estate brand (BARNES International, 150+ offices, 20+ countries). L'Artigiano brings premium Italian gourmet credibility unavailable to local competitors. These licensed brands provide instant market positioning that would take years to build independently.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Mauritius luxury property market is growing — international HNW buyers, residency-by-investment schemes, and post-COVID lifestyle migration are driving sustained demand in the premium segment BARNES serves. The APTIS acquisition timing is well-judged.
  • Global tobacco regulation tightening — The WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control continues to drive plain packaging, advertising bans, and tax increases. The BAT franchise will face volume pressure over a 5–10 year horizon. This is APTIS's strategic time bomb.
  • Sustainability and pool/water management demand — Resiglas's fiberglass pool and tank business benefits from Mauritius's growing residential construction market and increasing awareness of water storage needs. New product lines in sustainable materials are a growth lever.
  • Fleet electrification and GPS compliance — RACSO's OmniComm GPS monopoly and fuel management systems are well-positioned as fleet operators face increasing regulatory and ESG pressure to monitor and optimise fuel consumption. The market for fleet telematics in Mauritius is underpenetrated.
  • FMCG distribution consolidation — TNS Consumer's 11-brand portfolio and Danette/L'Artigiano premium positioning benefit from a broader trend toward premium FMCG consumption in Mauritius as disposable incomes rise in the middle class.
  • Healthcare demand growth — Harley Street Fertility Centre operates in a sector with structural demographic tailwinds (delayed family formation, medical tourism from the region) and very limited local competition in specialist fertility services.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. APTIS presents an unusually rich EOS opportunity: a family-controlled group in active transition, with a new CEO, aggressive M&A, multi-entity complexity, and an operational infrastructure that has not yet caught up with strategic ambition. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and the acquisition/leadership record.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 8 / 10

The group operates seven subsidiaries across five business clusters with an estimated 200+ employees. Multi-entity structures with separate legal wrappers (Holdings, Property, Services) and family members leading individual businesses (Jean-Alain Mamet at RACSO, Oscar Mamet at group level) create exactly the coordination and accountability complexity EOS was built to address. The BARNES acquisition (May 2025) added 35 people with a distinct luxury sales culture to a manufacturing and distribution group — integration without a shared operating system is the textbook EOS challenge.

02 — Leadership Openness 7 / 10

Oscar Mamet's public positioning is distinctly self-aware and growth-oriented. His stated priorities — "efficiency and innovation," "remaining alert for expansion opportunities while optimising resources" — reflect a leader who knows the organisation needs to improve how it runs, not just what it sells. At 33, a new CEO successor to a founder, he is more likely to be open to external frameworks than an entrenched owner-operator. The family retention of operational control (Jean-Alain Mamet at RACSO) is a nuance — the EOS pitch must address how the framework works in a family business context. The Founder (Jean-Pierre) remaining as President could be either an ally (if he champions EOS) or a blocker (if Oscar defers to him on structural decisions).

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 9 / 10

APTIS is in exactly the growth stage where EOS delivers maximum value: past early-stage survival, proven in multiple markets, actively acquiring, but facing the integration and coordination complexity that comes with scale. The BARNES acquisition (May 2025), the L'Artigiano franchise expansion (2022–ongoing), the Resiglas acquisition (2018), and the CEO transition (January 2024) all happened within a short window. Each of these events generates accountability gaps, unclear ownership of outcomes, and coordination friction — the exact pain points EOS resolves. Pain level: very high.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

The group's internal marketing cell (managed by Karen Sarah) demonstrates that some cross-subsidiary coordination exists. The group's data-driven marketing language ("consumer data and insights drive Group marketing decisions") suggests some analytical infrastructure. However, with seven subsidiaries, a family-run holding structure, and no visible ERP or shared performance management framework disclosed, it is probable that each entity runs its own informal systems. The absence of published processes, a group scorecard, or any mention of a shared meeting rhythm supports a mid-range score. There is enough existing structure to implement EOS without starting from zero, but enough fragmentation to make EOS feel immediately relevant.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

APTIS's brand identity — captured in the "UP TO IT" magazine series featuring Oscar Mamet — projects an entrepreneurial, achievement-oriented culture with clear leadership pride. The mission statement ("We make a difference by providing opportunities for people to unleash their full potential") and the vision ("diversified group enabling our businesses to grow and be leaders in their respective fields") are EOS-compatible values. However, a conglomerate culture spanning tobacco distribution, fiberglass manufacturing, fertility medicine, and luxury real estate will have radically different sub-cultures at the entity level. EOS's core values and accountability tools are designed precisely to create cultural coherence across diverse businesses.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 6 / 10

The group's acquisition decisions (Resiglas 2018, L'Artigiano 2022, BARNES 2025) show strategic boldness and reasonable deal judgment, suggesting capable top-level decision-making. However, in family-controlled conglomerates with a Founder-President and a new CEO-son, decision authority is rarely fully delegated. The risk is that Oscar can set direction but cannot fully commit organisational resources without Jean-Pierre's backing. EOS's Accountability Chart and IDS (Issues, Discuss, Solve) tools directly address this — they create a forum where decisions are made at the right level with clear ownership.

07 — Vision Clarity 6 / 10

APTIS has a clear strategic direction (diversification away from tobacco dependency, premium brand acquisition, island market leadership) and Oscar Mamet has articulated priorities (efficiency, innovation, market leadership). What is missing is a shared, written, precise V/TO — a 10-year target, a 3-year picture, and 1-year plan that every subsidiary leader understands and is executing toward. The current vision lives in Oscar Mamet's head and in brand magazine interviews. EOS's V/TO would crystallise this into a document the whole leadership team can run from.

08 — Meeting Culture 4 / 10

No evidence of a structured group-level meeting rhythm is visible in any public source. With seven subsidiaries, five clusters, and multiple family and non-family MDs, the absence of a Level 10 Meeting structure means strategic issues almost certainly surface late, get resolved bilaterally, or get deferred. The BARNES integration in particular — combining a luxury real estate culture with a distribution and manufacturing group — needs a structured weekly forum to catch and resolve issues before they compound. This is the lowest-scoring dimension and one of the most actionable entry points for an EOS conversation.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for APTIS Group

Accountability Chart

Seven subsidiaries, five clusters, three Mamet family leaders, and five external MDs — all without a publicly defined organisational structure. Who owns what? Who reports to whom across entities? The Accountability Chart is the first tool APTIS needs: it will surface every authority gap, family hierarchy tension, and role duplication. This is where the EOS conversation starts.

Vision / Traction Organiser (V/TO)

Oscar Mamet's stated vision — diversification, market leadership, efficiency — exists in his head and in interview quotes. The V/TO would crystallise it into a shared 10-year target, 3-year picture, and annual plan that every subsidiary MD (from Fabien Harel at Resiglas to François Colin at BARNES) can execute against. Without this, each business is optimising for its own definition of success.

Scorecard & Measurables

With subsidiaries as diverse as a 167-person manufacturer and a 5-person fuel distributor, group-level performance visibility is almost certainly non-existent. A group Scorecard with 5–15 weekly leading indicators would give Oscar Mamet the single-page view of the entire business he currently lacks. This is the most immediate pain relief tool — and the one he will recognise fastest.

Rocks

The BARNES integration, Resiglas productivity improvement, RACSO growth, and L'Artigiano franchise expansion are all running simultaneously with no shared prioritisation framework. Rocks would give Oscar Mamet and his leadership team a way to set 3 priorities per quarter per entity, ensure focus, and measure completion. The current state — multiple acquisitions, multiple expansions, new CEO, all at once — is a recipe for execution dilution.

Level 10 Meeting
