Executive Summary

A 23-year-old founder-owned OOH media business with genuinely strong profitability and a clear digital pivot underway — but dark on financials since 2020 and operating with no formal management infrastructure whatsoever.

Rent A Sign Ltd (File No. C44964, BRN C09044964) was incorporated on 14 February 2003 and operates as Mauritius's leading Out-of-Home (OOH) advertising company. Founded and wholly owned by Serret Régis Thierry (known publicly as Thierry Serret), the company connects brands to audiences at key transit, retail, and lifestyle touchpoints across the island through a network of static and digital billboard assets. It operates principally from Suite 110, Moka Business Centre, Mount Ory Road, Moka, with a secondary operational base at Socota Phoenicia Ltd, Solferino, for its printing/graphics activities.

Ownership is 100% consolidated in a single individual: Serret Régis Thierry holds all 250 ordinary shares (stated capital MUR 25,000). There are no subsidiary investments, no group entities disclosed on the CBRIS record, and no institutional shareholders. This is a classic closely-held Mauritian founder business — every strategic, financial, and operational decision runs through one person. The secretary is Chaperon Didier Armand, appointed February 2018. Annual registration fees are current to 2026.

The last filed financial statements (FY ended 30 June 2020) show revenue of MUR 34.4 million, a gross margin of 87.3%, and a net profit of MUR 9.85 million (28.7% net margin) — exceptionally strong for a Mauritian SME. Total assets stood at MUR 53.6 million, dominated by MUR 31.1 million in PP&E (billboard infrastructure) and MUR 19.1 million in receivables. Equity is deep at MUR 39.4 million. The critical financial flag is the 5+ year gap in public filings: no accounts have been submitted since December 2020 (covering FY2020). The business has grown significantly since then — the 10-year Victoria Urban Terminal partnership (2022) and ongoing digital expansion suggest materially higher revenue — but there is no public evidence of the current financial position. EOS suitability scores 7.0/10: the growth tension is clear, the leadership openness signals are positive, but single-owner concentration is both a strength and a structural risk.

Key Finding — Founder Bottleneck in a Scaling Media Business
Rent A Sign has built a genuinely defensible OOH business with the strongest gross margin profile in its category (87%+), a 10-year institutional partnership at Victoria Urban Terminal, and a clear move toward premium digital formats. The company self-describes as collaborative and coaching-led — but the CBRIS record reveals absolute single-owner control, no co-directors, and a financial reporting gap that suggests the business has outgrown its administrative infrastructure. The entry angle for EOS is not operational — it is the classic founder-as-bottleneck story: Thierry Serret is simultaneously the sole shareholder, the sole director, the CEO, the rainmaker, and the strategy function. EOS's Accountability Chart and Rocks framework speak directly to this tension.

Products and Services Portfolio

Rent A Sign operates across three integrated capability clusters. First, static OOH media: the core business of billboard ownership, site leasing, and advertising space rental across high-traffic locations in Mauritius — roadsides, commercial districts, and transit corridors. Second, digital and interactive OOH: the company has been actively transitioning its inventory toward digital billboard formats, which allow time-based content scheduling, audience measurement, and interactive engagement. The Victoria Urban Terminal partnership (signed 2022, 10-year term) is the most visible manifestation of this pivot — deploying digital advertising networks within Port Louis's major public transport hub. Third, print and graphics production: computer-produced graphics, animation, large-format printing, and related visual production services, operated with 10+ staff at the Socota Phoenicia facility in Solferino. This vertical integration reduces reliance on external print vendors and supports faster campaign turnaround for clients.

The company brands its platform as "OOHLAA" (oohlaa.net — currently under maintenance), which appears to be a digital booking or campaign management interface, signalling an ambition to move beyond traditional agency-mediated sales toward self-serve or programmatic OOH — a global industry trend Rent A Sign appears to be pursuing locally.

Geographic Presence

Rent A Sign's primary commercial and administrative office is at Moka Business Centre, which positions it centrally on the island with good access to the main highway corridor. Operational presence extends to Solferino (production) and Port Louis (Victoria Urban Terminal digital network). The company describes itself as connecting brands "where they live, shop, work, travel and play" — suggesting island-wide billboard coverage rather than a concentration in a single area. No export or international operations have been disclosed or identified.

Digital Presence Gap
Both the primary website (billboard-mauritius.com) and the OOHLAA platform (oohlaa.net) are currently in maintenance/coming-soon status as of April 2026. For a company whose competitive differentiation rests partly on digital sophistication, a prolonged website absence is a credibility and lead-generation risk. This gap should be a conversation opener with Thierry Serret.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Incumbent site network built over 23 years. In OOH advertising, the primary competitive asset is not technology or talent — it is the physical location portfolio. Rent A Sign's billboard sites represent relationships, lease agreements, and planning permissions accumulated over two decades. This is a genuine barrier to entry that cannot be replicated quickly or cheaply by new entrants.

2. The Victoria Urban Terminal contract. A 10-year exclusive partnership signed in 2022 with a major institutional landlord (Victoria Station Ltd) for digital OOH rights across Port Louis's primary transport interchange. This contract provides a guaranteed revenue floor, an institutional reference for future pitches, and a showcase installation for digital capability. Exclusive long-term contracts of this nature are rare and extremely defensible.

3. Founder credibility and industry positioning. Thierry Serret's role as Secretary of the Outdoor Advertising Association of Mauritius gives Rent A Sign disproportionate voice in industry regulation, site-access advocacy, and standard-setting. This soft power is a real commercial advantage in a small-island market where relationships and reputation are the primary currency.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Global DOOH acceleration: The global digital OOH market reached USD 21.5 billion in 2024 and is forecast to grow at 10.3% CAGR to 2033. Rent A Sign's digital pivot is directionally aligned with the strongest growth vector in the global OOH industry.
  • Programmatic OOH emergence: The OOHLAA platform signals an ambition to participate in programmatic/self-serve OOH buying — a trend that is transforming OOH economics globally by reducing reliance on agencies and enabling data-driven targeting.
  • Mauritius tourism recovery and brand spend: Tourism-dependent advertising spend in Mauritius rebounded strongly post-COVID. OOH is a primary channel for hotel, airline, F&B, and financial services brands — Rent A Sign's client base is likely benefiting from this structural tailwind.
  • Metro Express advertising inventory: The Mauritius Metro Express rail network has created new OOH inventory in station and transit environments. Rent A Sign's experience at Victoria Urban Terminal positions it as a logical operator for transit-adjacent advertising contracts.
  • Competitive pressure from JCDecaux and Alliance Media: International OOH operators (JCDecaux has a presence at the Waterfront) bring capital, technology, and programmatic infrastructure that may outpace locally-owned operators unless Rent A Sign continues to invest aggressively in digital assets.
  • Digital shift reduces static print economics: The structural decline of vinyl/static format advertising over a 5–10 year horizon will compress margins in the print production unit and require continued capex investment in digital screen infrastructure to maintain overall revenue quality.
Digital Presence Gap — Strategic Risk
Both billboard-mauritius.com and oohlaa.net are currently offline or in maintenance. For a company selling digital advertising sophistication to clients, an inactive online presence is a credibility contradiction. This gap may reflect a deliberate rebranding in progress — but if prolonged, it risks ceding digital discovery and inbound lead generation to Alliance Media and JCDecaux who maintain active web presences.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

The company's own hiring language is notably EOS-compatible: "open, kind, inclusive culture that values teamwork and collaboration," "smart, driven team members," "coaching and training to empower people," "collaborative, results-driven approach with transparency and shared decision-making." 06 — Decision-Making Patterns 6 / 10

Single director, single shareholder, no evidence of a leadership team or co-founder — all strategic and operational decisions almost certainly route through Thierry Serret. The 10-year VUT partnership, the OOHLAA platform, the digital pivot — these are clearly founder-driven strategic calls. Score 6/10 acknowledges the aspiration for shared decision-making (per hiring language) but recognises the structural reality of concentrated authority.

Score 5/10 is neutral — not evidence of dysfunction, but no evidence of structure either.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Rent A Sign

Accountability Chart

Rent A Sign's most urgent need. With a single director managing media sales, digital operations, institutional partnerships, print production, finance, and strategy simultaneously, the absence of a defined leadership structure is the root cause of most operational friction. The Accountability Chart forces the company to define the seats it needs — and who (today or in future) sits in them.

Scorecard & Measurables

The 203-day DSO would not persist in a business that tracked weekly receivables ageing as a Scorecard metric. A simple set of 5–10 weekly numbers — DSO, new contracts signed, digital inventory utilisation, cash balance, payroll cost ratio — would surface the collections crisis and force accountability without requiring a management consultant.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

Three projects are visibly stalled or running long: (1) OOHLAA platform launch, (2) website restoration, (3) financial filings catch-up. A Rocks framework would force each of these into a defined 90-day commitment with a named owner and measurable completion criteria — instead of remaining indefinitely "in progress."

V/TO (Vision/Traction Organiser)

Thierry Serret has a clear personal vision for the digital OOH future. The V/TO converts that from a founder's private mental model into a written, shared document that the whole team can align to. In a company transitioning from static to digital infrastructure, having a written 3-year picture and 1-year plan is not optional — it is the difference between strategic growth and reactive firefighting.

Issues List & IDS

The billing/collections problem, the website gap, and the compliance filing backlog are all "issues" in EOS terms — persistent problems that require identification, discussion, and solution. IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve) gives the leadership team a structured method to work through these systematically rather than leaving them to the founder to resolve alone.

Level 10 Meeting

A regular weekly rhythm is the single most practical tool for a founder who currently has no formal management cadence.
