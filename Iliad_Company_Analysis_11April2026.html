Executive Summary

Iliad Group is a 12-year-old Mauritius property services conglomerate controlling six subsidiaries across real estate agency, syndic management, leisure, and investment — founder-led, quietly profitable at the holding level, yet operationally dependent on a management entity carrying deeply negative equity and MUR 32M in short-term borrowings.

Founded in January 2014 by Sébastien Lamusse and his father Joseph Yves Robert Lamusse (with co-founder Jacques Christian Jérôme Pilot), Iliad Group Ltd (C120602) is the apex holding company of the Iliad property services ecosystem in Mauritius. The group operates predominantly through Iliad Management Ltd (trading as Citya Iliad Immobilier under a franchise licence from the French Citya Group), delivering real estate sales, rental management, syndic (co-ownership) administration, and property advisory services to residential and commercial clients across the island. The group's registered address is Office 07, Le Hub Industrial Zone Road, Valentina, Phoenix, though the active operating offices are now situated at Ebène Junction and The Strand (Beau Plan), with further branches in Pointe aux Canonniers and Belle Rivière.

Ownership of Iliad Group Ltd is shared primarily between Sébastien Lamusse Thomas (593,895 shares, the largest individual block), Iliad Investment Ltd (910,009 + 200 shares — the Lamusse family vehicle), Jacques Christian Jérôme Pilot (331,377 shares across three tranches), and minority investors MIGI Alliance Ltd (111,893 shares), MDEUXT Holdings Ltd (163,536 shares), and Reef Retreat Limited (107,590 shares). The holding company in turn wholly owns Iliad Management Ltd, Iliad Grand Baie Ltd, and Iliad Properties Ltd, and holds a 50% joint venture in Iliad Leisure Ltd (the other 50% belonging to Currimjee Saeed Ali). At the holding level, two additional non-resident directors were appointed in November 2018: Jean Michel Pitot (c/o Attitude Resorts, Bain Boeuf) and Dominique Jean Rivière (Montpellier, France), suggesting strategic or investor linkage beyond the founding family.

Financially, the picture is bifurcated. Iliad Group Ltd itself reports MUR 20.9M in other income (FY June 2023) — almost certainly intercompany dividends or management fees — and sits on MUR 53.8M of total assets with positive equity of MUR 36.8M, making the holding healthy on paper. The operating entity, Iliad Management Ltd, tells a different story: MUR 3.35M revenue (FY June 2025), MUR 919K net profit, but deeply negative equity of −MUR 19.6M driven by accumulated losses of −MUR 19.3M and offset only by MUR 32.4M in short-term borrowings. Two subsidiaries (Iliad Grand Baie Ltd and Iliad Properties Ltd) are actively in winding-up proceedings. The EOS score is 6.4/10 — a qualified positive, with strong structural signals.

Key Finding — Structural Complexity Masking Operational Risk
The most important insight for Thierry's approach: Iliad is not a simple owner-managed business — it is a deliberately constructed multi-entity group where the founder retains majority control through a holding/investment vehicle structure, while the operational entity (Iliad Management) is technically insolvent on paper. Two subsidiaries are being wound up. This tells a story of a founder who is sophisticated about corporate architecture but may be running multiple balls without a coherent execution system tying them together. The entry angle is not financial distress (the group is actually viable) — it is growth complexity: a founder managing a franchise relationship, a 20+ person team across multiple locations, a JV, minority shareholders, and winding-down entities, all without an evident shared operating system. That is exactly the EOS sweet spot.

At a Glance

Commercial Strengths

Citya franchise licence provides brand credibility and a proven European real estate operating framework. The group manages 3,000+ syndic units across 35+ developments — sticky, recurring revenue. Multi-location presence (Ebène, Beau Plan, Pointe aux Canonniers, Belle Rivière) provides island-wide coverage. Founder has hospitality/luxury tourism background, giving genuine client relationship skills in the high-net-worth segment.

Financial Vulnerabilities

Iliad Management carries MUR −19.6M negative equity against only MUR 3.35M annual revenue — a 5.8× insolvency ratio that makes the operating entity technically dependent on group support. MUR 32.4M in short-term borrowings at the operating level is the critical number. Two subsidiaries (Grand Baie, Properties) have active winding-up minutes and MRA objections. Revenue is thin relative to asset base and group complexity.

EOS Readiness Signal

The multi-entity structure with a founding duo, outside investors, a JV partner, a franchise franchisor, and 20+ employees is a textbook "complexity has outgrown informal systems" scenario. No evidence of a formal operating cadence, accountability structure, or written vision/values. Sébastien Lamusse's hospitality school background suggests he is a relationship-driven operator who may feel the absence of structure but hasn't yet named EOS as the solution.

Products and Services Portfolio

Through Iliad Management Ltd (trading as Citya Iliad Immobilier), the group offers a comprehensive real estate services stack: (1) Transaction services — property sales (residential villas, apartments, PDS/IRS schemes, commercial, agricultural land) and rental transactions, executing 10+ transactions per month; (2) Rental management — full property management for landlords including tenant screening, rent collection, maintenance coordination, financial reporting, and litigation; (3) Syndic / co-ownership administration — the highest-value recurring revenue stream: 3,000+ units across 35+ developments, with dedicated syndic managers, accountants, and assistants delivering budget management, AGM facilitation, contractor tendering, and compliance; (4) Investment advisory — guidance for foreign investors (notably South African market focus) on IRS/PDS/Smart City schemes; (5) Digital portal — MyIliad client self-service platform for property owners and co-owners.

Iliad Leisure Ltd (50% owned, Currimjee JV) operates in amusement and recreation activities — the exact nature is not publicly disclosed but generated MUR 1.01M in revenue in FY June 2025 with an 85% gross margin before overheads pushed it to a MUR 290K loss.

The 2 Numbers That Matter Most
1. MUR 32.4M intercompany short-term borrowing at Iliad Management level — this is the structural time bomb. Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Citya franchise affiliation: The Citya brand (250+ agencies, France) provides instant credibility with French and European buyers/investors — Mauritius's largest foreign buyer segment. It also provides operational playbooks, training, and a referral pipeline that independent agencies cannot replicate. However, the franchise fee is a fixed overhead cost regardless of revenue performance.

2. Syndic scale: Managing 3,000+ co-ownership units across 35+ developments creates a data and relationship advantage that compounds over time. Once Iliad manages a development's syndic, they have first-mover access to every rental and resale transaction in that building. Competitors cannot easily displace a competent incumbent syndic without majority AGM votes from co-owners who have inertia on their side.

3. Multi-location island coverage: Four office locations (Ebène, Beau Plan, Pointe aux Canonniers, Belle Rivière) mirror the geographic distribution of Mauritius's property development activity. Most competing boutique agencies operate from a single location. This multi-location model increases brand visibility and allows the team to serve clients without long travel times — a real differentiator on an island where business relationships are local and personal.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds / Headwinds

  • Tailwind: Mauritius PDS/IRS/Smart City scheme pipeline remains robust into 2027–2028, creating new apartment complexes that will all need syndic management — Iliad's strongest revenue segment grows automatically with new completions.
  • Tailwind: South African and European high-net-worth investor appetite for Mauritius residential property remains strong, particularly post-COVID. Iliad's Citya affiliation and explicit South African investor focus positions it well.
  • Headwind: Digital platforms (Propertycloud.mu, PropertyMap.mu) are creating peer-to-peer listing markets that reduce the transaction intermediary role of agents. Commission-only revenue is at risk of long-term compression.
  • Headwind: International franchise agencies (JLL, Pam Golding, Coldwell Banker) are investing in Mauritius and bringing global brands and deeper capital — direct competitive pressure on Iliad's transaction and rental management segments.
  • Tailwind: Growing co-owner demand for digital syndic portals and transparent financial reporting plays to Iliad's MyIliad platform investment and its Citya systems infrastructure.
  • Risk: Mauritius Revenue Authority objections against Iliad Properties Ltd (two objections filed April 2025) suggest potential tax compliance issues in one subsidiary. Reputational spillover to the operating entity is a manageable but real risk if MRA proceedings escalate publicly.
Digital Gap — iliadproperties.mu Domain Failure
The iliadproperties.mu domain is not resolving as of April 2026. Given that Iliad Properties Ltd is in winding-up proceedings, this is likely a deliberate or neglected closure of that subsidiary's web presence. However, it creates brand confusion: clients searching for "Iliad Properties Mauritius" will hit a dead site before finding the active iliad.mu or iliadproperties.com. The group should either redirect the .mu domain to the main site or formally communicate the brand consolidation to the market.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 8 / 10

The group has 6 registered entities, 5 directors at the holding level (including 2 non-residents), a JV partner (Currimjee), a franchise franchisor (Citya France), 20+ staff across 4 locations, and 2 entities in active winding-up. This is precisely the organisational complexity that EOS was built for — multiple moving parts that have outgrown the founders' ability to manage by relationship and informal communication alone. Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Iliad Group

Accountability Chart

Vision/Traction Organiser (V/TO)

The proliferation of entities (some now being wound down) signals a lack of strategic filter. The V/TO forces Sébastien and Jérôme to answer: what are Iliad's Core Values, Core Focus, and 10-Year Target? Is the group building a syndic-dominant business, a full-service franchise network, or a hospitality-adjacent property platform? Until this is explicit, every new opportunity will distract the team rather than compound the strategy.

Scorecard with Measurables

MUR 3.35M revenue across 20+ staff suggests the team is not measured on output. A weekly Scorecard with 5–10 measurables (transactions closed, new syndic units onboarded, rental management mandates active, client NPS, aged debtors, revenue pipeline) would immediately surface which team members and locations are performing and which are not. Without measurables, the founders cannot distinguish effort from results.

Level 10 Meeting

Four locations, 20+ staff, a JV partner, and a franchise relationship create constant communication noise without a structured weekly rhythm. The Level 10 Meeting cadence — same time, same agenda, Issues List, IDS — would replace ad hoc WhatsApp coordination with a predictable execution loop. The multi-location structure makes this especially valuable: remote branch managers need a consistent touchpoint where their issues are actually resolved rather than deferred.

Rocks (Quarterly Priorities)

The evidence of the group running too many simultaneous initiatives (Leisure JV, Properties subsidiary, Grand Baie entity, all now being wound down) points to a classic lack of quarterly focus. The Rocks discipline — 3–7 priorities per quarter, clearly owned, clearly defined — would have likely killed these experiments earlier and redirected energy to the syndic growth engine, which is the group's real competitive moat.

Issues List and IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve)

The MUR 32.4M intercompany loan with no evident formal documentation, the two MRA objections on Iliad Properties, and the dual winding-up proceedings all represent issues that were allowed to accumulate rather than being identified and resolved quarterly. The Issues List and IDS process is the mechanism that surfaces and kills these problems before they become structural liabilities. Installing this habit in the leadership team is arguably the most protective financial intervention available.
