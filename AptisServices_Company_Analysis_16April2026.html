Executive Summary

APTIS Services is the operational engine and shared services hub of the APTIS Group — a 7-year-old management consultancy entity with MUR 58.8M in revenue but a loss-making P&L, negative equity, and a balance sheet that reveals the cost of running a group-wide services function that charges less than it costs to operate.

APTIS Services Company Limited (File No. C162742, BRN: C19162742) was incorporated on 5 March 2019 — one day before its sister company APTIS Property — and shares an identical governance structure: the same four directors, the same secretary (Navitas Corporate Services Ltd, appointed January 2024), the same auditor (BDO, appointed October 2025), and the same registered office at 2nd Floor, Hermes House, Inova Business Park, Riche Terre. Its registered business activity is "Management consultancy activities (Firm)" — confirming its role as the group's centralised services and management function.

The company is 100% owned by APTIS Holdings Ltd, the ultimate parent of the APTIS Group. The APTIS Group has evolved from TNS Tobacco (founded 2009 with an exclusive BAT distribution agreement) into a diversified group with five activity poles: Distribution (TNS Tobacco, TNS Consumer, L'Artigiano, Racso), Commerce (Resiglas), Services (Navitas, Harley Street Fertility Centre), Investment (Bigarade sur Mer, Dominos), and Real Estate (APTIS Property, BARNES Mauritius). APTIS Services appears to act as the management company providing shared services — HR, finance, administration, commercial support — across these divisions.

Financially, FY2024 is concerning. Revenue is a healthy MUR 58.8M, but administration costs of MUR 60.3M exceed revenue, producing a loss before tax of MUR -1.5M and a net loss of MUR -1.4M. With accumulated retained losses of MUR -2.3M, total equity is negative at MUR -2.3M (share capital of MUR 10,000 less accumulated deficit). Current liabilities of MUR 5.9M (all trade payables) against current assets of MUR 4.1M give a current ratio of 0.69× — below benchmark but workable. The MUR 2.1M long-term provisions suggest employee benefit obligations. This is a company that is operating as a cost centre for the group, with revenue likely being inter-company management fees that do not fully cover operating costs — a model that works only as long as the parent group chooses to sustain it.

Key Finding — Group Cost Centre Operating Below Cost Recovery
APTIS Services is spending MUR 1.50 to generate MUR 1.00 in revenue. The MUR 60.3M in administration costs against MUR 58.8M in management fee income suggests this entity operates as a group shared services centre that prices its services below full cost — either deliberately (as a subsidy to operating subsidiaries) or inadvertently (through inadequate cost allocation modelling). With negative equity of MUR -2.3M, the company is technically insolvent and entirely dependent on group support. The EOS suitability score of 6.5/10 reflects that the genuine operational complexity of running group-wide shared services is exactly where EOS tools deliver value — but the financial model must first be corrected.

At a Glance

Commercial Strengths

Substantial revenue base of MUR 58.8M indicating the group is large enough to sustain a meaningful shared services operation. Identical governance to APTIS Property means seamless group coordination. Centralised HR, finance, and commercial support functions provide structural value that is difficult to replicate at divisional level. BDO audit provides credibility.

Financial Vulnerabilities

Negative equity of MUR -2.3M is the critical alert — this entity cannot pay its own way. Administration costs 102.6% of revenue means every rupee of income generates a loss. The long-term provision of MUR 2.1M (likely staff gratuity obligations) represents a growing liability. The company is entirely dependent on group financial support to remain solvent.

This is the most EOS-ready entity in the APTIS group. A shared services operation managing HR, finance, commercial support, and management consultancy across five activity poles is precisely the kind of complex operational organisation that benefits from EOS Accountability Charts, Scorecard discipline, and structured meeting rhythms. The loss-making position signals a classic EOS symptom: lack of clarity about who owns what, resulting in cost overruns.

The 2 Numbers That Matter Most
1. Administration Costs at 102.6% of Revenue (Red) — MUR 60.3M in admin costs against MUR 58.8M in management fee revenue means this entity consistently destroys value. To reach a 10% EBIT margin, management fees would need to increase by approximately MUR 7.4M (to MUR 66.2M), or costs would need to fall by the same amount. The CFO (Sabbi Appanna) and CEO (Oscar Mamet) must address the transfer pricing model that determines intra-group fee levels — this is the most urgent financial action in the entire APTIS group.

Without a capital injection of at least MUR 2.35M from APTIS Holdings, or a return to profitability sustained over 2–3 years, the technical insolvency will worsen. Under the Mauritius Companies Act 2001, directors have obligations when a company is insolvent — the BDO audit appointment should be accompanied by a board resolution addressing the solvency position and documenting the parent's ongoing support commitment.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Captive Group Client Base: APTIS Services has a guaranteed revenue stream from its sister companies — it cannot lose its clients to a competitor as long as the group chooses to maintain centralised shared services. This is both an advantage (revenue security) and a risk (no market validation of pricing or quality).

2. Institutional Knowledge and Group Alignment: Having operated since 2019 with the same leadership team across all entities, APTIS Services has accumulated institutional knowledge of the group's operations, history, supplier relationships, and culture that an external provider would take years to develop.

3. Platform for Post-BARNES Integration: With the May 2025 acquisition of BARNES Mauritius (35 staff, six offices), APTIS Services is positioned as the natural integration vehicle — providing BARNES with group HR, finance, IT, and administrative infrastructure. This represents an expansion of its mandate and a potential revenue opportunity.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Shared services consolidation trend: Mauritius businesses are increasingly recognising the efficiency benefits of shared services models. APTIS's approach is ahead of many comparable Mauritius family groups — though the financial model needs correction to realise the value.
  • BARNES integration creates immediate demand: The acquisition of a 35-person, six-office luxury real estate operation immediately expands APTIS Services' mandate — HR onboarding, payroll integration, IT infrastructure, and policy alignment for BARNES staff represent a concrete near-term growth driver.
  • Mauritius labour law complexity increasing: The Mauritius Workers' Rights Act and related employment legislation is creating increasing compliance complexity. A centralised HR function (APTIS Services) is better positioned than each subsidiary managing this independently.
  • Rising professional services costs (headwind): Mauritius is experiencing professional salary inflation, particularly in finance, HR, and ICT. This directly pressures APTIS Services' MUR 60.3M cost base — which is predominantly people costs — making the break-even problem harder to solve without fee increases.
  • Digital transformation requirements: Group-scale digital transformation (ERP, HRIS, CRM) requires centralised management capability. APTIS Services is the logical vehicle for group-wide technology implementation — but this requires investment that will initially worsen the cost position.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 6 / 10

The MUR 60.3M administration cost base implies a significant operational headcount — conservatively, at Mauritius private sector salary levels (average professional MUR 40,000–80,000/month), this suggests a team of 60–120 employees. Job postings under APTIS Services confirm deployment across multiple Mauritius locations. At 60–120 people, the company sits squarely in the EOS sweet spot. The post-BARNES integration (adding 35 people) is moving this further into the zone where people management complexity creates measurable friction.

02 — Leadership Openness 6 / 10

Oscar Mamet's January 2024 appointment as CEO with a clear succession narrative, his active LinkedIn presence, and the structured group governance evolution (Navitas as secretary, BDO as auditor) signal openness to professionalisation. However, the loss-making position — if not proactively addressed — suggests that leadership has not yet installed the financial discipline frameworks that EOS complements. Anne Régine de Ravel's HR Director role at the group level indicates someone who understands people systems — a potential EOS champion internally.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

This is the highest-scoring dimension. Classic EOS pain points are all visible: the shared services entity is loss-making (accountability gap); administration costs exceed revenue without anyone appearing to own a cost reduction mandate (execution gap); the BARNES acquisition creates immediate integration complexity with people from a different culture (people and process gap); a new CEO in his second year of tenure is building his leadership team (vision and traction gap). These are textbook EOS-addressable symptoms — this company is ready for EOS now.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 4 / 10

The fact that APTIS Services has been operating at a loss for multiple years (negative retained earnings implies sustained losses since incorporation) without any visible corrective action suggests that management reporting systems are either inadequate or not driving decision-making. The identical four-director boards across all entities means there is no dedicated management layer between the group board and operational staff — a structural gap that EOS's Accountability Chart would directly address. Score: 4/10 — professional services providers in place externally, but internal management systems appear informal.

05 — Company Culture 6 / 10

The MUR 2.1M long-term provision for employee gratuities suggests a stable, relatively long-tenured workforce — you only accumulate significant gratuity provisions if staff stay. This points to a culture where people remain, which is a positive EOS indicator. The APTIS Group's stated values of entrepreneurial spirit and growth suggest an energetic culture. The risk is that a cost-centre entity that is perpetually loss-making signals to staff that their function is undervalued — a cultural erosion risk if not addressed.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 4 / 10

The sustained loss-making position (multiple years of accumulated deficit) without apparent corrective pricing or cost action suggests that the management fee transfer pricing model was set at inception and has not been reviewed as costs evolved. This points to decision-making inertia — possibly because the decision to reprice intra-group fees requires a group board decision, and no individual has clear accountability for P&L at the APTIS Services level. EOS's concept of a single accountable seat owner for each function would directly resolve this.

07 — Vision Clarity 5 / 10

The role of APTIS Services within the group — management company, shared services hub, or strategic consultancy — is not clearly defined in any public-facing material. This ambiguity at entity level is a proxy for ambiguity internally: do the 60–120 employees of APTIS Services know exactly what the company's 3-year goal is, and how their work contributes? EOS V/TO would force this clarity. The CEO transition creates a natural moment to re-establish the vision for the management function.

08 — Meeting Culture 4 / 10

No evidence of structured meeting rhythms. The co-location of all four directors means informal alignment replaces structured decision-making. The sustained loss without corrective action suggests that regular structured review of financial performance (what EOS's Scorecard and L10 would provide) is absent. The annual return meetings (filed promptly, but only once a year) are the only structured governance rhythm visible externally.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for APTIS Services

Accountability Chart

The four-director board with identical composition across all APTIS entities has no operational management layer beneath it. An Accountability Chart would define who runs APTIS Services day-to-day — HR lead, Finance lead, Commercial lead — creating seat-based ownership that the current structure entirely lacks.

Scorecard

APTIS Services has been losing money for multiple years without a visible corrective response. A weekly Scorecard tracking: management fee revenue, cost-to-revenue ratio, headcount, BARNES integration milestones, and cash position would have surfaced the P&L deterioration immediately and forced action.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

Three immediate Rocks write themselves: (1) management fee repricing exercise with Sabbi Appanna as accountable owner, (2) BARNES HR integration plan with Anne Régine as owner, (3) capital injection / balance sheet resolution with Oscar as owner. 90-day Rocks would convert these from chronic issues into time-bound commitments.

Issues List (IDS)

The sustained loss, the negative equity, and the unclear role definition of APTIS Services are all issues that have been quietly tolerated. EOS's IDS process — Identify, Discuss, Solve — is designed precisely for issues that have been avoided because they involve cross-entity decisions or founder-level authority to resolve.

People Analyser

With a team of 60–120 people deployed across the group, and a post-BARNES integration adding 35 more, the People Analyser (Core Values + GWC — Get it, Want it, Capacity to do it) would help identify the right seats for the right people in the enlarged APTIS Services function.

Level 10 Meeting (L10)

The most impactful single EOS tool for APTIS Services. A weekly 90-minute L10 with the management team — reviewing Scorecard, Rocks, and Issues List — would replace the informal corridor governance model with structured accountability, bringing the loss-making P&L under active management within weeks.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations prioritised by urgency. Financial stabilisation must precede EOS implementation — but EOS should be proposed as the tool that prevents the financial situation from recurring.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Reprice Intra-Group Management Fees to Full Cost Recovery Plus Margin

The most urgent action: commission Sabbi Appanna (CFO) and BDO to model the full cost of delivering group management services and set management fees at cost plus 10–15% margin. At the current cost level (MUR 60.3M), the target management fee revenue should be MUR 66–69M — an increase of MUR 7–11M, to be allocated across APTIS subsidiaries in proportion to headcount or services consumed. This single action, implemented in Q3 2026, resolves the P&L deficit within one year.

Immediate Priority — Before Next Financial Year
Recommendation 02
