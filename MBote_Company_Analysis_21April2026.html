Executive Summary

M'Bote is the property development brand of the Aptis Group — a profitable but structurally undercapitalised real estate holding entity whose MUR 286M investment property portfolio generates strong margins but sits behind a wall of MUR 155M in current liabilities it cannot easily service from its MUR 6.5M in liquid assets.

M'Bote is not a standalone registered company but a brand within APTIS Property Company Limited (File No. C162801, BRN C19162801), incorporated 6 March 2019 and wholly owned by APTIS Holdings Ltd. The Aptis Group, headquartered at Hermes House, Innova Business Park, Riche Terre, has operated in Mauritius since 2009. Its Property cluster — branded externally as M'Bote and TNS Property — manages a portfolio of investment properties and has an EDB-approved Real Estate Scheme (RES) project comprising 5 luxury villas and a gym at Grand Gaube in northern Mauritius. The group completed a significant strategic pivot in May 2025 with the acquisition of BARNES Mauritius, one of the island's top three luxury real estate agencies, adding 35 professionals and MUR 78M in annual revenue to the property cluster.

APTIS Property is 100% owned by APTIS Holdings Ltd (C162483), which in turn is controlled by CHT Holdings Co Ltd (77.8%) with Jean-Pierre Mamet (Founder & President) and Anne Régine de Ravel (Group HR) each holding 11.1%. The group's leadership succession is in progress: Oscar Mamet was appointed Group CEO in January 2024, signalling a generational transition. The holding company is well-capitalised (equity MUR 635M, total assets MUR 856M) but the Property subsidiary carries a serious liquidity mismatch that demands attention before any EOS engagement conversation.

APTIS Property's FY2024 financials show revenue of MUR 21.3M (rental income from its MUR 286M investment property portfolio), a clean gross profit margin of 100% (no cost of sales), and a net margin of 57.5% (MUR 12.3M PAT after MUR 8.5M other income and MUR 6.6M admin costs). The critical concern is the balance sheet: current liabilities of MUR 155.6M — almost entirely trade and other payables — dwarf current assets of only MUR 6.5M, producing a current ratio of 0.04x. This is consistent with intercompany payables to the parent group rather than third-party debt, but the structure warrants direct clarification in any commercial conversation. The EOS suitability score for the group overall is 5.3/10 — a moderate-positive candidate at the holding/group level, where the real leadership team and operational complexity reside.

Key Finding — The Real EOS Conversation Is at Aptis Group Level, Not Entity Level
M'Bote/APTIS Property is a holding vehicle for property assets, not an operating business in the EOS sense — it has no staff of its own visible in filings, no sales team, and no management layer beyond group directors. The EOS conversation must be framed at the Aptis Group level with Oscar Mamet, where the real organisational complexity, team structure, and growth pain points exist. The property cluster's rapid expansion (from a small RES development to the BARNES acquisition adding 35 professionals) is precisely the kind of scaling inflection point that creates EOS-classic symptoms: role ambiguity, lack of accountability structure, and strained management bandwidth across four business clusters simultaneously.

Company Overview

M'Bote is the property development and investment brand of Aptis Group, operated through APTIS Property Company Limited — a private domestic company incorporated in 2019 to hold and develop the group's real estate assets, now managing a MUR 286M investment property portfolio and an EDB-approved luxury villa project in Grand Gaube.

Founding and Corporate Record

APTIS Property Company Limited was incorporated on 6 March 2019 (File No. C162801, BRN C19162801) as a private domestic company under Mauritius law. Its registered office is 2nd Floor, Hermes House, Inova Business Park, Riche Terre, Mauritius — the same address as the parent group. Stated capital is MUR 10,000 (100 ordinary shares at MUR 100 par value). The company's secretary is Navitas Corporate Services Ltd (appointed 1 January 2024, Navitas House, Floreal), and its auditor is Messrs. BDO (appointed 24 October 2025). The registered business name is "APTIS Property" with the declared nature of business being real estate activities with rental of own or leased property. Annual returns have been filed promptly for all three available years (2022, 2023, 2024) and financial statements approved on time through FY2024. No charges are registered against the company.

The M'Bote brand has a longer heritage: M'Boté Company Limited (C111481) was incorporated on 2 August 2012, held under Floor to Roof Ltd, and operated as the original vehicle for the group's residential construction activities including the Grand Gaube RES project (5 villas + gym). M'Boté Company Limited was removed under Section 309(1)D of the Companies Act on 10 February 2025 — this is a voluntary strike-off mechanism, suggesting the project or its assets were folded into APTIS Property Company Limited as the group rationalised its corporate structure. Floor to Roof Ltd (C105878) itself entered creditors' winding up under the Insolvency Act 2009 in March 2023 and remains in liquidation as of the report date, with liquidator Abdul Aleem Ramanokhan appointed.

Products and Services Portfolio

The M'Bote / APTIS Property cluster operates across three distinct segments. First, investment property holding and management: the company's balance sheet shows MUR 286.23M in investment properties generating MUR 21.3M in annual rental income — a commercial property portfolio (likely including the Hermes House / Innova Business Park presence) let to third parties or intra-group entities. Second, residential development: the EDB-approved RES project at Grand Gaube comprising 5 luxury villas and a gym, originally developed under M'Boté Company Limited, now folded into APTIS Property. Grand Gaube is a premium coastal location in northern Mauritius, attractive to both local high-net-worth buyers and foreign investors under the RES scheme (no minimum price threshold for foreigners). Third, luxury real estate brokerage: the May 2025 acquisition of BARNES Mauritius (6 offices, 35 professionals, MUR 78M revenue in 2024) positions the group as an end-to-end property player — developer, investor, and agent — across the full market cycle. BARNES operates across Grand Baie, Moka, Belle Mare, Floréal, Mahébourg, and Tamarin, giving the property cluster island-wide presence.

Geographic Presence

APTIS Property's registered office and investment properties are concentrated at the Hermes House / Innova Business Park, Riche Terre — an established commercial and light-industrial zone near Port Louis and the port, well-served by the motorway network. The Grand Gaube RES project extends the footprint to the premium northern coast, where property values have appreciated significantly (northern Mauritius accounted for 51% of EDB scheme transactions in 2023–2024). The BARNES acquisition adds six sales offices across the island, transforming APTIS Property from a concentrated north-coast player into a genuinely national property group. The group headquarters at Riche Terre serves all cluster operations.

Digital Presence Gap — M'Bote Brand
Despite being listed on the Aptis Group website under the Property cluster, M'Bote has no independent website, no dedicated social media presence, and no searchable property listings under the M'Bote brand. All digital equity flows to APTIS Group (aptis.mu) and BARNES Mauritius. This creates brand confusion for potential buyers or tenants who may search for M'Bote directly and find nothing. The Grand Gaube RES project also has no public-facing marketing page, suggesting the development may be sold through private networks or the nascent BARNES channel.

Charge Register

No charges are registered against APTIS Property Company Limited as at the CBRIS report date (22 April 2026). This is noteworthy given the MUR 286M investment property portfolio — the absence of mortgage charges suggests either the properties are unencumbered, or that financing structures (if any) are held at the APTIS Holdings level rather than the property subsidiary. The MUR 4.57M long-term borrowings on the balance sheet are modest and unsecured in the public register. This clean charge register is a commercial positive and supports a straightforward conversation about property assets with any financial partner or prospective EOS client.

The MUR 155M Payables Question
The single most important financial clarification before any commercial engagement is the nature of the MUR 155.5M in current trade and other payables. If these are intercompany balances owed to APTIS Holdings (the most likely interpretation given the group structure and the fact that APTIS Holdings shows MUR 150.4M in its own current liabilities), they represent a reclassification of intra-group financing rather than genuine third-party creditor exposure. If they were third-party, the company would be technically insolvent on a current basis. Thierry should probe this directly with Oscar Mamet or Sabbi Appanna in the first meeting.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Vertical integration across the property value chain: APTIS Property is now uniquely positioned as a developer (Grand Gaube RES), investor (MUR 286M portfolio), and broker (BARNES, top-3 luxury agency) simultaneously. Very few Mauritius property players operate across all three roles. This creates the ability to originate deals, hold assets, and sell properties through owned distribution — a genuine competitive moat if managed with integrated strategy.

2. Conglomerate synergies and captive demand: As part of a group with four business clusters (Distribution, Commerce, Property, Associates), APTIS Property benefits from captive tenancy demand from sister companies (TNS Consumer, RACSO, Resiglas, Navitas) occupying or using Riche Terre premises. This provides a baseline occupancy floor that third-party developers cannot replicate — reducing void risk materially.

3. BARNES international brand and client network: The BARNES international network (operating in 20+ countries) gives APTIS Property direct access to offshore HNW clients seeking Mauritius property investment. The Mauritius market is heavily dependent on foreign buyer demand (RES scheme transactions were 33% of all EDB approvals), and BARNES provides a legitimate channel to source those buyers — particularly from France, South Africa, and the UAE.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Strong foreign buyer demand: 6,000+ EDB scheme transactions in 2024 (646 in 2023 alone, +21% YoY) with average apartment prices up 23% — the market is in a sustained upcycle that validates the BARNES acquisition timing.
  • Northern Mauritius premium: Grand Gaube sits in the northern region that accounted for 51% of all EDB scheme transactions in 2023–2024. The M'Bote Grand Gaube project is in the right geography at the right time.
  • Rising registration duties from July 2026: Non-citizen buyers face registration duty and land transfer tax increases from 5% to 10% from 1 July 2026 — this creates an incentive to transact before the deadline, potentially accelerating demand for the Grand Gaube villas in the next 12–18 months.
  • Generational leadership transition risk: Oscar Mamet (new CEO, January 2024) is managing a complex four-cluster group plus a significant acquisition (BARNES) in his first two years. Without strong governance infrastructure, this transition creates execution risk across all business units including Property.
  • Floor to Roof Ltd winding up — legacy risk: The creditors' winding up of Floor to Roof Ltd (original parent of M'Boté Company Limited, in liquidation since March 2023) could generate lingering claims or liabilities that surface against the current APTIS Property entity if not properly ring-fenced. The November 2022 objection on the liquidation file is a flag to investigate.
  • Mauritius commercial property yield compression: As institutional capital flows into Mauritius real estate, commercial yields are expected to compress from the current 5–9% range toward 4–6% over 2025–2027 — reducing the attractiveness of holding unleveraged commercial property and increasing pressure to either add value or redeploy capital into development.

This sits squarely in the EOS sweet spot of 10–250 employees. Highest-Priority EOS Tools for APTIS Group / M'Bote

Accountability Chart

The group has four cluster MDs and a four-person holding board but no visible cross-cluster accountability structure. The property cluster alone now has three sub-businesses (M'Bote, TNS Property, BARNES) with unclear reporting lines below group directors. An Accountability Chart is the single most urgent tool — it will immediately surface role duplication, gaps, and the founder/CEO authority question that must be resolved for BARNES integration to succeed.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

The BARNES acquisition creates at least three major 90-day priorities that currently have no formal owner: integration of 35 BARNES staff, establishing a BARNES/APTIS Property joint pipeline, and resolving the Grand Gaube RES project status (5 villas, development progress unknown). Without Rocks, these priorities will compete for attention and none will be completed on time.

V/TO (Vision/Traction Organiser)

Oscar Mamet needs to articulate a 3-year and 10-year vision that integrates all four clusters under a single strategic narrative. The current group story (started in tobacco, now diversified) is a history, not a vision. A V/TO session would force the leadership team to answer the hardest question: what does APTIS Group want to be in 10 years? The answer will determine whether M'Bote is a core asset or an intermediary step.

Level 10 Meeting (L10)

With four clusters, six BARNES offices, and a group board that functions as the operating committee, weekly L10 meetings at both the group and cluster level are essential. The BARNES integration is being managed from Riche Terre by a GM (François Colin) who previously reported to Oscar — without a structured weekly cadence, alignment will drift within months. L10 is the anchor that keeps the BARNES acquisition on track.

Issues List and IDS

The M'Boté Company Limited strike-off, Floor to Roof winding up, the MUR 155M intercompany payables, and the BARNES integration all represent unresolved organisational issues that will fester without a formal Issues List and Issue-Discuss-Solve process. In a family-owned conglomerate, difficult issues tend to be avoided to preserve relationships. EOS's IDS process gives the leadership team permission to surface and resolve what is currently undiscussable.

Scorecard

APTIS Property has one meaningful financial metric visible from outside (rental yield of 7.4%) but no evidence of a weekly or monthly management scorecard across the property cluster. With BARNES now added, the property cluster should track: occupancy rate, rental income per property, BARNES transaction volume, Grand Gaube villa sales progress, and pipeline value. A Scorecard would give Oscar Mamet a real-time pulse on the cluster's health without needing to attend every meeting.
