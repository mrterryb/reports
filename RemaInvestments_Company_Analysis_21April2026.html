Executive Summary

Rema Investments Ltd is an eight-year-old domestic holding company incorporated in 2017, sitting at the apex of the Aptis Group's investment portfolio, whose entire strategic value lies in MUR 58.7 million of subsidiary investments — not in any trading activity of its own.

Rema Investments Ltd (File No. C151754, BRN C17151754) was incorporated on 14 November 2017 as a private domestic company in Mauritius. It operates exclusively as a holding and investment vehicle, generating no turnover of its own. Its registered office is at the Inova Business Park, 2nd Floor, Hermes House, Riche Terre — the same address used by the broader Aptis Group. The company has no employees in its own right; its function is to hold, manage, and invest through subsidiary entities. Its secretary since January 2024 is Navitas Corporate Services Ltd, itself an Aptis Group entity.

Rema Investments is jointly owned by APTIS Holdings Ltd (59.1% — 34,704 shares) and OLKAT Investments Ltd (40.9% — 24,073 shares), out of a total stated capital of MUR 58,777,020 across 58,777 ordinary shares. APTIS Holdings is the main listed group holding vehicle for the Aptis Group, a diversified Mauritian conglomerate active in distribution (TNS Tobacco, TNS Consumer, L'Artigiano), commerce (RACSO, Resiglas), property (TNS Property, M'Bote, BARNES Mauritius), and investment (Rema Investments). The Aptis Group traces its roots to 2009, rebranding from TNS to Aptis to reflect its growing diversification. OLKAT Investments represents a secondary founding-family co-investment vehicle. The six directors — Karin Harel, Simon Harel, Jean-Pierre Mamet, Andre Rey, Dominique Rey, and Sabbi Appanna — are all senior principals of the Aptis Group, appointed on the day of incorporation.

Financially, Rema Investments is structurally a dormant holding shell at the P&L level: FY2024 revenue is MUR 0, with a net loss of MUR 151,989 arising purely from administrative costs. Its balance sheet is dominated by MUR 58,716,000 in investments in subsidiaries, representing 99.97% of total assets. The company is adequately capitalised (equity MUR 58.5M vs. total liabilities of just MUR 182,549) and carries no debt or charges. The EOS suitability score of 4.0/10 reflects the structural reality: EOS is a framework for operating businesses, not holding vehicles. The meaningful EOS conversation is at the Aptis Group operational entity level.

Key Finding — This Is the Group's Investment Arm, Not an Operating Business
Rema Investments Ltd functions purely as a holding vehicle within the Aptis Group structure. It has no revenue, no employees, and no independent operational capability. The real strategic conversation with Oscar Mamet and Jean-Pierre Mamet should focus on which operating entities sit beneath Rema — and whether EOS can be implemented there. The MUR 58.7M in subsidiary investments is the asset to understand: who manages those subsidiaries, how they perform, and whether accountability and execution systems are in place at that level is the central question for LMA Growth Partners.

3. Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Growing sophistication of Mauritius holding structures: The Registrar's consistent annual return and financial filing requirements create pressure for professionalisation of holding company governance — an EOS-adjacent opportunity at the group level.
  • Aptis Group's proven acquisition capability: The BARNES Mauritius acquisition (luxury real estate, 2024) demonstrates the group's willingness and ability to deploy capital for strategic bolt-ons — Rema may be the legal vehicle through which future Associates-division deals are structured.
  • Mauritius healthcare infrastructure gap: The Harley Street Fertility Centre partnership reflects a broader trend of private capital filling healthcare service gaps in Mauritius. Fertility treatment demand is rising regionally. This could be an emerging growth investment theme for the Associates division.

  • Interest rate environment: With no debt, Rema is insulated from interest rate risk. But the flip side is that the company is not using leverage to amplify returns on its MUR 58.7M asset base — a potential missed opportunity if subsidiariy returns are strong.
  • Family-group succession risk: The Aptis Group's six-director board structure across Rema — all founding generation — carries implicit succession planning risk. No next-generation leadership is visible in the public record. This is an EOS-relevant pain point at the group level.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 2 / 10

Rema Investments itself has zero employees and no operational activity — it is structurally outside the EOS sweet spot. However, the Aptis Group as a whole is very much within it: a multi-sector group with at least 6 operating subsidiaries, leadership across 4+ clusters, and demonstrated scaling complexity (distribution to healthcare to luxury real estate). The EOS conversation belongs at the Aptis Group level, not at Rema specifically. Score reflects the holding vehicle assessed in isolation.

02 — Leadership Openness 6 / 10

The Aptis Group leadership — Jean-Pierre Mamet (Founder/President), Oscar Mamet (Group CEO), and Sabbi Appanna (Group CFO) — has demonstrated a pattern of strategic openness: rebranding from TNS to Aptis, entering luxury real estate via BARNES Mauritius, partnering with Harley Street Fertility Centre, and professionalising through Navitas. These are signals of a leadership team that is willing to evolve structures. The multi-family board, however, introduces relationship dynamics that can complicate accountability installations. Score: moderate-high openness at the executive level, with family governance friction as the key qualifier.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 7 / 10

The Aptis Group exhibits classic scaling-complexity pain points: five-sector diversification since 2009, seven-plus operating entities under a single group umbrella, a multi-family leadership board, simultaneous expansion into luxury real estate and healthcare, and Navitas being professionalised with a new CEO (Julien Rey). These are textbook EOS symptoms — too many moving parts, unclear accountability across clusters, potential vision fragmentation. The holding structure (Rema + APTIS Holdings) suggests the group is trying to create structure but has not yet installed a formal management operating system. Strong EOS candidate at the group level.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

At the Rema holding level, governance is functional: annual returns filed on time, financial statements approved and filed, a professional secretary (Navitas Corporate Services) in place since 2024. This is baseline compliance, not sophisticated management infrastructure. At the operating entity level within Aptis, the group has developed cluster structures (four divisions) and appointed sector-specific leaders (Fabien Harel at Resiglas, Jean-Alain Mamet at RACSO, Daniela Quaranta at L'Artigiano, Julien Rey at Navitas, François Colin at BARNES). This is partial organisational architecture — more advanced than most Mauritius SMEs, but likely lacking the consistent rhythms, scorecards, and accountability structures EOS provides.

05 — Company Culture 6 / 10

The Aptis Group publishes a quarterly internal magazine ("UP TO IT") and profiles employees on its blog, signalling investment in culture and employer branding. Navitas was highlighted as "une marque employeur exemplaire" (exemplary employer brand). These are green flags for a culture that values people development — a strong EOS prerequisite. The multi-family board dynamic may create competing cultural sub-groups within the group, however. The vision statement ("enabling businesses to grow and be leaders in their fields") is directionally sound but lacks the precision that a V/TO would provide.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 4 / 10

A six-person board with no publicly visible operating committee, committee charter, or governance framework below board level is a classic accountability ambiguity signal. The simultaneous multi-sector expansion — distribution, commerce, property, healthcare, luxury real estate — across seven-plus entities, all with different sector heads but no visible integrator role or operating cadence, suggests decision-making is founder-heavy and relationship-driven. Oscar Mamet as Group CEO is the logical EOS integrator candidate, but no evidence exists of a structured decision-making framework beneath him.

07 — Vision Clarity 5 / 10

The Aptis Group's public vision — "to be a diversified group enabling our businesses to grow and be leaders in their respective fields" — is aspirational but not specific. No 10-year target, no 3-year picture, no defined core focus or primary market. The diversification into luxury real estate (BARNES), healthcare (Harley Street), and distribution (L'Artigiano franchise expansion) in parallel suggests opportunistic growth strategy rather than a clearly articulated focus. This is exactly the ambiguity a V/TO session would resolve. Jean-Pierre Mamet's public statements focus on sector access and people potential — not on specific growth targets or a defined future state.

08 — Meeting Culture 3 / 10

No evidence of any structured meeting rhythms at the Aptis Group level — no quarterly business reviews, no published annual planning cycle, no board committee cadence visible in public records. Rema's annual shareholder meetings are implied by the annual return filing dates (June/July each year), suggesting a once-a-year formal governance rhythm. For an operating group of this complexity and size, reactive or ad-hoc meeting patterns are likely. The internal magazine "UP TO IT" suggests communication exists, but communication is not the same as structured decision-making rhythms. This dimension is the single biggest EOS opportunity.

The recommended conversation with Oscar Mamet is not "can we implement EOS at Rema?" but "can we implement EOS across the Aptis Group's operating entities — starting with the cluster that has the most traction-to-accountability gap?" The gating factor is the multi-family board dynamic: any EOS implementation will need explicit buy-in from the Harel and Rey family principals, not just the Mamet family alone.

Rocks — 90-Day Priorities

The group's simultaneous execution of BARNES luxury real estate entry, L'Artigiano franchise expansion, Navitas CEO transition, and Harley Street partnership suggests an execution-bandwidth problem. Rocks would force prioritisation across competing group-level initiatives — preventing the classic multi-sector conglomerate trap of spreading leadership attention too thin across too many fronts simultaneously.

Level 10 Meeting

No evidence of structured weekly or monthly leadership rhythms across the Aptis cluster heads. Implementing Level 10 Meetings at the group leadership level (CEO + cluster heads) would be the single fastest way to improve execution velocity across the seven-plus operating entities. The pattern of strategic announcements (BARNES acquisition, Harley Street partnership) without visible follow-through tracking suggests decisions are made but not systematically executed against.

Scorecard & Measurables

As a holding group with seven operating entities across five sectors, Aptis almost certainly lacks a single-page group-level scorecard that gives Jean-Pierre Mamet and Oscar Mamet a weekly pulse on the health of the entire portfolio. A group-level scorecard with 5–10 leading indicators per cluster would replace subjective judgment calls with data-driven triage. Sabbi Appanna as Group CFO is the natural owner of this tool.

Issues List & IDS

Multi-family, multi-sector conglomerates accumulate issues that are never fully resolved — they are managed around, deferred, or avoided to preserve family relationships. IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve) would give the Aptis leadership a structured, depersonalised framework for resolving the cross-cluster tensions (resource allocation, succession, inter-entity service pricing, governance) that inevitably build up when three founding families and a professional CFO co-govern seven-plus businesses.
