Executive Summary

A 68-year-old pioneering manufacturer that has quietly become Mauritius's dominant fibreglass brand, now recording MUR 253M in revenue under new professional leadership — but operating in a structure where EOS adoption requires unlocking group-level permission.

Resiglas Company Limited (file C1504, BRN C07001504) was incorporated in December 1965 and traces its commercial roots to 1956, when founder Hervé Closel began importing fibreglass from Europe. By 1958 the company was building boats; by 1970 it was manufacturing water tanks; and by 1991 it had pioneered fibreglass swimming pools in the Indian Ocean. Today, Resiglas operates from its headquarters at Royal Road, Calebasses, and manufactures across five product universes — Storage, Nautical, Poolside, Professional, and Sustainable — holding ISO 9001:2015 certification and serving both household and B2B customers across Mauritius and the wider Indian Ocean region.

The company has been 100% owned by Aptis Holdings Ltd since 2018, which in turn is the vehicle of the Mamet family (Jean-Pierre Mamet, Chairman and Founder of Aptis Group; Oscar Mamet, Group CEO). Aptis is a diversified Mauritian conglomerate with clusters in Distribution (TNS Tobacco, TNS Consumer, L'Artigiano), Commerce (Resiglas, RACSO), and Property (TNS Property, M'Bote). Resiglas is the flagship of the Commerce cluster. The group provides financial backing, corporate services (secretary: Navitas Corporate Services Ltd), and strategic direction — but also imposes a layer of governance that any EOS conversation must navigate before reaching Resiglas's own management team.

Financially, Resiglas is in strong shape: FY2024 (year ended 31 December 2024) shows turnover of MUR 253.4M, gross profit of MUR 62.4M (24.6% gross margin), and net profit after tax of MUR 28.4M (11.2% net margin). The balance sheet is robust — MUR 284.5M in total assets, MUR 196.1M in equity, and no long-term borrowings. The company is effectively debt-free and self-funding. The four charges registered with CBRIS (dated 1998–2002, totalling MUR 24.5M) are legacy instruments from pre-Aptis ownership and appear to have no current operational significance. EOS suitability scores 7.2/10, driven by the company's ideal size (~150–167 staff), organic growth complexity, new leadership hungry for systems, and a product portfolio that has diversified faster than internal process capability. The primary constraint is group ownership — Fabien Harel (MD since April 2020) is the entry point, but Aptis Group's Jean-Pierre Mamet and Oscar Mamet hold ultimate decision authority.

Key Finding — The Group Permission Problem
Resiglas is operationally an ideal EOS candidate: right size, right complexity, right leadership profile (Fabien Harel is detail-oriented, people-first, systems-minded). The barrier is not the company — it is the parent. Aptis Holdings Ltd owns 100% of Resiglas, and all board seats are held by Aptis executives. Any EOS engagement that starts with Fabien Harel will eventually require a conversation with Jean-Pierre or Oscar Mamet at the group level. The strategic play is to treat Resiglas as the proof-of-concept opportunity that opens the door to a broader Aptis group relationship — the group has at least five operating subsidiaries, any of which could benefit from the system.

Products and Services Portfolio

Resiglas operates across five distinct product universes, each with its own customer profile and margin structure. Storage is the volume backbone — fibreglass and polyethylene water tanks, septic tanks, grease traps, and water pumps serving residential and commercial construction. This was the category that established Resiglas's market dominance and remains the highest-volume segment. Poolside targets the aspirational residential and hotel market — fibreglass pools (the category Resiglas pioneered in 1991), bubble spas including a recently launched plug-and-play spa model, pool equipment, and after-sales maintenance services; this category carries the highest ticket values. Nautical serves fishing communities and recreational users — Legend boats, traditional pirogues, Resicraft vessels, kayaks, and the Tri to Fly product; a niche segment but strategically differentiated. Sustainable is the newest cluster, launched in 2022 — solar water heaters, rainwater tanks, HDPE garbage bins, dome lights, water purifiers, and bio products; aligned with Mauritius's green economy agenda and government incentive structures. Professional serves B2B industrial buyers — raw fibreglass materials, acrylic sheets, polyethylene and polystyrene solutions sold to manufacturers, contractors, and fabricators.

Geographic Presence

Resiglas operates from a single campus at Royal Road, Calebasses (Pamplemousses district, north Mauritius), which houses both manufacturing and the main showroom — recently renovated as part of Harel's restructure programme. A second contact address noted in Aptis communications is Aptis House, Inova, Riche Terre Business Park, Riche Terre — likely the group head office used for corporate correspondence. Products are distributed nationally through Resiglas's own sales force and through retail partners (Sofo Soler Moris Ltd and myhome.mu confirmed). The company has explicitly stated regional aspirations in its vision statement — "the Indian Ocean region" — though no confirmed export operations have been identified. Business hours (8:30–16:00 weekdays, 8:30–12:30 Saturday) suggest a single-site operation without shift manufacturing.

Digital Presence Note
Resiglas maintains a reasonably modern website (resiglas.mu, rebuilt in 2020 rebrand) with active Facebook (ResiglasMaurice) and Instagram (@resiglas). However, the company has an older legacy URL (resiglascoltd.com) still appearing in search results — a potential brand confusion issue. The Yelo.mu listing is unverified and shows a 1-star rating from a single review mentioning employee compensation concerns. Online reputation management is an operational gap.

Charge Register

CBRIS records four charges against Resiglas Company Limited, all registered in 2000–2002 and all predating the Aptis acquisition by 16+ years. Total notional value: MUR 24,500,000 across four instruments (MUR 6.5M floating on movable/immovable, MUR 9M floating on movable/immovable, MUR 5M fixed and floating, and MUR 4M floating on movable/immovable). No redemption or satisfaction notices appear on the CBRIS record, which means these charges technically remain outstanding on the public register. However, given their age, the zero long-term borrowings declared in the FY2024 balance sheet, and the company's 68.9% equity ratio, the most likely explanation is that these charges were not formally discharged with the Registrar when the underlying debt was repaid — a common administrative oversight in Mauritius. They do not appear to represent live financial obligations but could create friction in any future property transaction or third-party due diligence process. Aptis or BDO & Co should be encouraged to formally discharge these instruments.

Charge Register Advisory
Four CBRIS charges dated 1998–2002 totalling MUR 24.5M remain formally uncleared on the public register. The balance sheet shows zero long-term borrowings, confirming these are legacy. However, in any commercial due diligence context, undischarged charges create a negative signal. Recommend Resiglas management formally apply to have these removed from the register — a straightforward administrative action with BDO or Navitas support.

Structural Competitive Advantages

First-mover and pioneer brand equity across all three major segments. No other company in Mauritius can claim to have invented the market for water tanks (1970), swimming pools (1991), and fibreglass boats (1958) simultaneously. This heritage creates a trust advantage with consumers that takes competitors decades to replicate — and in a relationship-driven market like Mauritius, brand trust is a durable moat.

ISO 9001:2015 certification with BDO-audited financials. Resiglas is one of the few Mauritian manufacturers with a certified quality management system — a genuine differentiator in B2B and hotel/hospitality sales where procurement teams require quality certification. The BDO appointment (from October 2025) further elevates financial credibility for institutional clients.

Aptis Group financial backing and shared infrastructure. Access to group capital, shared corporate services (Navitas), group HR (Regine de Ravel), and group marketing (Karen Sarah) means Resiglas competes with a professional corporate infrastructure that independent competitors cannot match at equivalent scale. In a crisis or investment cycle, Aptis can deploy group resources to Resiglas rapidly.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Construction boom in Mauritius and IRS/RES developments — continued villa, hotel, and residential construction drives demand for water tanks, pools, and building materials. Resiglas is structurally aligned to Mauritius's property-led growth cycle.
  • Green economy and renewable energy agenda — the Sustainable product universe (solar heaters, rainwater tanks, bio products) is directly aligned with Mauritius's government policy direction. Government incentives and green building codes are structural tailwinds for this fastest-growing Resiglas segment.
  • Rising raw material and shipping costs — flagged explicitly by Resiglas management as a key challenge. Import-dependent polymer and fibreglass materials are subject to global commodity volatility and Indian Ocean shipping premiums. This compresses the CoS ratio and requires active procurement and inventory strategy.
  • Increasing competition from direct importers — the removal of import barriers, combined with e-commerce price transparency, enables consumers to source cheaper imported water tanks and pool equipment directly. Resiglas must compete on service, quality, and warranty rather than price.
  • Tourism sector resilience and hotel refurbishment cycles — Mauritius hotel sector is in active refurbishment and new-build mode. Pool replacement and spa installation in hotel properties represents a recurring B2B revenue opportunity, particularly for the Poolside universe.
  • Digital and e-commerce penetration in Mauritius — consumer purchasing decisions for home improvement products are increasingly digital-first. Resiglas's 2020 website rebuild and Instagram presence are early steps, but the online commerce opportunity is significantly underdeveloped relative to the brand's scale.
Digital Gap — Online Commerce Underdeveloped
Despite a 2020 rebrand and a functional website, Resiglas does not appear to have a transactional e-commerce capability. Products are displayed but not purchasable online. In a market where myhome.mu and sofo-soler.mu are listing and distributing Resiglas products, the company is ceding the online customer relationship (and margin) to intermediaries. A direct e-commerce channel would be both a revenue opportunity and a data capture advantage.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 8 / 10

With approximately 150–167 employees across manufacturing, sales, after-sales, nautical, finance, and sustainable product lines — plus a six-person senior management team (Harel, Pougnet, Ramsahye, Aujgobee, Chinapah, Sinnassamy, Tyack) — Resiglas sits precisely in the EOS sweet spot. The company is large enough to have genuine coordination problems between departments but small enough that a single leadership team can be aligned. Five product universes each with their own operational logic create the classic cross-functional complexity that EOS is designed to resolve.

02 — Leadership Openness 7 / 10

Fabien Harel's public statements demonstrate a leadership philosophy strongly aligned with EOS principles: "I like things to be clear and well-defined" (precision and process); "human resources as the company's most valuable asset" (people-first); hands-on field leadership; and a stated priority of "consolidating foundations before pursuing expansion" (discipline before growth). These are exactly the instincts that make a leader receptive to EOS. The constraint is the board — four of the five directors are Aptis group executives, and any EOS commitment requires their endorsement. Jean-Pierre Mamet's entrepreneurial philosophy ("grow and become leaders in your fields") is compatible but needs to be explicitly engaged.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

Resiglas is in a classic post-acquisition growth-and-integrate phase: rebranded in 2020, team restructured, new product categories launched (Sustainable 2022), IT systems being rebuilt, new management functions being created (Customer Excellence Officer, Marketing Manager, Digital Sales Assistant). Revenue has grown significantly (the MUR 148M cited in prior intelligence to MUR 253M in FY2024 — a ~71% uplift). This is the exact growth inflection point where companies outgrow informal management and need a structured operating system. Distribution costs at 21.4% of revenue and inventory days at 124 are measurable symptoms of a business that is scaling without the process infrastructure to support it.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 6 / 10
