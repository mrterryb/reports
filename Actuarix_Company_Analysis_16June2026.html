Executive Summary

A 29-year-old actuarial institution reborn as an independent Mauritius-owned firm following a 2024 management buyout — profitable, debt-free, and holding a landmark government mandate, but carrying slow debtor collection that quietly strains cash despite strong earnings.

Actuarix Consulting Limited (File No. C17939, BRN C06017939) is Mauritius's only fully independent actuarial, employee benefits, investment consulting, and insurance broking firm. Founded in 1997 as Bacon, Woodrow & Legris Ltd and subsequently operated as Aon Solutions Ltd under the global Aon network, the company was rebranded Actuarix Consulting Limited following a management buyout in which founding actuary Yvan Legris reacquired the Mauritius operations from Aon. Today Actuarix serves over 400 organisations locally and across Africa, advising on retirement, benefits, and risk solutions. It is audited by Ernst & Young and its registered office is at 1st Floor, The Pod, Vivéa Business Park, Moka.

The ownership structure is clean and concentrated. Actuarix Consulting Limited is 100% owned by Actuarix Holdings Limited (File No. C209645), a private holding company incorporated on 13 June 2024 — the vehicle through which the MBO was executed. The three individual shareholders of the holding company are Vincent Alain Yvan Legris (25%), Jeeban Samuel (26%), and Noor Ahmad Raza Hotee (49%). Noor Hotee, the majority beneficial owner and Managing Director, is a qualified actuary educated at LSE with over 25 years of industry experience. Yvan Legris, non-executive Chairman, founded the Aon Mauritius office in 1997 and has served as Global CEO of Aon Consulting. The leadership team is experienced, credentialed, and owner-managed — a strong EOS indicator.

For FY2024, Actuarix reported revenue of MUR 106.8M, a 100% gross margin (pure services business, no COGS), and a net profit after tax of MUR 4.9M (4.6% net margin). The company is entirely debt-free with MUR 33.8M cash on hand and MUR 41.4M total equity. The one material concern is debtor days of 114 days — MUR 33.4M in trade receivables sitting against MUR 106.8M revenue. In a services firm with zero inventory and no borrowings, this is the single biggest operational lever available. In May 2026, Actuarix was awarded the government mandate to serve as investment strategy consultant to the National Pensions Fund and National Savings Fund — a Rs 3.84M contract covering Rs 220 billion in combined public assets. EOS suitability is rated 6.4/10 — a firm at the right scale, with owner-managers, recently restructured, and likely experiencing the accountability and vision clarity challenges that follow any ownership transition.

Key Finding — Post-MBO Leadership Alignment is the Entry Point
Actuarix has just completed a significant corporate transformation: a global corporate parent replaced by three local owner-managers with different equity stakes (49/26/25). This structural change almost always creates hidden tension — around strategic direction, decision rights, accountability between peers, and meeting culture. The company's financial performance is sound and its market position is strong, but the real EOS conversation is about how three highly credentialed professionals with different ownership stakes make decisions, resolve disagreements, and align on a 3-year vision now that there is no parent company to defer to. That is the precise problem EOS solves.

Products and Services Portfolio

Actuarix operates across four integrated service lines, positioning itself as Mauritius's only firm that spans the full spectrum of actuarial and financial advisory for institutions:

  • Actuarial Services: Actuarial valuations for pension funds, insurance companies, and provident funds. IAS 19 accounting disclosures, curtailments, settlements, acquisitions, and divestitures. Accredited actuaries for insurance and pension schemes across Mauritius and several African jurisdictions.
  • Employee Benefits Consulting: Design, benchmarking, and management of retirement and group risk benefit schemes. Serves over 400 organisations across Mauritius and Africa. Covers pension scheme governance, trustee advisory, and benefit restructuring.
  • Investment Consulting: Long-term investment strategy, risk management, investment recommendations, manager selection, and performance monitoring for institutional investors (pension funds, insurers, provident funds). Led by Imrith Ramtohul. Mandated in May 2026 by the Ministry of Finance to advise the NPF/NSF Investment Committee on ~MUR 220 billion in combined assets.
  • Insurance Broking: Independent insurance broking services, complementing the actuarial and employee benefits offering for corporate clients.
  • Training and Education: Registered as a Training Institution under Mauritius business classifications at the same address, indicating an active learning or CPD offering.

The CBRIS business description lists two registered activities: Training Institution / Education (n.e.c.) and Other professional, scientific and technical activities — coaches, trainers, consultants, motor surveyors, opticians. The breadth of the registered categories reflects the firm's multi-line professional services scope.

Geographic Presence

Actuarix operates from a single physical office at 1st Floor, The Pod, Vivéa Business Park, Moka 81406, Mauritius — a modern business park in the geographic centre of the island. The firm's client reach, however, extends well beyond Mauritius: it partners with organisations across sub-Saharan Africa and is accredited as an actuarial consultant in multiple African regulatory frameworks, including Rwanda (listed as an accredited actuary for insurance companies and pension schemes as of March 2026). The currency capability on the Actuarix platform includes MUR, EGP, EUR, GBP, RWF, SZL, USD, ZAR, and ZMW — confirming multi-country African operations in at least Rwanda, South Africa, Eswatini, and Zambia.

The Actuarix website (www.actuarix.mu) is live and functional but appears to be in a revamp phase — the company itself notes the site is being updated. The public-facing content is limited. No team page, no detailed service descriptions, and no published case studies are currently accessible. For a firm positioning itself across Africa and seeking to grow its investment consulting mandate, the digital presence is underdeveloped relative to its institutional standing. This is a moderate commercial risk but also a clear near-term opportunity.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Independence as a differentiator. Actuarix is the only actuarial firm in Mauritius that is not affiliated with a global consulting network (Aon, Mercer, Willis Towers Watson). For clients who are sensitive to conflicts of interest — particularly pension fund trustees and insurance regulators — this independence is commercially valuable and structurally defensible. No acquisition by a global player could easily replicate this positioning without undermining it.

2. Founder-led institutional memory and relationships. Yvan Legris has been the face of actuarial consulting in Mauritius since 1997. The client relationships built over nearly three decades under the Bacon Woodrow, Aon, and Actuarix brands constitute a relationship moat that cannot be acquired or copied in the short term. The MBO preserves this continuity in a way that a trade sale to an outsider could not.

3. Scarcity of qualified actuaries. With approximately ten practising actuaries in Mauritius, Actuarix's team constitutes a disproportionate share of the country's total actuarial capacity. Entry barriers — the time and cost of actuarial qualification — are exceptionally high. This creates a durable supply-side constraint that protects the firm's pricing power.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Growing pan-African pension infrastructure: Sub-Saharan Africa is expanding formal pension coverage, creating demand for actuarial and investment consulting services across multiple markets where Actuarix already has accreditation. Rwanda, South Africa, Zambia, and Eswatini are all active based on the currency data.
  • Mauritius as African financial hub: The island's well-developed regulatory framework, IFC status, and bilateral tax treaties position it as a gateway for institutional investment flows into Africa — directly benefiting Actuarix's investment consulting practice.
  • Ageing workforce and pension reform pressure: Mauritius's demographic shift toward an older population increases the urgency and complexity of pension fund management, driving demand for actuarial valuations, IAS 19 work, and investment strategy.
  • Post-MBO growth potential: Independence from Aon's global priorities allows Actuarix to pursue mandates and pricing strategies that a global network subsidiary could not. The NPF/NSF mandate won within a year of the MBO is an early signal of this.
  • Headwind — key-person concentration risk: With ~10 actuaries in Mauritius nationally, the firm's technical capacity is critically dependent on Noor Hotee and potentially one or two other qualified practitioners. Loss of a key actuary would be disproportionately damaging.
  • Headwind — digital transformation expectations: Institutional clients increasingly expect digital reporting portals, data analytics, and real-time performance dashboards. Actuarix's current digital presence is underdeveloped and the website is in revamp. Failure to invest here creates vulnerability to better-resourced regional competitors.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 7 / 10

RocketReach and public sources indicate approximately 16 employees. This is within the EOS sweet spot of 10–250 people. The firm operates four distinct service lines (actuarial, employee benefits, investment consulting, insurance broking) plus a training function — meaningful complexity for the headcount. The recent addition of the NPF/NSF mandate will add reporting obligations, client communication cadence, and coordination demands that make structured operating rhythms increasingly necessary. Score 7 rather than 8–9 because at 16 people, the firm is relatively small and the complexity may not yet feel acute to leadership.

02 — Leadership Openness 7 / 10

The MBO itself is a strong signal of entrepreneurial mindset and willingness to take ownership — literally. Owner-managers who have put their own capital and careers into a business are typically more open to frameworks that improve accountability and performance than professional managers operating under corporate oversight. Yvan Legris's background as Global CEO of Aon Consulting suggests comfort with structured management frameworks. Noor Hotee's public profile demonstrates a reflective, professionally credentialed leader. The risk is that high professional qualifications can create resistance to frameworks perceived as "too simple" for experts — this is a known pattern in professional services EOS engagements and must be addressed in the first conversation.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

The company is in a textbook EOS-addressable moment: a major ownership transition (MBO completed ~18 months ago), a new government mandate that will increase operational demands, a DSO problem that requires cross-functional accountability to fix, and three owner-managers with different equity stakes who need to align on a common 3-year vision. The classic EOS pain points are all present: "We don't have the right people in the right seats" (post-MBO role clarity), "We're not all on the same page" (three owners, different priorities), "We're not getting traction" (114-day DSO, thin margins). This is a high-probability EOS engagement trigger.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

The company runs its CRM and client portal on Odoo (confirmed from actuarix.mu/website/info — an Odoo-powered platform), which suggests some operational infrastructure. Ernst & Young-audited financials imply disciplined financial reporting. However, the 114-day DSO suggests the billing and collections process is either not systematised or not being enforced. The website revamp and what appears to be a relatively lean team managing a large client base suggest processes are individual-driven rather than system-driven. Post-MBO, the inherited Aon governance structures have likely been partially dismantled without replacement EOS-equivalent rhythms yet in place.

05 — Company Culture 6 / 10

The stability signals are positive: a 29-year tenure, the same founding actuary still active, a team that chose to execute an MBO rather than accept external acquisition. This speaks to loyalty and shared purpose. However, the culture is likely shaped by a professional partnership model — high individual expertise, peer accountability norms, limited hierarchical direction. For EOS purposes, this means core values are probably lived but never documented, accountability is professional rather than structural, and "difficult conversations" are likely avoided in favour of collegial deference. This is common in professional services and is exactly what the Accountability Chart and Rocks address.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 5 / 10

Three owner-managers with stakes of 49%, 26%, and 25% — no single majority controller, no documented decision-making protocol visible from public data. Under Aon, decisions above a threshold were escalated to the regional/global parent; that backstop no longer exists. The distribution of equity (designed to prevent any single individual from having unilateral control) is deliberately balanced, which can default to either consensus-paralysis or informal dominance by the most assertive personality. Neither is optimal. An Accountability Chart with clearly defined roles and a GWC (Get it, Want it, Capacity to do it) assessment per seat would directly address this.

07 — Vision Clarity 5 / 10

No V/TO or publicly articulated 3-year or 10-year vision is available from any public source. The company's stated identity — "since inception in 1997, dedicated to providing exceptional financial and risk management solutions" — is continuity language, not directional strategy. The MBO creates a natural inflection point: the new owners now need to answer "What does Actuarix look like in 2030?" for the first time without a parent company's strategic plan to anchor them. Is the goal to remain a Mauritius-focused boutique? Expand across East Africa? Grow the investment consulting practice? Build a training academy? These are real open questions that a V/TO session would clarify.

08 — Meeting Culture 5 / 10

No evidence of a structured meeting rhythm is available from public data. In a firm of 16 people with three owner-managers at the top, meeting culture is almost certainly informal and driven by individual work patterns rather than a formal weekly L10 cadence. The Aon corporate parent likely imposed its own meeting structure; post-MBO, that structure has been removed without documented replacement. The risk is a drift toward reactive management — "meetings when there's a problem" rather than "meetings on a fixed schedule to stay ahead of problems." The DSO issue is a concrete example: a weekly metrics review with a receivables line item would have flagged this much earlier.

EOS Suitability

Overall Rating: Moderate-High — Qualified Positive

Actuarix scores well on size fit, growth-stage urgency, and leadership calibre — but the gaps in vision clarity, decision-making structure, and meeting culture keep the score from reaching the highest tier. The most compelling EOS entry point is the post-MBO alignment challenge: three highly capable owner-managers who need to transition from "we just survived the buyout" to "we have a shared 3-year plan and a weekly accountability rhythm." The 114-day DSO is the clearest, most quantifiable symptom of missing EOS infrastructure. A Focus Day framed around the question "What does Actuarix look like in 2028, and who owns what to get there?" would be the natural entry point for this firm.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Actuarix

Vision / Traction Organizer (V/TO)

The MBO created a leadership team without a shared documented vision for the first time in the company's history. A V/TO session to define the 10-year target, 3-year picture, 1-year plan, and quarterly Rocks is the foundational requirement. Without it, the three owners are making daily decisions against different unstated assumptions about where the firm is headed.

Accountability Chart

Three owner-managers with different equity stakes and (likely) overlapping functional responsibilities. The Accountability Chart clarifies who sits in the Visionary seat, who sits in the Integrator seat, and who owns each major function. In a 16-person professional services firm, role ambiguity at the top creates friction all the way down.

Scorecard & Measurables

The 114-day DSO would have been visible on a weekly Scorecard months before it appeared on the balance sheet. A 5–15 metric Scorecard with DSO, revenue pipeline, billable hours, and client retention as weekly line items gives the leadership team early-warning data instead of annual-filing surprises.

Rocks (Quarterly Priorities)

Post-MBO, Actuarix has multiple competing priorities: website revamp, NPF/NSF mandate delivery, DSO reduction, African expansion, and internal role clarity. Without Rocks, all of these compete for attention simultaneously and none get resolved. Setting 3–7 company Rocks per quarter forces explicit prioritisation and creates accountability for delivery.

Level 10 Meeting

A fixed weekly L10 for the leadership team replaces the informal, reactive meeting culture that almost certainly developed post-MBO. For a firm managing a MUR 220B government mandate, a 90-minute weekly rhythm with a standing agenda — Scorecard review, Rock updates, Headlines, To-Do list — is not optional; it is a risk management tool.

Issues List & IDS

The DSO problem, the website revamp, the tax rate anomaly, and the post-MBO role ambiguity are all Issues that need Identify-Discuss-Solve treatment rather than indefinite deferral. Implementing IDS discipline — with a visible Issues list owned collectively by the leadership team — would unlock resolution of several problems that currently drift without a clear owner or deadline.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations in total — four financial/operational and four EOS-specific. Sequenced in order of impact: fix the cash cycle first, align leadership second, then build the growth infrastructure. No EOS work should commence before the DSO issue is addressed, as it provides the most tangible proof-of-concept for accountability frameworks.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Implement a Formal Receivables Collection Protocol — Target DSO of 60 Days Within 12 Months

Assign one named owner (likely Jeeban Samuel or a designated finance manager) accountable for the weekly receivables report. Introduce 30/60/90-day billing terms in all new and renewed client contracts. Implement automated invoice reminders at day 30, 45, and 60. Escalate outstanding invoices above 75 days to a director. Target: reduce DSO from 114 to 60 days within 12 months. Cash impact: MUR 16.2M released — equivalent to 3.3× annual net profit. This single action transforms the working capital position and directly improves the liquidity available for owner distributions or reinvestment.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Commission a Tax Position Review — Resolve the 32.4% Effective Tax Rate

The effective tax rate of 32.4% on FY2024 PBT — more than double the standard 15% Mauritius corporate rate — is costing approximately MUR 1.2M per year in excess tax versus the statutory rate. Commission Ernst & Young to prepare a formal memo explaining the components of the FY2024 tax charge: deferred tax movements, non-deductible MBO transaction costs, prior-year adjustments, and any permanent differences. If the rate is correctable (e.g., MBO-related one-offs normalising), no further action is needed. If structural, a tax optimisation strategy should be implemented. Success metric: confirmed explanation of the rate within 30 days of engagement.

Immediate Priority — 30 Days

The actuarix.mu website is acknowledged to be in revamp. Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

Actuarix is a rare company: 29 years old, debt-free, profitable, holding a government mandate for MUR 220 billion in public assets, and recently reborn as an independently owned firm under a credentialed and committed leadership team. The commercial opportunity is real. The risks are internal rather than market-facing — a DSO that is draining cash, a tax charge that is suppressing profit, and three owner-managers who have completed the hardest part (the MBO) but not yet done the next hardest part: agreeing on where they are going and who owns what to get there. EOS addresses exactly that problem. The timing of the approach — approximately 18 months post-MBO — is optimal: the excitement of the buyout has settled, the operational reality has arrived, and the need for structured accountability is becoming felt.
