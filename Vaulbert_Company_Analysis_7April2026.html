Executive Summary

A 70-year-old Mauritian FMCG distribution house, recently rebranded and under its third-generation owner-director, delivering strong top-line revenue but carrying structurally heavy short-term debt that limits strategic freedom.

H. Vaulbert de Chantilly Ltd — trading as HVC Ltd and rebranded as Vaulbert in 2024 — is a privately held Mauritian FMCG distribution and light manufacturing company incorporated on 4 December 2006 (File No. C067249), with commercial roots tracing back to JVC Ltd, founded by Jean Vaulbert de Chantilly in 1952. Operating from Bonair Industrial Zone in Triolet, Pamplemousses, the company distributes Mauritian and international branded goods — spanning food, beverages, alcohol, tobacco, cosmetics, and household products — to both modern retail and the 4- and 5-star hotel (HoReCa) segments across Mauritius. It also operates a manufacturing and bottling facility invested in from 2017, producing soft drinks, distilled potable alcoholic beverages, and its own RTD brand Baie (launched December 2025).

The company is 100% owned by Vauchan Investment Ltd, a holding vehicle that consolidates the Vaulbert family's stake. Henri Dugald Vaulbert de Chantilly is the sole registered director and has led the company since assuming ownership in 2008. There are no registered charges on the company's assets, and annual registration fees are paid up to 2026 — a positive compliance signal. The company secretary is CAS Services Ltd (UHY House, Vivea Business Park, Moka), appointed in August 2024.

Financially, FY2022 (the latest filed accounts) shows turnover of MUR 208.6M, gross profit of MUR 51.7M (24.8% margin), and net profit after tax of MUR 16.9M (8.1% margin) — solid performance for a distribution business. However, the balance sheet reveals a concerning dependency on short-term borrowings of MUR 88.5M, representing 63.8% of total equity, and a current ratio of just 1.15x. The company's debt structure raises working capital risk, particularly given inventory-heavy operations. EOS suitability is rated 6.8 / 10 — a genuine opportunity, with an owner-founder who has driven transformation but is likely running the business without a structured execution system.

Key Finding — Owner-Led Transition at Inflection Point
Vaulbert is at a textbook EOS entry point: a founder-led, third-generation family business that has grown through personal drive and market intuition, is actively expanding its brand portfolio and launching its own manufactured products, and is now at a scale (MUR 200M+ revenue) where informal management creates structural risk. The 2024 rebrand from HVC to Vaulbert signals a deliberate identity shift — Henri is building something, not maintaining something. The critical leverage point for Thierry is the combination of growth ambition, brand complexity (8+ international brands plus own-label), and a balance sheet under pressure from short-term debt. Henri needs to delegate, systematise, and build accountability structures. That is exactly what EOS provides.

There is no evidence of operations in Rodrigues or overseas export markets, though import sourcing spans 10+ countries. The company's stated focus remains the Mauritian domestic market.

Charge Register

The CBRIS charge register shows no registered charges against H. Vaulbert de Chantilly Ltd as at 7 April 2026. This is a positive governance signal — despite MUR 88.5M in short-term borrowings, no fixed or floating charges over company assets appear to have been formally registered. In Mauritius, bank overdrafts and revolving credit facilities are sometimes secured by personal guarantee of the director rather than a registered charge over company assets, which would explain this position. Alternatively, the security may be held by way of a pledge on inventories or receivables that does not require CBRIS registration. This warrants clarification in due diligence.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Exclusive brand distribution rights. Vaulbert holds exclusive or preferred distributor status for multiple international brands (Perrier, Musgrave Spirits, Spier, Loacker, Alpro, Glenryck). In a small island market with a population of approximately 1.3M, exclusivity is the highest-value form of competitive protection. These relationships, built over decades, are not easily replicable by a new entrant.

2. Dual-channel expertise. The ability to serve both modern retail (supermarket chains, convenience stores) and premium HoReCa (4- and 5-star hotels) simultaneously requires different sales approaches, cold chain logistics, and credit management — capabilities Vaulbert has developed over 70+ years of commercial presence. This dual-channel positioning protects against channel concentration risk and gives brand principals broader reach than single-channel distributors.

3. Integrated manufacturing capability. The 2017 investment in a bottling and manufacturing plant gives Vaulbert the ability to produce own-label products (Cyclone Rum, Baie RTD, soft drinks) with margins significantly above pure distribution. This vertical integration, combined with bonded warehouse licensing, creates a capability stack that smaller distributors cannot replicate without capital investment.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Premium and wellness beverages are growing in Mauritius — Vaulbert's Dec 2025 launch of Peroni, Prime (energy drink), and Baie RTD directly targets this trend. Consumer premiumisation in a high-GDP-per-capita island economy favours Vaulbert's portfolio positioning.
  • Tourism recovery driving HoReCa demand — Mauritius tourism arrivals rebounded strongly post-COVID. 4- and 5-star hotel F&B spending is a structural growth driver for premium alcohol and beverage distributors like Vaulbert.
  • RTD (Ready-to-Drink) alcoholic beverages is the fastest-growing global spirits subcategory — Vaulbert's own-label Baie RTD positions it at the front of this wave in the local market.
  • Plant-based food distribution growing — Alpro (Danone) in the portfolio captures the health-conscious consumer trend gaining traction in Mauritius's expatriate and wellness-oriented consumer segments.
  • Consolidation risk in Mauritian distribution — Larger conglomerates (IBL, Rogers) continue to acquire distribution assets. Vaulbert's independence is an asset for brand principals who want a dedicated, focused partner — but it also makes the company a potential acquisition target if Henri considers an exit or strategic partnership.
  • Inflation and input cost pressure — Import-dependent distributors face margin compression from MUR/USD exchange rate volatility and global logistics costs. Vaulbert's 24.8% gross margin provides some buffer, but own-label raw material costs are a new exposure introduced by the manufacturing operations.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 7 / 10

With MUR 208M+ revenue, nine registered business activities (distribution, import/export, manufacturing, bonded warehouse, retail, wholesale alcohol/tobacco), and an identified management team of at least 4 named individuals (Henri, Nicolas Commins, Anaelle Delaitre, Jessinee Rajaratnam), Vaulbert is well inside the EOS sweet spot of 10–250 people. The manufacturing + distribution combination creates multi-functional complexity that informal management cannot hold together at scale. The 2024 rebrand signals a deliberate growth ambition — complexity is increasing, not stable.

02 — Leadership Openness 6 / 10

Henri is a third-generation entrepreneur who voluntarily took over and transformed a legacy business — this signals entrepreneurial mindset and openness to change. The 2024 rebrand from HVC to Vaulbert shows willingness to break with heritage when needed. However, as the sole director with 100% family ownership through a holding company, there are no external accountability mechanisms — board, investors, or partners — to drive governance discipline. Henri may be highly confident in his own judgement and potentially resistant to systems that challenge his operational control. The leadership openness score sits at mid — positive signals offset by the concentrated control structure.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

This is the highest-scoring dimension. Vaulbert is at a classic growth inflection: revenue above MUR 200M, manufacturing added to a distribution base, new brand launches accelerating (three in December 2025 alone), a full rebrand executed, and a new company secretary appointed — all within the last two years. These are textbook symptoms of a business that has outgrown its informal management model. The financial pain is real: MUR 88.5M short-term debt, 104-day DSO, and thin liquidity are direct consequences of growth without systems. This is exactly where EOS creates the most value.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

Financial reporting is filed approximately 18 months after fiscal year end (FY2022 approved June 2023, filed mid-2023). Distribution costs are reported as nil — suggesting cost allocation systems are not granular enough to separately track logistics from administration. DSO of 104 days suggests accounts receivable management is informal or under-monitored. The bonded warehouse, manufacturing, and distribution operations running from a single site suggests process discipline exists at the operational level — but financial and management reporting systems appear immature relative to the business's scale.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

Vaulbert's published values — ownership, excellence, partnership, trust — are well-articulated and consistent with a family business that has survived generational transitions. The company describes its team as "a majority of women" with an average age under 40, signalling a relatively young, progressive workforce. Henri's quoted ambition to "raise innovation and quality standards" suggests a culture that aspires to more than maintenance. The 70-year heritage provides cultural continuity that EOS can build on — there are deep roots, not a blank slate. This is a facilitator-friendly cultural environment.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 5 / 10

With one director and 100% family ownership, all material decisions flow through Henri. The brand launches, rebrand, manufacturing investment, and new company secretary appointment all reflect solo executive decision-making. This creates speed in good times but is a bottleneck risk as the business complexity grows. The EOS Accountability Chart and Rocks process directly addresses this — creating clarity about who owns what decision and establishing a decision-making rhythm that does not require Henri's involvement in every operational call. The mid score reflects high centralisation but also the positive signal that Henri is clearly decisive and action-oriented.

07 — Vision Clarity 7 / 10
