Executive Summary

Racso Company Limited is a 15-year-old Mauritian wholesale fuel, general merchandise, and road freight business that generates MUR 104.7M in revenue but sits on a retained earnings deficit — a group that is operationally growing while its books carry legacy losses that need addressing before any strategic engagement.

Racso Company Limited (File No. C102087, BRN C11102087) was incorporated on 15 April 2011 and is headquartered at 2nd Floor, Aptis House, Inova Riche Terre Business Park, Riche Terre, Mauritius. The company operates across three registered business activities: wholesale of solid, liquid, and gaseous fuels and related products; freight transport by road (Taxi Lorry, Carrier A licence); and non-specialised wholesale trade including general merchandise and seafood distribution. This positions Racso as a diversified B2B supply and logistics business operating in the north of the island with access to port and industrial corridor infrastructure at Riche Terre.

Ownership is overwhelmingly concentrated in Aptis Holdings Ltd, which holds 140,725 of 141,000 ordinary shares (99.8%), with the remaining 275 shares held by director Jean-Alain Mamet personally. Aptis Holdings Ltd (C162483) is itself a domestic private holding company incorporated on 21 February 2019, owned by CHT Holdings Co Ltd (77.8%), Jean-Pierre Oscar Mamet (11.1%), and Anne Regine de Ravel de l'Argentiere (11.1%). Aptis Holdings carries total assets of MUR 855.6M, investments in subsidiaries of MUR 489.5M, and retained earnings of MUR 620.4M — it is a financially substantial parent. Racso is one operating arm of this wider group. Racso in turn wholly owns two subsidiaries: Racso Distribution Co Ltd (C231420, incorporated February 2026 — brand new) and Racso Logistics Co Ltd (C196301, incorporated April 2023).

At the Racso Company Limited level, FY2024 revenue came in at MUR 104.7M with a gross profit of MUR 33.7M (32.2% margin) — a genuinely strong gross margin for a fuel and distribution business. However, administration costs of MUR 29.8M consumed nearly all of that gross profit, leaving a net profit of only MUR 2.7M (2.6% net margin). The balance sheet shows retained earnings of negative MUR 5.2M, indicating the company has not yet recovered from prior-year losses. This tension between a commercially active, growing operation and a structurally thin bottom line is the defining financial narrative. The EOS suitability score is 6.5/10 — the group structure presents both an opportunity (a motivated, growth-oriented ownership group) and a complexity (decision-making may not sit cleanly with Racso's own leadership).

Key Finding — Subsidiary of a MUR 855M Group With a Fresh Expansion Play Underway
Racso Company Limited is not a standalone SME — it is the primary trading arm of the Aptis Holdings group, which carries MUR 855M in total assets and MUR 489M in subsidiary investments. The incorporation of Racso Distribution Co Ltd in February 2026 (just two months ago) and Racso Logistics Co Ltd in April 2023 signals a deliberate group strategy to separate and scale its fuel distribution and logistics functions into distinct legal entities. This is a group mid-transformation: creating structure, not just running a business. That transformation is precisely the moment EOS is most valuable — but Thierry must approach this at the Aptis Holdings level, not just Racso. The real conversation is with the Mamet family (Jean-Pierre, Jean-Alain, Oscar) and their co-director Anne Regine de Ravel de l'Argentiere, who are actively structuring the group for growth.

Products and Services Portfolio

Racso Company Limited operates across three distinct business lines registered with the Mauritius Registrar of Companies:

  • Wholesale Fuel Distribution: Wholesale of solid, liquid, and gaseous fuels and related products. This is likely the highest-revenue segment given the scale and the group's strategic decision to create a dedicated subsidiary (Racso Distribution) specifically for gas and fuel wholesaling in 2026.
  • Road Freight Transport: Freight transport by road under Carrier A (Taxi Lorry) licence. This is the logistics arm, now being spun off into Racso Logistics Co Ltd. Racso Logistics generated MUR 54.9M in revenue in FY2024 as an independent entity, suggesting significant standalone scale.
  • General Merchandise & Seafood Wholesale: Non-specialised wholesale trade covering general merchandise and seafood distribution. This third vertical adds diversification and may serve hospitality, retail, or institutional buyers across Mauritius.

The group's combined revenue (Racso Company + Racso Logistics) exceeds MUR 159M in FY2024, making this a substantial trading group by Mauritian SME standards. The deliberate separation into specialist entities (distribution, logistics, parent trading company) indicates ambition to scale each vertical independently with dedicated management and cost structures.

Geographic Presence

The fuel and general merchandise distribution footprint likely covers the full island given the company's scale.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Integrated Supply Chain Ownership: The group's deliberate structure — a holding company (Aptis), a trading entity (Racso Company), a logistics arm (Racso Logistics), and a specialist distribution entity (Racso Distribution) — gives it a vertically integrated supply chain that competitors without their own logistics capabilities cannot replicate easily. This integration allows Racso to offer delivered pricing and logistics services as a bundle, which is a genuine differentiator in the Mauritius B2B market.

2. Riche Terre Location: Operating from the Inova Riche Terre Business Park positions the group immediately adjacent to Port Louis Free Port and the main north-island commercial corridor. For fuel, seafood, and general merchandise distribution, this location provides first-mover access to imported goods at port and efficient distribution northward to Grand Baie and the tourism corridor — a structural advantage not easily replicated.

3. Parent Group Capitalisation: The MUR 855M Aptis Holdings balance sheet provides a financial backstop that most independent Mauritius SMEs cannot match. This enables Racso to offer credit terms, bid on larger contracts, and absorb operational volatility without the existential risk faced by under-capitalised competitors.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Energy transition pressure: Mauritius has committed to 60% renewable energy by 2030. Fuel distribution businesses face a structural long-term headwind as LPG and liquid fuel volumes may decline. The 2026 creation of Racso Distribution (specifically for gas wholesale) may be a response — pivoting toward gas as a cleaner transition fuel.
  • Tourism sector recovery and growth: Mauritius tourism arrivals are recovering strongly post-COVID. Hotel and hospitality operators represent a key buyer segment for general merchandise and seafood distribution, creating tailwinds for that segment.
  • Logistics sector formalisation: The Mauritius government has signalled infrastructure investment in Port Louis and Riche Terre. For a logistics operator in Riche Terre, this is a direct tailwind.
  • B2B consolidation: Smaller independent distributors in Mauritius are struggling post-COVID. Larger, better-capitalised operators like Racso are well-positioned to absorb market share or acquire distressed competitors.
  • Cost of doing business pressure: Rising fuel costs, wage inflation, and utility costs are squeezing margins across all sectors. Racso's admin cost bloat (28.5% of revenue) leaves it particularly exposed to this trend if not addressed.
  • Digital procurement adoption: Large institutional buyers (hotels, supermarkets) are increasingly using digital procurement platforms. Racso's complete absence of digital presence is a vulnerability if buyer procurement migrates online.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis. Racso is assessed as the primary engagement target, with awareness that Aptis Holdings is the strategic decision-maker.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 7 / 10

Racso Company Limited now controls two subsidiaries (Racso Logistics, Racso Distribution), operates three distinct business lines, and sits within a multi-tier corporate structure under Aptis Holdings. Combined revenue across the group exceeds MUR 159M. The group is large enough to have real organisational complexity — multiple entities, shared services, intercompany transactions — but likely still small enough (estimated 30–80 employees across the group) that EOS can be implemented without enterprise-level customisation. The recent addition of a new subsidiary in February 2026 indicates active growth, which increases organisational strain and EOS relevance.

02 — Leadership Openness 6 / 10

The directorship structure shows a family-led business (Mamet family holds key operational and holding roles) with a long-standing partner (Dev Anand Sabbi) and one external investor (Anne Regine de Ravel de l'Argentiere at Aptis level). Family businesses can be either highly open to EOS (if the founder/patriarch is the champion) or resistant (if family dynamics override accountability structures). The appointment of BDO as auditor in late 2025 and Navitas Corporate Services as company secretary in 2024 suggests the group is investing in governance and is not averse to external professional input — a positive signal. The deliberate structural redesign of the group (creating subsidiaries, separating functions) also indicates strategic thinking rather than ad hoc management, which correlates with EOS readiness.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

This is the strongest EOS signal in the entire profile. The group is visibly at the inflection point where organic growth has forced a structural response: the Mamet family has created two new subsidiaries in three years (Racso Logistics in 2023, Racso Distribution in 2026) to manage complexity. This is a classic "we're creating new companies to solve management problems" pattern — which EOS is explicitly designed to address without the cost and complexity of legal fragmentation. The retained earnings deficit at Racso Company level, combined with strong gross margins, indicates that cost management and accountability are pain points the leadership has not yet solved operationally. Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Administration Cost Diagnostic — Identify and Eliminate the Hidden MUR 15M Overhead

Administration costs of MUR 29.8M (28.5% of revenue) are consuming 88.5% of gross profit. The industry benchmark for a distribution business is 10–15% of revenue — meaning MUR 14–20M in excess admin costs exists and is unaccounted for in the public filing. The root causes are likely: management fee charges from Aptis Holdings, duplicated overhead across the three Racso entities, and unallocated group-level costs. The leadership team should commission a cost-by-line-item analysis (this is an internal exercise BDO could support) with a target to reduce admin to 18% of revenue within 12 months. That single action would add approximately MUR 11M to net profit — a 4× increase.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Receivables Acceleration — Reduce DSO from 50 to 35 Days to Release MUR 4M Cash

Trade receivables of MUR 14.4M against revenue of MUR 104.7M implies 50 days to collect. Reducing this to 35 days (still generous for B2B) would release approximately MUR 4.3M in working capital. This requires: (1) a named Collections Owner in the Accountability Chart; (2) weekly receivables ageing review in the Scorecard; (3) proactive credit terms negotiation with top 10 customers. For a business carrying a retained earnings deficit of MUR 5.2M, injecting MUR 4M in free cash without borrowing or equity injection is a material strategic win.

Recommendation 03

Racso Logistics generated MUR 54.9M in revenue in its second year of operation (FY2024) but only a 6.4% gross margin, compared to Racso Company's 32.2%. Before creating Racso Distribution, the board should ensure the intercompany cost and revenue flows between Racso Company and Racso Logistics are correctly structured — otherwise the Distribution entity will compound the same structural ambiguity.
