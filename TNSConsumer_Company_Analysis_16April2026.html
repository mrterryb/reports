Executive Summary

A four-year-old FMCG distributor wholly owned by Aptis Holdings Ltd, generating MUR 49M in revenue but burning through capital at a rate that has produced a cumulative deficit of MUR 30.7M — a company still in its investment and market-building phase with no path to profitability yet visible in filed accounts.

TNS Consumer Goods Company Limited was incorporated on 11 August 2021 and operates from the 2nd Floor of Hermes House, Inova Business Park, Riche Terre, Mauritius — the same Aptis Group hub. The company is the FMCG distribution arm of Aptis Holdings Ltd, established to commercialise a wide range of fast-moving consumer goods in Mauritius. Its business model centres on partnering with international brands and multinationals to supply the Mauritian retail market with quality products at competitive prices. Product lines include Danone dairy, Clover dairy products, energy drinks, snacks, personal care items, and confectionery — distributed through wholesale, retail foodstuff, import/export, and warehousing channels.

Ownership is entirely concentrated: APTIS Holdings Ltd holds all 500,000 ordinary shares (MUR 50,000,000 stated capital). The three directors — Jean-Pierre Mamet (CEO of Aptis Group), Oscar Mamet (son), and Sabbi Appanna Dev Anand (Group CFO) — are all Aptis Group principals, and Navitas Corporate Services Ltd acts as company secretary. TNS Consumer is therefore a fully integrated subsidiary of the Aptis Group, not an independently governed entity.

Financially, FY2023 reveals a business under severe stress: revenue of MUR 49.0M is overwhelmed by administration costs of MUR 32.4M (66% of revenue), producing a net loss of MUR 20.4M. Retained earnings stand at negative MUR 30.7M, meaning accumulated losses exceed the MUR 20M contributed by parent Aptis beyond stated capital. The balance sheet is technically insolvent on an equity basis (total equity is negative MUR 9.3M). EOS suitability scores 5.1/10 — moderate potential constrained by the group-controlled governance model.

Key Finding — Negative Equity and Mounting Losses
TNS Consumer is in negative equity (MUR -9.3M) with accumulated losses of MUR 30.7M against a stated capital of MUR 50M. Administration costs of MUR 32.4M — representing 66% of revenue — are the dominant structural problem. This cost-to-revenue ratio is consistent with a startup still building brand portfolio, distribution infrastructure and headcount, but is unsustainable without parent capital injections. The company cannot service itself from trading cash flows in FY2023. Key strategic question: is the Aptis Group running TNS Consumer as a loss-leader investment in FMCG market share, or has the business model failed to scale as projected?

At a Glance

Commercial Strengths

Aptis Group infrastructure and capital backing provides operational continuity despite trading losses. Strong brand portfolio (Danone, Clover) gives credibility with retailers. Multi-channel model (wholesale, retail, import/export, warehousing) creates diversified revenue streams. Incumbent position in a growing Mauritian FMCG market.

Financial Vulnerabilities

Administration costs consuming 66% of revenue are the existential threat. Negative retained earnings of MUR -30.7M represent cumulative capital destruction. Near-zero current assets (MUR 17.6M) with no cash disclosed separately. Entire equity is negative — the company survives on parent group support, not own economic strength.

EOS Readiness Signal

Startup-phase chaos likely present — a company less than 3 years old losing money at scale exhibits classic EOS symptoms: unclear accountability, reactive decision-making, no documented operating rhythms. However, Aptis Group governance overlay reduces the independence needed for an authentic EOS engagement. Engagement through the group, not TNS alone, is the entry point.

Structural Competitive Advantages

Aptis Group infrastructure: TNS Consumer benefits from the group's existing logistics network, Riche Terre warehousing, management expertise accumulated through TNS Tobacco and Aptis Distribution, and financial backing that allows it to absorb startup losses while building brand relationships. Independent distributors cannot match this level of parent support.

Danone and Clover exclusivities (if held): Distribution rights for Danone and Clover in Mauritius — if held exclusively — represent genuine competitive moats. Danone is one of the world's largest food companies, and Clover is a major African dairy brand. Exclusive country distribution agreements are the most defensible position in FMCG.

Complementary group positioning: TNS Consumer and TNS Tobacco (the group's tobacco distribution arm with MUR 6.8 billion turnover) share management, logistics and commercial infrastructure. This cross-subsidiary efficiency is unavailable to standalone FMCG distributors.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Premiumisation trend in Mauritius FMCG — rising middle class seeking branded international products over unbranded alternatives creates tailwind for TNS Consumer's model.
  • Retail consolidation — growth of supermarket chains (Winner's, Intermart, Jumbo) creates larger, more efficient distribution targets for a well-organised FMCG distributor.
  • Consumer purchasing power pressure — MUR inflation and cost-of-living squeeze may constrain discretionary FMCG spending and compress distribution margins in the short term.
  • Health and wellness category growth — Danone's nutritional products, personal care, and health items align with growing Mauritian consumer health consciousness.
  • Competition intensifying — established players (Innodis, IBL, Rogers Food) are not conceding share easily; new entrant challenges are significant in a small island market.
  • Digital commerce emergence — online grocery delivery in Mauritius remains nascent but growing; TNS Consumer's digital presence (website, social media) positions it to participate in this channel shift.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 5 / 10

Aptis website lists approximately 6 employees at TNS Consumer — below the EOS sweet spot of 10–250. However, the company is growing rapidly (incorporated 2021, already at MUR 49M revenue) and almost certainly shares operational staff with the broader Aptis Group. The effective organisational complexity — 4 directors, multiple business licences, multi-brand distribution, import/export, warehousing — is greater than the headcount suggests. Scored mid-range: too small as a standalone entity, but the Aptis Group context creates genuine complexity that EOS could address.

02 — Leadership Openness 5 / 10

Jean-Pierre Mamet is a former BAT executive turned entrepreneur — a profile that often correlates with strong personal leadership but variable openness to external operating frameworks. The Aptis Group's active diversification and acquisition strategy shows leadership ambition and willingness to test new models. However, TNS Consumer's governance is entirely interlocked with Aptis Holdings — all directors are group principals. True leadership openness for EOS purposes would require the Aptis Group leadership team to commit, not just TNS Consumer in isolation. The EOS conversation must be pitched at group level.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 7 / 10

A company generating MUR 49M revenue in only its second full year of operations, losing MUR 20M, with 4 directors all wearing multiple group hats — this is a textbook EOS growth-pain situation. The classic symptoms are almost certainly present: unclear individual accountability (who owns P&L at TNS Consumer vs. the group?), absence of a dedicated leadership team with single-entity focus, reactive decision-making driven by short-term cash pressure, and no documented scaling plan visible from public data. The financial crisis creates urgency that EOS conversations can capitalise on.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 4 / 10

No evidence of documented operating processes, formal meeting cadences, or structured scorecards at TNS Consumer. As an Aptis Group subsidiary launched in 2021, it likely inherits some group-level financial reporting processes (BDO audit, annual returns filed promptly), but at the operational level — brand management, distribution execution, supplier relationships — the systems are almost certainly embryonic. The administration cost crisis (MUR 32.4M overhead) could itself be a symptom of process inefficiency: duplication, poor procurement, uncontrolled headcount.

05 — Company Culture 5 / 10

Culture signals are limited given the company's youth and group integration. The Aptis Group presents an entrepreneurial, ambitious external identity (active website, regular press releases, acquisition strategy). Within TNS Consumer, the small team operating under intense financial pressure could develop either a high-accountability performance culture or a reactive firefighting one — public data cannot distinguish between these. BDO audit engagement and prompt annual return filing suggest basic governance discipline is present.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 3 / 10

All four TNS Consumer directors are simultaneously directors or senior executives of multiple Aptis Group entities. Decision-making authority is therefore almost certainly concentrated at the Aptis Holdings level, with TNS Consumer having limited autonomous decision-making capacity. This is the most significant EOS constraint: without a dedicated leadership team with genuine P&L ownership, EOS tools like the Accountability Chart and Rocks cannot function as designed. The group structure fundamentally muddles the decision rights that EOS requires clarity on.

07 — Vision Clarity 5 / 10

At the Aptis Group level, there is clear vision: diversified group across distribution, commerce, property, and services; ambitious acquisition strategy; African expansion ambition via Navitas. At the TNS Consumer level, the vision is less defined — "quality FMCG at affordable prices for Mauritians" is a positioning statement, not a 10-year target. What revenue, margin, and brand portfolio does TNS Consumer aim to achieve by 2030? The answer, if it exists, is not visible from public data. V/TO work at the group level, with TNS Consumer embedded within it, would be the appropriate EOS approach.

08 — Meeting Culture 4 / 10

No evidence of structured meeting rhythms, weekly Level 10 Meetings, or quarterly planning cadences at either TNS Consumer or the Aptis Group level is publicly visible. The multi-entity structure — where the same 3-4 people sit on boards of 4+ companies simultaneously — makes disciplined meeting cadences structurally difficult. Crisis-driven reactive meetings are the almost certain current mode. This is a significant EOS implementation opportunity but requires group-level commitment.

EOS Suitability

Overall Rating: Moderate Potential — Group-Level Entry Required

TNS Consumer as a standalone entity is too small and too deeply embedded in Aptis Group governance to be a viable standalone EOS client. The financial crisis (MUR 20.4M net loss, negative equity trajectory) creates real urgency and EOS-addressable symptoms, but the governance structure means any EOS engagement must begin at the Aptis Holdings / Jean-Pierre Mamet level. If the Aptis Group as a whole adopted EOS — with V/TO at group level and subsidiary Rocks and Scorecard cascaded to TNS Consumer, L'Artigiano, Navitas, and Harley Street — the score would rise significantly. The entry point is the group CEO conversation, not TNS Consumer in isolation.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for TNS Consumer

Accountability Chart

With 4 directors each wearing 3+ Aptis Group hats simultaneously, clarity on who owns what at TNS Consumer is absent. An Accountability Chart would force the conversation: does TNS Consumer have its own Integrator / GM? Who owns Sales, Operations, Finance day-to-day? Without this, the company cannot scale beyond its current chaos.

Scorecard

MUR 49M revenue with MUR 20.4M net loss demands a weekly numbers-driven management dashboard. A Scorecard for TNS Consumer would track revenue, gross margin, admin cost ratio, DSO, and brand-by-brand performance — creating the early warning system the company desperately needs to identify which cost lines are recoverable.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

The company's existential priority is closing the gap between MUR 32.4M admin costs and MUR 12.5M gross profit. Rocks would force the leadership team to identify 3–5 specific, measurable quarterly priorities — brand additions, cost reduction initiatives, revenue targets — rather than operating in perpetual reactive mode under financial pressure.

V/TO (at Group Level)

TNS Consumer needs the Aptis Group to answer: what is this subsidiary for in 10 years? If it's a MUR 200M FMCG platform, the current losses are an investment. If it's a MUR 50M satellite, the business model doesn't work. A Group V/TO that positions each cluster — including TNS Consumer — would provide the strategic clarity that funding decisions, headcount decisions, and brand strategy decisions require.

Issues List (IDS)

The administration cost crisis (MUR 32.4M) is a massive unresolved issue. IDS — Identify, Discuss, Solve — would give the leadership team a disciplined framework for confronting and resolving the structural problems killing profitability: which overhead categories are truly necessary, which are duplications from group-level services, and what the rationalisation plan looks like.

Level 10 Meeting Cadence

Directors splitting time across Aptis Holdings, TNS Tobacco, TNS Consumer and other entities creates a meeting culture driven by urgency rather than discipline. A dedicated weekly L10 Meeting for TNS Consumer's operational team — even if virtual — would create the rhythmic review that a loss-making, fast-growing distributor urgently needs to course-correct in real time.

Recommendations

MUR 32.4M in administration costs is the single biggest threat to viability. Immediately commission a line-by-line audit of all admin cost categories: headcount costs, inter-company service charges from Aptis Holdings, rent, marketing, logistics. Identify what is genuinely TNS Consumer cost vs. group overhead allocation. Success: admin costs reduced to below MUR 15M, bringing the company within reach of break-even at current revenue.
