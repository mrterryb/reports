Executive Summary

A long-established specialist roofing contractor with solid profits and decent liquidity, but with a balance sheet that is much thinner than its operating scale and a working-capital model leaning heavily on suppliers.

Skill Roofing Limited is a private Mauritian company incorporated on 21 January 1994 and registered at Lot No 4, Industrial Zone, Geoffroy, Bambous. Public-facing material positions the company as a specialist in pre-painted steel and aluminium roof coverings, metal works, partitions, insulation, and steel-building related work. The business presents itself as having operated continuously since 1994 and as a family business founded by Bernard Gallet.

The corporate ownership picture is simple at the immediate level and fuzzy at the ultimate level. The CBRIS extract shows Skillsons Ltd holding 100% of the shares, while the operating directors are Bernard Gallet and Marie Sylvie Audrey Gallet. That strongly suggests family control, but the beneficial-owner picture cannot be fully completed from the documents currently in hand.

Financially, the company is not distressed. FY2025 revenue reached MUR 68,009,033, gross margin was a robust 42.2%, and net profit after tax was MUR 6,660,966. The catch is in the structure underneath: equity is only MUR 6,608,333 against total liabilities of MUR 28,444,980, and trade payables sit at MUR 16,329,851, equivalent to around 152 days of cost of sales. That is not a collapse signal, but it is exactly the kind of hidden operational tension that makes EOS relevant.

Key Finding — Good Business, Thin Shock Absorbers
Skill Roofing looks like a competent, profitable specialist contractor rather than a mess. The real issue is not obvious commercial weakness; it is the combination of thin capital, large supplier funding, and likely founder-centred decision flow. That is a very different EOS sales conversation. The entry point is not “you are chaotic.” It is “you have built something real, but you are carrying more execution and cash-cycle risk than your profit line suggests.”

At a Glance

Commercial Strengths

Thirty-plus years of operating history, clear specialist positioning in metal roofing, steel works and related installation, visible local contact infrastructure, and a profit profile that suggests the company is not competing purely on suicidal pricing.

Financial Vulnerabilities

Working-capital dependence on suppliers is the biggest red flag. Equity is thin for the level of activity, current liabilities are high relative to the capital base, and retained earnings are shown at nil despite profitable trading.

EOS Readiness Signal

Established founder-led family business entering the “good company, fragile structure” zone. These businesses often need clearer accountability, better measurables, and leadership decongestion before the hidden bottlenecks start biting.

Structural Competitive Advantages

The first defensible advantage is longevity. In roofing and specialised steel work, 30+ years of survival matters because it implies reference projects, supplier credibility and practical know-how. The second is skill continuity: the company publicly claims unusually long employee tenure, which is not easy to replicate in a craft-heavy execution business. The third is focused positioning. The business is not trying to be everything in construction; it appears to sit in a specialised slice where competence is visible and margins can still exist.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Demand for lighter, faster-to-install roofing and steel building systems remains structurally attractive in Mauritius.
  • Construction and project businesses are brutally exposed to labour quality, site coordination and rework risk; margin can disappear fast when leadership bandwidth is thin.
  • Clients increasingly expect better finishing, insulation and system quality, which favours specialists over random generalists.
  • Working-capital pressure in the local market can quietly punish even profitable contractors through slow debtor collection and stretched supplier terms.
  • A basic digital presence is no longer fatal, but it does cap credibility with certain commercial buyers and consultants.
Competitive Gap
Skill Roofing’s public positioning is credible, but the digital presentation is still old-school and founder-centric. In a tighter market, that can become a subtle drag on higher-value commercial opportunities and recruitment.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension below is rated from public data, corporate records and the financial pattern visible in the latest filing.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 6 / 10

Headcount is not publicly confirmed, so no fantasy here. Still, the revenue scale, 30-year history and service complexity strongly suggest a real operating company rather than a micro-operator with a pickup truck and a prayer.

02 — Leadership Openness 6 / 10

There is no public evidence of modern management systems, but the existence of a maintained website, visible founder profile and continuity of corporate filings suggests this is not a closed bunker either. Open-minded enough to engage; not yet proven willing to install hard accountability.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

This is where the fit is strongest. Good gross margin, good net margin, big payables, thin equity, and likely founder-led operational concentration — classic signs of a company that has grown into complexity and now needs stronger internal structure.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 6 / 10

The company looks competent operationally, but the financial structure hints that process discipline around cash, quoting, collections or project handoff is not fully institutionalised. Good companies often hide messy inner plumbing.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

The public claim that employees average more than two decades of service, if broadly true, is a huge signal. That usually points to craft pride, loyalty and continuity. It can also point to informal power structures. Both are real.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 7 / 10

The governance picture suggests a small family leadership circle. That usually means decisions can be fast, but ownership of roles may be blurry and too much may still route through one or two people.

07 — Vision Clarity 6 / 10

The market identity is clear enough: specialist roofing and related metal works. What is not visible publicly is a sharper strategic direction around growth, capacity, margins, customer mix or leadership succession.

08 — Meeting Culture 4 / 10

There is simply no evidence of structured meeting rhythm. In founder-led Mauritian SMEs, reactive coordination is still the default until proven otherwise. I would assume firefighting until shown otherwise.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Skill Roofing

Scorecard & Measurables

The business needs a brutally practical weekly view of quoted jobs, awarded jobs, cash collected, aged receivables, supplier exposure, site issues and labour capacity. Right now the balance sheet is telling us the scorecard story after the damage.

Accountability Chart

Founder-led specialists usually blur sales, estimating, purchasing, site execution and collections. A real Accountability Chart would expose where the business is still person-dependent instead of seat-dependent.

Rocks

The first 90 days should not be philosophical. They should attack debtor discipline, creditor discipline, job costing visibility and one leadership bottleneck that everyone already knows is there.

Issues List & IDS

This company likely has recurring “same movie, different week” issues hiding under operational normality. IDS is useful precisely because profitable founder-led businesses are excellent at tolerating problems for years.

Level 10 Meeting

If there is no structured weekly leadership rhythm, this is low-hanging fruit. It turns tribal knowledge into shared visibility and stops everything from living in Bernard’s head or in corridor conversations.

Vision / Traction Organizer

The business identity is clear, but the next-stage strategic direction is not. V/TO would force clarity around ideal projects, profit expectations, capacity ceilings, and what the company should stop doing.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations in total: four financial / operational and four EOS-specific. Sequence matters. First stabilise the cash and control picture; then institutionalise the business without breaking what already works.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Attack creditor stretch before it attacks you

Have finance and leadership map the top 10 suppliers by exposure, current days outstanding, and dependency risk within 14 days. The goal is not to pay everyone overnight like a hero in a bad movie. The goal is to identify who can hurt the business first and create a controlled reduction plan.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Decode the other-expense and reserves lines

The CFO-equivalent or external accountant should reconcile admin costs, other expenses, other reserves and nil retained earnings into something leadership can actually understand. Success looks like a management pack where nobody needs a Ouija board to interpret the equity section.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 03

Install project-by-project cash visibility

For every major job, track quote, revised budget, materials committed, labour used, billing issued, cash collected and margin left. Give one named owner responsibility for the report every week. This is where hidden profitability leakage usually lives.

Within 30–60 Days
Recommendation 04

Formalise receivables discipline

Set a weekly aged-debt review with clear escalation rules, not vague hope. Success means no receivable over agreed threshold days without a documented action owner and next step. In this business, cash collection is operations wearing a finance hat.

Within 30–60 Days

EOS Implementation Recommendations

Recommendation 05

Run a real Focus Day around leadership load and cash-cycle pain

Do not begin with motivational wallpaper. Start with the practical truth: where jobs stall, where decisions bunch up, where supplier pressure shows up, and where the founder is still the unofficial answer to everything.

During EOS Kickoff
Recommendation 06

Build the first Accountability Chart hypothesis

At minimum, separate Sales / Estimating, Operations / Sites, Procurement / Supply, Finance / Administration and Leadership. The point is to expose role confusion fast. This alone will reveal whether the company is organised by seats or by personalities.

During EOS Kickoff
Recommendation 07

Make the first Rock a cash-and-margin control Rock

Not “improve communication.” That is fluffy nonsense. The first Rock should target a concrete result such as reducing DPO concentration risk, tightening DSO, and installing weekly job margin visibility for all active major projects.

First 90 Days
Recommendation 08

Use the V/TO to answer one hard strategic question

What business should Skill Roofing deliberately become over the next three years: premium specialist installer, broader steel-envelope contractor, or more process-driven scalable operator? The answer changes hiring, pricing, and structure.

Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

Skill Roofing looks like a good underlying business with more structural fragility than its profit line admits. That is exactly why it is interesting. The opportunity is not to rescue the company; it is to help leadership convert long-built know-how into a sturdier, less person-dependent, better-controlled machine.

The Recommended Sequence

  1. 01Within 2 weeks: validate supplier exposure, debtor ageing and the meaning of reserves / retained earnings.
  2. 02Within 30 days: map leadership roles, decision bottlenecks and live-project reporting gaps.
  3. 03Within 45 days: run Focus Day and surface the real issues list, not the polite version.
  4. 04Within 60 days: install first weekly scorecard and Level 10 meeting rhythm.
  5. 05Within 90 days: launch first Rocks around cash discipline, accountability and project-margin visibility.
