Executive Summary

A 1983 Mauritian chemicals and FMCG manufacturer with real commercial heft, but the financial profile says one thing clearly: profit is decent, cash discipline is doing the heavy lifting.

Archemics Ltd is a long-established Mauritian manufacturer and distributor of detergents, personal care products, adhesives, textile auxiliaries and industrial cleaning solutions. The company operates from Terre Rouge and trades across both B2C and B2B channels, with activity spanning home care, beauty care, pool care, DIY, car care, professional hygiene and industrial applications.

Ownership is highly concentrated. The CBRIS extract shows Harel Mallac & Co. Ltd holding 100% of the 40,000 ordinary shares, which makes Archemics a fully controlled subsidiary rather than a founder-led standalone business. That matters. It likely gives Archemics brand backing, governance structure and procurement leverage, but it may also mean slower decision rights where group approval is needed.

FY2024 turnover reached MUR 713,905,928 with a healthy gross margin of 44.8%, but net margin landed at just 4.9%. Liquidity is serviceable rather than comfortable, with a current ratio of 1.19× and quick ratio of 1.14×. The sharper issue is working capital shape: receivables sit at roughly 82 days while payables stretch to about 143 days. Translation: the business is financing itself partly through supplier timing, which is effective until it is not.

Key Finding — Strong Commercial Engine, Tight Cash Conversion
Archemics looks like a credible, scaled operating business with solid brand assets, decent margin structure and a diversified offer. The real conversation is not “can they sell?” It is “how much operational friction, cross-functional complexity and working-capital drag sits behind those sales?” That makes it a legitimate EOS prospect, especially if leadership feels the pain of coordination, speed and accountability across business lines.

At a Glance

Commercial Strengths

Scale and portfolio breadth stand out. Archemics combines local manufacturing with distribution of recognised international brands, serves both B2C and institutional markets, and operates with a real multi-channel footprint rather than a one-trick product line.

Financial Vulnerabilities

Working-capital dependence is the main concern. Receivables are slow, payables are heavily stretched, and current liabilities materially exceed the level you would want to see if the business hits a disruption in collections or supplier terms.

EOS Readiness Signal

Complexity is visible from the outside. Multiple product families, mixed B2B/B2C routes to market, group ownership, brand partnerships, and a recent procurement leadership hire all point to a business that likely benefits from tighter clarity, rhythm and accountability.

Company Overview

A mature Mauritian chemicals platform with group backing, franchise relationships and a much wider portfolio than the name alone suggests.

Founding and Corporate Record

Archemics Ltd was incorporated on 16 May 1983 as File No. C3964. It is a live private domestic company with registered office at 18 Edith Cavell Street, Port Louis, Mauritius. The business registration number is C07003964. The company was formerly known as Henkel Chemicals (Mtius) Limited before its rebranding to Archemics.

CBRIS shows stated capital of MUR 400,000 divided into 40,000 ordinary shares with par value MUR 10. Annual returns for 2023, 2024 and 2025 were filed, which is a good basic compliance signal. HM Secretaries Ltd acts as company secretary. Nexia Baker & Arenson is listed as auditor from July 2023, replacing the earlier long-standing BDO & Co. record.

Products and Services Portfolio

The portfolio is broad and commercially useful. Public materials show activity across household detergents, industrial and institutional hygiene, cosmetics and beauty care, industrial and consumer adhesives, textile auxiliaries, pool care, car care and DIY. Well-known brands associated with Archemics include Le Chat, Super Croix, Xtra, Mir, Fa, Diadermine, Schwarzkopf, Pattex, Loctite and Tangit. The professional segment also includes technical support, dosing systems, audits and training, which means this is not just a product reseller; it has a solutions angle.

Geographic Presence

CBRIS business activity points to an operating base off Motorway M2, Terre Rouge, Mauritius, with retail traces in Quatre Bornes for older DeClic activity. Public-facing material positions Archemics as serving Mauritius and the wider Indian Ocean region. The company’s website includes e-commerce functionality and explicitly markets to both domestic consumers and institutional buyers.

Digital Presence Check
Unlike many Mauritian manufacturers that still treat digital as decorative wallpaper, Archemics has a live website, social media activity and an online shop. That does not prove digital excellence, but it does show basic commercial seriousness.

Charge Register

The charges register shows a long history of secured facilities, mostly floating charges over undertaking, goodwill, movable and immovable property. Recorded charge amounts total approximately MUR 191,950,000 across filings from 1999 through 2023. The largest recent disclosed charge is MUR 53.0 million dated 4 May 2023, with other notable 2021 charges of MUR 20.0 million, MUR 7.0 million and MUR 20.0 million.

Charge Pattern Interpretation
The low long-term borrowing balance in the summary accounts does not mean the company operates debt-free. The charge history says Archemics keeps banking lines and secured flexibility available. Sensible, yes. But it also means treasury discipline matters. Businesses that live on facilities and supplier patience can look calm right up until the day they don’t.

Structural Competitive Advantages

Three advantages look genuinely defensible. First, Archemics combines local manufacturing and imported principal brands, which is stronger than being just an importer or just a toll manufacturer. Second, it appears to wrap products with technical support, training and audits in the professional segment, which creates stickier institutional relationships. Third, Harel Mallac ownership adds credibility, channels and resilience that smaller local chemical players cannot easily fake with a nice brochure and a prayer.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Institutional hygiene remains structurally important after the pandemic, especially in hospitality, food and industrial settings.
  • “Made in Moris” positioning can support local manufacturing brands where supply reliability and national sourcing matter.
  • Imported-brand dependence remains a double-edged sword: strong brand pull, but vulnerability to principal strategy and foreign supply disruptions.
  • Local chemical and cleaning markets are noisy and price-sensitive, which punishes businesses that do not differentiate beyond cheapest bottle wins.
  • Sustainability expectations are rising, and Archemics is at least visibly trying to play in that lane rather than pretending the world is still 2006.
  • Procurement, stock planning and credit control become more critical as product breadth and channel complexity increase.
Competitive Reality Check
Archemics probably does not lose on credibility. It risks losing on complexity tax. In businesses like this, competitors do not always beat you with a better product. Sometimes they beat you because they are simpler, faster and more focused.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity8 / 10

Public sources indicate roughly 201–500 employees, which is slightly above the classic EOS sweet spot but still very workable. More importantly, the business is operationally messy enough in a productive way: multiple product families, mixed B2B and B2C channels, technical service layers and group governance.

02 — Leadership Openness6 / 10

There are signs of quality systems, sustainability language, board refresh and professional recruitment. That usually correlates with some openness to structure. But group ownership can also mean openness is conditional: “yes, but only if it fits the mothership.”

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points8 / 10

The classic EOS pain signals are easy to imagine here: cross-functional drag, competing priorities, working-capital friction, likely siloed functions, and complexity from serving different customer groups under one roof. The March 2026 procurement hire is another tell.

04 — Current Systems and Processes6 / 10

ISO and formal quality references suggest some system discipline exists. Good. But the balance-sheet shape still says process performance is not fully harmonised, especially around collections, liabilities visibility and probably cross-functional execution.

05 — Company Culture6 / 10

Public communications stress people, sustainability and quality. That usually points to an intention toward a disciplined culture. But public messaging is lipstick; it is not proof. Without internal evidence, this deserves a cautious middle score.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns6 / 10

Full ownership by Harel Mallac likely brings clarity at the very top, but not necessarily at operating-seat level. EOS would add most value if leaders feel that things fall between functions, approvals travel too slowly, or accountability gets blurred by group structure.

07 — Vision Clarity7 / 10

Externally, the company can articulate what it is, what it sells and where it plays. That is already better than half the market. The question is whether that clarity is translated internally into a shared operating vision, not just marketing copy.

08 — Meeting Culture5 / 10

No public evidence proves a disciplined operating rhythm. In companies with this many moving parts, the default is reactive fire-fighting disguised as busy professionalism. A proper Level 10 cadence would likely expose and improve that fast.

EOS Suitability

Overall Rating: Moderate-High — Qualified Positive

Archemics looks like a credible EOS candidate because the company appears large enough, complex enough and commercially serious enough to benefit from tighter accountability, execution rhythm and cross-functional clarity. The gating factor is not whether EOS could help. It almost certainly could. The gating factor is whether group ownership allows the operating team enough decision space to actually use it properly.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for Archemics

Scorecard & Measurables

Because the numbers already hint at working-capital friction, Archemics needs weekly leading indicators around collections, overdue debtors, supplier aging, fill rate, stock cover, service levels and margin by channel. Monthly nostalgia is useless here.

Accountability Chart

The public profile screams functional overlap risk: manufacturing, procurement, sales, professional hygiene, retail and group oversight. EOS would force a brutally clear answer to who truly owns what.

Issues List & IDS

Businesses like this accumulate operational friction everywhere. Without a disciplined issues discipline, the same problems keep changing shirts and walking back into the room.

Level 10 Meeting

If Archemics is suffering from reactive coordination, a proper weekly meeting rhythm would create alignment fast. Not a chat. Not a reporting séance. A real problem-solving cadence.

Rocks

The first 90 days should likely target one or two major execution bottlenecks, not a wish list. Credit-control improvement and current-liability clarity are obvious candidates.

Vision/Traction Organizer

Because Archemics sits inside a larger group, the V/TO becomes useful for translating group ambition into a clean operating roadmap for the Archemics leadership team itself.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations follow: four financial/operational first, then four EOS-specific. Sequence matters. Cash and clarity before theatre.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Attack receivables ageing like it is a profit initiative

CFO/Finance Lead should produce a full debtor ageing by major customer and channel within 14 days, set weekly collection targets, and assign named owners to the top 20 overdue balances. Success = cut DSO from roughly 82 days to below 65 days within two quarters.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Explode the “other current liabilities” bucket

Finance should break down the MUR 103,530,382 line into real categories and identify which items are structural versus timing-based. Success = a clean map of liabilities by nature, age and owner within 30 days, with no mystery line left hiding in the shadows.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 03

Protect supplier confidence before it becomes a negotiation problem

Procurement and Finance should review top suppliers by spend and payment terms, identify any stretch risk, and define a controlled payable strategy rather than accidental drift. Success = maintain supply continuity while reducing the most stressed creditor positions over 60–90 days.

Within 30–60 Days
Recommendation 04

Split margin by channel, not just in total

Commercial leadership should calculate gross margin by consumer retail, professional hygiene and specialty industrial lines. Success = a ranked view of where margin is earned, leaked or subsidised, allowing pricing and product-mix decisions with teeth instead of vibes.

Within 30–60 Days

EOS Implementation Recommendations

Recommendation 05

Run a Focus Day with the true operating leadership team

Start with a one-day working session involving the actual leaders who run the business day to day, not just directors. Success = shared diagnosis of the biggest constraints, agreement on the leadership team, and commitment to a 90-day operating cadence.

Within 90 Days / During EOS
Recommendation 06

Build an Accountability Chart hypothesis before org-change debates begin

Draft the major seats first: commercial, operations/manufacturing, procurement/supply chain, finance, professional solutions, and possibly product/category leadership. Success = clear seat ownership and reduced cross-functional confusion before anyone starts defending turf.

Within 90 Days / During EOS
Recommendation 07

Make the first Rock brutally practical: cash conversion

The first quarter Rock should target working-capital improvement, not a fluffy transformation slogan. Success = measurable reduction in overdue receivables, visibility on liability buckets and a live weekly scorecard reviewed by the leadership team.

Within 90 Days / During EOS
Recommendation 08

Use the V/TO to answer one strategic question: what must Archemics own locally?

Because the company combines local manufacturing and principal brands, the V/TO should force a decision on where Archemics wants to differentiate: formulation, service model, speed, local brands, channel strength or industrial expertise. Success = a sharper strategic identity that guides execution.

Within 90 Days / During EOS

Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

Archemics looks like a real business with real muscle, not a fragile vanity operation. The opportunity is obvious: a diversified chemical and FMCG platform with good gross economics and group backing. The obstacle is equally obvious: complexity tax, working-capital strain and the risk that accountability gets blurred inside a group-owned structure.

The Recommended Sequence

  1. 01Within 2 weeks: build debtor ageing, liability breakdown and supplier-risk map.
  2. 02Within 30 days: confirm the real operating leadership team and decision rights.
  3. 03Within 45 days: run an EOS Focus Day around execution friction and cash conversion.
  4. 04Within 60–90 days: install weekly Scorecard, Issues List and Level 10 Meeting.
  5. 05Within the first quarter: complete Accountability Chart and launch first cash-focused Rock.
