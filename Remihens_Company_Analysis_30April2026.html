Executive Summary

A founder-led engineering contractor with 11 years of growth behind it, exceptional profitability for its size, and a debtor book that is quietly consuming its operational flexibility.

Remihens Co Ltd is a Mauritius-registered private company incorporated on 25 April 2014, operating under the trading name REMIHENS. The company specialises in water treatment, wastewater management, and mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) engineering services. It designs, supplies, installs and maintains water treatment plants and MEP systems across Mauritius and the wider Indian Ocean — with project references in Madagascar, Rodrigues and the Seychelles. In 2019, within five years of its founding, it became the official distributor of German wastewater treatment plant manufacturer Clearfox® for the Indian Ocean and South African region — a commercially significant milestone that signalled both technical credibility and export ambition. The company operates from KL House, Motorway M3, Riche Terre, and its website trades as remihensgroup.com.

Remihens is 100% owned by its founding director, Sookurun Priscilla, who holds all 100 ordinary shares and has been director since 18 February 2020. A second director, Sookurun Ramkissoon Mahendra Madun Mohun, was appointed on 1 January 2024 — almost certainly a family member given the shared surname, pointing to a classic founder-family governance structure. The company is entirely privately held with no group connections, subsidiaries, or external investors on record. Its secretary is Fortenberry Corporate Services Ltd, appointed in July 2024. There are no registered charges against the company.

Financially, Remihens is in excellent shape for a business of its size. For the year ended 30 June 2025, revenue reached MUR 163.8 million — a striking figure for a sub-25-employee SME in Mauritius. The gross margin stands at 42.6%, the net margin at 15.5%, and the return on equity at 60.1%. The balance sheet is debt-free in terms of formal borrowings, and the current ratio is a comfortable 3.27x. The one structural vulnerability that stands out is the debtor days figure of 87 days — MUR 39.1 million sitting in trade receivables, which in a project-based business reflects normal billing patterns but creates meaningful working capital risk. The MUR 38.4 million under non-current liabilities classified as "Others" likely represents deferred income or advance payments from clients, and warrants clarification. EOS suitability scores 6.5 out of 10 — a qualified positive.

Key Finding — Exceptional Profitability Masking a Collections Problem
Remihens punches well above its weight: MUR 163.8 million in revenue, a 42.6% gross margin and MUR 25.5 million net profit for a company with fewer than 25 employees is an extraordinary result. However, 87 debtor days means MUR 39.1 million — nearly 24% of annual revenue — is on the street at any given time. In a project-based engineering business operating across multiple territories, this creates cash flow dependency on client payment discipline that the company does not fully control. The Clearfox distributor role and the Indian Ocean footprint position Remihens for further growth, but without a formal collections rhythm and billing cadence built into operations, that growth will be funded by the bank account rather than by the business itself.

Products and Services Portfolio

Remihens operates across four integrated service lines that create natural cross-sell opportunities and sticky recurring revenue from operations and maintenance contracts:

Water Treatment: Design, supply and installation of potable and process water treatment systems. Includes reverse osmosis, filtration and disinfection plants for commercial, industrial and public-sector clients. Regional expertise across island environments where imported technology is required.

Wastewater Treatment: As the exclusive Clearfox® distributor for the Indian Ocean and South Africa, Remihens supplies, installs and commissions modular and bespoke wastewater treatment plants. Clearfox technology is used in hotels, resorts, industrial facilities and municipal applications. Reference project includes Le Meridien hotel in Mauritius.

MEP (Mechanical, Electrical and Plumbing): Full MEP design, supply and installation for building and infrastructure projects. Covers commercial, hospitality, industrial and residential developments. MEP services are often bundled with water or wastewater scope, enabling comprehensive project delivery.

Operation and Maintenance: Post-installation O&M contracts for water and wastewater plants. This service line is strategically important — it provides recurring, lower-capital-intensity revenue and builds long-term client relationships that are hard for competitors to displace without performance failure.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Exclusive Clearfox distributorship for the Indian Ocean and South Africa. This is the most strategically significant moat Remihens possesses. Competitors in Mauritius, Seychelles, Madagascar and South Africa cannot legally supply or install Clearfox wastewater treatment systems — they must come through Remihens. This creates a captive supply chain position in a growing market segment driven by environmental regulation and hospitality sector expansion. The distributorship has been held since approximately 2019 and, with seven years of tenure, is likely well-entrenched.

2. Integrated water-wastewater-MEP capability with O&M continuity. Very few Mauritius contractors can deliver all three disciplines under one contract and then maintain the installed systems on a recurring basis. This integration reduces client coordination cost, simplifies procurement, and creates high switching costs once O&M contracts are in place. The combination of design, supply, install and maintain under one roof is a genuine structural advantage, not a marketing claim.

3. Multi-territory project track record with island-environment expertise. Operating effectively in Rodrigues, Seychelles and Madagascar requires logistics, import clearance, and regulatory knowledge that most Mauritius contractors lack. This creates a meaningful barrier to entry for local competitors attempting to follow Remihens into these markets.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Growing environmental regulation in Mauritius and the Indian Ocean: Increasing pressure from government and international hotel chains for certified wastewater treatment positions Clearfox-equipped installers at the front of the compliance queue.
  • Hospitality sector expansion: New hotel and resort construction across Mauritius, Seychelles and the Maldives consistently requires MEP and water treatment services — Remihens's core client segment is structurally growing.
  • Climate-driven water scarcity awareness: Islands across the Indian Ocean face freshwater supply pressure, creating sustained demand for water treatment and recycling systems that Remihens can supply and maintain.
  • African infrastructure demand: The Clearfox South Africa territorial right gives Remihens access to one of Africa's largest infrastructure markets — a significant optionality not yet fully monetised given the company's current scale.
  • Price and margin pressure from international contractors: Large-scale MEP projects on the main island are increasingly contested by international groups with lower overheads and stronger balance sheets — Remihens must protect its niche positioning rather than compete head-to-head on large commercial developments.
  • Key-person concentration risk: With a sub-25-employee team and a founder-led structure, the departure or incapacitation of the founder would create significant operational disruption — a risk amplified by the multi-territory scope of the business.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 6 / 10

ZoomInfo reports fewer than 25 employees; however MUR 163.8M in revenue implies extraordinarily high productivity per head, suggesting either significant subcontractor reliance or that the employee count is understated. The company is squarely within the EOS size sweet spot in headcount terms. The multi-territory operation (Mauritius, Rodrigues, Seychelles, Madagascar) and multi-service-line model (water treatment, wastewater, MEP, O&M) create genuine organisational complexity that EOS tools are designed to address. Score is mid-range because sub-25 people is at the lower end of the range where EOS delivers maximum value — optimal EOS companies tend to have 30–150 employees.

02 — Leadership Openness 6 / 10

The founder's personal origin story — cashing in a provident fund, starting from scratch, growing to MUR 163.8M — indicates high agency, resilience and ambition. These traits are positive EOS signals. The appointment of a second director (likely family) in January 2024 shows willingness to share governance responsibility, even if informally. The appointment of a professional corporate secretary in July 2024 shows growing compliance awareness. However, a 100%-founder-owned, founder-led company with no external investors and no visible management structure below director level suggests a strong controlling personality who may resist the vulnerability and transparency that EOS requires. Score held at 6 — strong potential but the conversation must surface whether the founder is truly open to being held accountable by a Leadership Team, not just managing them.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

MUR 163.8M in revenue for a sub-25-person team is the single clearest signal of a business in classic EOS pain. The founder is almost certainly the hub of all major decisions, project relationships, and technical authority. The 87-day DSO suggests no systematic billing and collections process exists. The MUR 38.4M unspecified non-current liability suggests financial complexity that has outgrown informal management. Multi-territory operations running from one office, one founding director, and a small team are textbook signs of a business that has grown faster than its management infrastructure. The Clearfox distributorship creates regional expansion optionality that will require structured processes and delegated accountability to exploit — precisely what EOS delivers.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

Annual returns are filed on time, financial statements are produced and approved within six months of year-end, and a corporate secretary was formalised in 2024 — these are signs of baseline administrative competence. However, the zero distribution costs (no formalised sales function), the 87-day DSO (no systematic AR process), and the absence of any named auditor (no third-party financial review) suggest that operating processes are largely informal and founder-dependent. There is no public evidence of project management systems, ERP, or CRM usage. The business is likely run on Excel, WhatsApp, and founder intuition — which works at this scale but will break as the company grows.

05 — Company Culture 6 / 10

The founding story — bootstrapped from a provident fund payout — instils a culture of resourcefulness and accountability that is genuinely positive. A company that has grown to MUR 163.8M on those origins has attracted and retained capable people. The Clearfox distributorship renewal implies the German principal has confidence in the company's professionalism. However, with no visibility into HR practices, team stability, or internal culture, the score is held at mid-range. The family director appointment could strengthen or fragment culture depending on execution.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 4 / 10

With one director since founding and a second added only in January 2024, all strategic and operational decisions have almost certainly flowed through one person for 10 years. A company generating MUR 163.8M in revenue with this governance structure will have severe decision-making bottlenecks — the founder is simultaneously the CEO, the key account manager, the technical authority, and the financial controller. This is the most EOS-relevant pain point in the business. An Accountability Chart would immediately surface role confusion; Rocks would force structured quarterly prioritisation; the L10 would create a forum where decisions are made collectively rather than by the founder in real time.

07 — Vision Clarity 6 / 10

The company's website states an ambition to become "the most reliable MEP and water treatment company in Mauritius and the Indian Ocean" — this is a clear directional statement, even if not a formally documented V/TO. The Clearfox distributorship gives the business a concrete growth vector (Indian Ocean and South Africa). The 11-year trajectory from nothing to MUR 163.8M suggests the founder has a clear personal vision. However, whether this vision is shared with, and understood by, the wider team is unknown — and in a founder-led business it rarely is without a formal process like the V/TO to make it explicit.

08 — Meeting Culture 4 / 10

No evidence of structured meeting rhythms exists. In a sub-25-person founder-led company with multi-territory project delivery, meetings are almost certainly reactive — called to solve problems, not to review scorecards, clear issues, and set quarterly rocks. The 87-day DSO alone is evidence that nobody is reviewing a weekly collections number in a structured setting. The Level 10 Meeting cadence — 90-minute weekly with a fixed agenda — would be among the highest-impact early EOS tools for Remihens.

A company operating across Mauritius, Rodrigues, Seychelles and Madagascar with no visible meeting cadence is resolving problems in the field, by phone, or not at all. The Level 10 Meeting — 90 minutes, every week, same agenda — creates the structured rhythm that converts reactive fire-fighting into proactive issue resolution. For Remihens, this is potentially the single highest-ROI tool in the first 90 days.

Rocks

The Clearfox distributorship creates a clear 90-day Rock candidate: formalise and grow the South Africa territory, or execute the first Rodrigues O&M contract expansion. Without quarterly Rocks, the company's growth priorities compete with daily project demands and the founder never gets strategic focus time. Rocks enforce the discipline that bridges vision and execution.

Issues List and IDS

The MUR 38.4M unclassified NCL liability, the 87-day DSO, the admin cost creep, the digital presence gap — these are all issues that a well-run business surfaces and resolves systematically. Identify, Discuss, Solve (IDS) in the L10 meeting gives the leadership team a repeatable framework for clearing these backlogs rather than carrying them indefinitely.

Vision / Traction Organiser (V/TO)

The company has a stated vision ("most reliable MEP and water company in Indian Ocean") but it lives in the founder's head and on the website. The V/TO forces the 10-year target, 3-year picture, 1-year plan and Core Values into one shared document. For a family-directed company with an 11-year track record and a regional distributorship, articulating a documented V/TO is the first step to building a leadership team that can execute without the founder in every room.

The Bottom Line

Remihens is a genuinely impressive business: bootstrapped from nothing in 2014, MUR 163.8M in revenue by 2025, 42.6% gross margin, zero debt, and an exclusive regional distributorship that no competitor can replicate.
