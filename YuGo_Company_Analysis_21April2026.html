Executive Summary

YuGo Limited is a seven-year-old Mauritian ride-hailing and mobility platform with a strong product, a growing user base, and a financially unsustainable cost structure that has accumulated MUR 11.9M in losses against a share capital of MUR 13.5M — the defining tension of this business.

Incorporated on 14 December 2017 and operating from 32 Rue St George, Port Louis, YuGo Limited (File No. C152433, BRN C17152433) is the operator of the Yugo app — Mauritius's leading native ride-hailing platform. Founded by Kaushik Rathod and Evanee Rathod, the company connects passengers to PSV-licensed taxi and van drivers across the island, with services expanded to include food delivery and grocery ordering. The app has achieved 50,000+ downloads on Google Play and passed the 20,000 active user milestone following its first angel funding round. Yugo holds the "Made in Moris" accreditation, signalling its status as a recognised local enterprise.

Ownership of YuGo Limited is held across three shareholders: Ker Ventures Ltd (6,000 shares, 50.6%), Mo Angels Second Limited (4,363 shares, 36.8%), and Media Alliance Ltd (1,500 shares, 12.6%). The Mo Angels connection is strategic: Isabelle Alvares Pereira de Melo, a co-founder of Mo Angels (Mauritius's first syndicated angel investment group) and a director at IBL Ltd and Five35 Ventures, sits on the YuGo board. Ker Ventures Ltd appears to be the Aptis Group investment vehicle — the link through which Oscar Mamet's Aptis Group holds its stake. Mohammed Reaz Emambocus, a certified accountant and director at Le Défi Media Group, provides governance and financial oversight. The shareholder structure reflects a startup that has moved through multiple funding rounds and assembled a commercially credible board.

Financially, FY2024 shows a company with genuine product-market fit — an 87.4% gross margin on MUR 1.49M revenue — but with an operating cost structure (MUR 2.4M in admin and other expenses, primarily platform and technology costs) that dwarfs its revenue by 62%. The net loss of MUR 686K on a base of MUR 1.49M in revenue, combined with MUR 11.9M in accumulated losses, signals that YuGo is in a classic startup scale-up dilemma: the unit economics work, but the volume is not yet there to absorb overhead. The EOS suitability score of 5.9/10 reflects the strong founder leadership and clear vision, tempered by the financial pressure and early-stage operating structure that has not yet been formalised.

Key Finding — A Commercially Sound Product Running Out of Runway
YuGo's 87.4% gross margin proves the service model works. The problem is not the product — it is scale. At MUR 1.49M in revenue against MUR 2.4M in operating expenses, the company needs to roughly double revenue before it reaches operating breakeven. With MUR 11.9M in accumulated losses already absorbed against MUR 13.5M in share capital, the equity cushion is almost entirely consumed. The next 12–18 months are critical: YuGo must either grow revenue sharply, secure additional funding, or rationalise its cost base. The Aptis Group connection (via Ker Ventures) is the most strategically important variable — if Aptis decides to actively support YuGo's growth (distribution channels, corporate accounts, fleet partnerships), the trajectory changes materially. The conversation with Oscar Mamet is not a standard EOS pitch — it is a strategic partnership conversation about how Aptis can accelerate YuGo's path to profitability.

Products and Services Portfolio

YuGo operates as an all-in-one mobility and delivery super-app for Mauritius. The platform connects users to PSV-licensed taxi and van drivers across the island through a native mobile application available on iOS (App Store) and Android (Google Play). Current version: 5.6.2 (updated February 2025), indicating active ongoing development. Core service lines include: (1) On-demand taxi rides with real-time tracking, transparent distance-based fare calculation, and multiple payment options (cash, credit card, my.t money, digital wallet); (2) Full-day taxi service for island tours and multi-destination travel; (3) Food and grocery delivery in partnership with restaurants and stores. Additional features include an in-app SOS function, ride-sharing capabilities, in-app chat and call, and a driver loyalty rewards programme ("Yugo Partner" programme). The driver-facing "Yugo Partner" app is a separate application enabling drivers to manage bookings and track earnings. YuGo 2.0 represents the evolution to a super-app model combining transport, food, and grocery into a single user interface.

Geographic Presence

YuGo operates exclusively within Mauritius — the island is the company's entire addressable market. The registered office at 32 Rue St George, Port Louis, serves as the principal place of business. The platform covers the full island, connecting users to a network of taxi and van drivers across all major zones including Port Louis, Curepipe, Rose Hill, and tourist corridors. No evidence of export or regional expansion activity has been identified. Mauritius's compact geography (2,040 km²) and population of approximately 1.3 million make full island coverage achievable with a relatively modest driver network. The absence of Uber, Bolt, or other international ride-hailing platforms — due to regulatory restrictions requiring PSV licensing — provides a protected domestic market.

Digital Presence Note
The YuGo website (yugo.mu) is operational with a professional interface. Social media presence is active across Facebook (yugo.mu), Instagram (yugomauritius), and LinkedIn (yugo-mauritius). However, the Google Play rating of 3.2 stars from 111 reviews — against 50K+ downloads — indicates a gap between reach and user satisfaction. Key complaints centre on payment processing friction (inability to save cards) and driver availability outside peak hours. These are operational and product execution issues, not market issues.

Charge Register

The CBRIS charge register is entirely clear. No floating charges, fixed charges, debentures, or secured lending facilities have been registered against YuGo Limited. This confirms the company has no external debt financing — funding has come exclusively through equity (share capital from Ker Ventures, Mo Angels Second Limited, and Media Alliance Ltd). While the absence of debt reduces financial risk in the traditional sense, it also means the company has not accessed bank credit lines, which is consistent with the financial profile of a loss-making startup without a clear path to near-term profitability. The zero-debt balance sheet means any future growth capital would need to come from: (a) a new equity round, (b) active group support from Aptis (Ker Ventures), or (c) revenue-based growth — none of which require debt financing but all of which require a deliberate strategic decision from the shareholder group.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Regulatory fortress: Mauritius's PSV licensing requirement for all transport-for-hire services has effectively blocked Uber, Bolt, and every other international ride-hailing platform from entering the market. YuGo has built its driver network exclusively around PSV-compliant vehicles — a 7-year head start that no new entrant can replicate overnight. This is not a moat that expires when regulation changes; it is embedded in the operating model.

2. "Made in Moris" brand and local trust: The Board of Investment's "Made in Moris" accreditation is a meaningful quality signal in the Mauritian consumer market. Combined with 7 years of operations, YuGo has brand recognition that a new entrant would require significant capital to build. The Mauritius market is small and relationship-driven — word-of-mouth and community trust compound over time.

3. Super-app infrastructure: The YuGo 2.0 expansion into food and grocery delivery means the platform is no longer a single-use-case app. Multi-service super-apps generate higher DAU (daily active users), more data, and stronger network effects. The Aptis Group's commercial relationships — particularly TNS Consumer and L'Artigiano — represent untapped distribution and delivery partnership opportunities that YuGo alone could not access.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Regulatory protection is durable: Mauritius transport regulation continues to require PSV licensing, and there is no current legislative signal toward liberalisation. The international platform ban is effectively permanent in the near-to-medium term.
  • Tourism recovery driving demand: Mauritius receives 1M+ tourists per year; taxi demand from airports, hotels, and tourist zones is structurally growing. YuGo is positioned as the digital-first option for this market segment.
  • Digital payment adoption accelerating: my.t money and mobile wallet penetration is increasing in Mauritius, reducing friction for cashless ride payments — directly supporting YuGo's revenue model.
  • Delivery economy normalisation: Post-pandemic food and grocery delivery habits have become structural in Mauritius urban areas. The market YuGo is entering with its delivery vertical is growing, not contracting.
  • Driver acquisition remains a constraint: PSV licensing requirements and the relatively small pool of licensed drivers limits how fast YuGo can scale its supply side. Competing for drivers with Espace-Taxi and other incumbents is an ongoing challenge.
  • App quality expectations rising: Mauritius consumers increasingly benchmark local apps against international standards (WhatsApp, Netflix, global e-commerce). YuGo's 3.2-star rating and payment UX issues are a risk as consumer expectations rise.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 6 / 10

YuGo is a small company — exact headcount is not public, but a company with MUR 1.49M in revenue and MUR 2.06M in admin costs likely employs between 8–15 people. This is at the lower end of the EOS sweet spot (10–250). The platform manages three service lines (taxi, full-day hire, delivery) with two operational apps (consumer and driver). This multi-product, multi-audience structure creates genuine coordination complexity even at small team size. The score of 6 reflects real complexity without being overpowering — EOS would not be over-engineered for this entity.

02 — Leadership Openness 7 / 10

Kaushik Rathod describes himself on LinkedIn as "an innovative thinker, dynamic and results-driven individual." He actively sought angel investment (Mo Angels), engaged with La Plage Factory as an incubator, and has built a diverse board including a former Arthur Andersen auditor and a Le Défi Media Group director. This pattern — deliberately building governance infrastructure around himself — signals openness to external frameworks, accountability, and advice. The co-founder pair (Kaushik and Evanee) appear to have a clear division of labour (commercial/technology vs. operations) which further supports leadership receptivity. No public signals of rigidity or resistance to external input.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

YuGo is experiencing the canonical EOS growth-stage pain: a clear vision that hasn't translated into financial sustainability, execution gaps visible in user reviews (payment friction, driver availability), and a cost structure that has outpaced revenue for seven years. The 3.2-star app rating points to execution and prioritisation problems — classic symptoms of a team managing by intuition rather than measurables and Rocks. The financial data confirms the urgency: with less than 7 months of cash runway, the pain is real and immediate. This is not a theoretical EOS case — the founders are actively living the execution gap every day.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

Positive signals: the company files annual returns and financial statements on time, uses Parker Russell as corporate secretary (professional governance infrastructure), and has maintained a functioning mobile platform with continuous updates across 7 years. These are not the actions of a completely unstructured entity. However, there is no public evidence of formal process documentation, an org chart, role clarity below director level, or structured operational rhythms. The app's UX issues (payment problems reported in reviews) suggest process gaps in product development cycles and user feedback loops.

05 — Company Culture 6 / 10

YuGo carries the "Made in Moris" label — a cultural marker of local pride and quality commitment. The founding pair have remained with the company for 7 years through significant financial difficulty, suggesting resilience and alignment to the mission. Mo Angels co-founder Isabelle de Melo joining the board in 2021 introduced a governance culture from a world-class professional background. However, the 3.2-star user rating and payment complaints suggest a culture that has not yet fully internalised user experience as a non-negotiable standard — there are accountability gaps in product quality management.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 5 / 10

As a founder-led company with two co-founders in the operational seats, decision-making is almost certainly concentrated. The board of four directors (Kaushik, Evanee, Isabelle, Reaz) provides a formal structure, but the day-to-day decision authority likely remains entirely with the Rathod pair. This creates the classic founder bottleneck: decisions are fast but capacity-limited. The fact that seven years of investment has not yet produced a breakeven business suggests some strategic decisions (on pricing, cost structure, or growth focus) have not been made with sufficient rigor. EOS's Issues List and IDS (Identify, Discuss, Solve) process would directly address this.

07 — Vision Clarity 7 / 10

The vision for YuGo is publicly articulated and coherent: become the dominant all-in-one mobility and delivery super-app for Mauritius. The super-app evolution (YuGo 2.0) demonstrates strategic thinking about where the business is going beyond taxi rides. The "Made in Moris" positioning and focus on regulatory compliance show clarity about the brand's market differentiation. Highest-Priority EOS Tools for YuGo

Accountability Chart

YuGo has two founders doing everything. The Accountability Chart will force clarity on who owns technology, operations, commercial, and finance — separating roles that are currently blurred. With Isabelle de Melo and Reaz Emambocus on the board, there is sufficient leadership depth to build a real structure. Scorecard & Measurables

This is tool #2 because it turns the financial alarm into an early-warning system.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

Without Rocks, the team operates in permanent firefighting mode — exactly the pattern that has kept the company loss-making for seven years.

Issues List & IDS

The 3.2-star app rating and recurring payment complaints are issues that have clearly not been resolved in a structured way. The Issues List with Identify-Discuss-Solve would force the founding team to surface, prioritise, and close problems rather than living with them. The payment friction issue alone — fixing it could improve the app rating and reduce churn significantly. This is a measurable, solvable problem that IDS would have closed 18 months ago.

Vision/Traction Organiser (V/TO)

YuGo's super-app vision needs to be documented. The V/TO would force Oscar Mamet, Kaushik Rathod, and the board to agree on the 10-year target, the 3-year picture, and the 1-year plan — and to surface any disagreements between shareholders about the direction of the company. Given the Aptis group connection and the Mo Angels investment horizon, a documented V/TO is the essential alignment tool before any growth capital is deployed.

Level 10 Meeting

YuGo almost certainly has no weekly structured management meeting. The Level 10 Meeting — 90 minutes, fixed agenda, Scorecard review, Rock update, Issues List — would transform the operational rhythm from reactive to proactive. For a small team under financial pressure, this structure provides both discipline and psychological safety: problems are surfaced weekly rather than exploding quarterly. This is the single most implementable EOS tool for a company at YuGo's stage.

The Bottom Line

YuGo Limited is a real business with a proven product, a protected market, and a financially sophisticated board — running out of time. The EOS opportunity is genuine but contingent: the first conversation is not about methodology — it is about whether the Aptis Group sees YuGo as a strategic asset worth funding to profitability, or a portfolio investment approaching its write-down horizon. If Oscar Mamet says yes, YuGo is an excellent EOS engagement.
