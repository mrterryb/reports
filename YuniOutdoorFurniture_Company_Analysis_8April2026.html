Executive Summary

A 30-year-old, founder-built Mauritian furniture institution now in first-generation succession — profitable on paper, but haemorrhaging at the bottom line through administrative cost structures that no longer fit a MUR 100M+ business.

Yuni (legal name: Outdoor Furniture Ltd, File No. C15937) was incorporated on 1 February 1996 and trades under the brand name "Yuni — Furniture & Lifestyle." Founded by Pascal Bauwens, the company began in 2004 as a Balinese furniture shop in Pointe aux Canonniers and has since grown into one of Mauritius's most recognised premium furniture and lifestyle brands. With two retail showrooms — the flagship 1,000 m² showroom at Pinpoint Business Park (Calebasses) and a second location at MDC Building, St Joseph Road (Terre Rouge) — and manufacturing partnerships in Indonesia, Yuni occupies the upper end of the local furniture market for both residential and hospitality clients.

The company is 100% family-owned. Founder Pascal Bauwens has stepped back from daily operations; his two sons — Kevin Bauwens (Managing Director) and Bryce Bauwens (Director) — now lead the business alongside Sharon Virassamy, who holds a 33.9% shareholder stake and serves as a third director. There is no institutional or private equity involvement. The share structure (7,000 ordinary shares at MUR 100 par) reflects a closely held, deeply personal business with no external accountability mechanism. This is both a risk and an opportunity for an EOS engagement.

For the financial year ended 31 August 2025, Yuni posted revenue of MUR 103.8 million — a milestone crossing the MUR 100M mark — with a healthy gross profit margin of 36.6% (MUR 38.0M). However, administration costs of MUR 33.9 million consumed 89% of that gross profit, leaving net profit of just MUR 1.0 million (1.0% net margin). This is the defining financial tension: the revenue engine is performing well; the cost structure is not keeping pace. The balance sheet shows no long-term debt, solid retained earnings of MUR 27.2M, but dangerously low cash (MUR 1.5M) and a heavy inventory position (MUR 45.3M), indicating a cash-constrained, inventory-rich business where working capital discipline is the priority.

Key Finding — The Admin Cost Crisis Hiding Inside a Strong Brand
Yuni presents the surface profile of a premium brand success story: MUR 103M in revenue, a 36.6% gross margin, prestigious exclusive distributor agreements (Manutti, Cane-line, Maiori, Jati & Kebon), two showrooms, and a well-recognised lifestyle brand. But below the gross profit line, the numbers tell a different story. Administration costs of MUR 33.97M — representing 32.7% of turnover — are structurally suppressing net profitability to just 1.0%. The company has crossed the MUR 100M revenue threshold without building the management systems, cost discipline, or financial visibility needed to operate at that scale. This is a textbook EOS symptoms profile: a founder-era cost structure inherited by next-generation leaders who have the ambition but not yet the execution infrastructure. The entry conversation should not lead with EOS — it should lead with the profitability gap. EOS is the answer to why it exists.

Products and Services Portfolio

Yuni operates across four product/service clusters: (1) Premium Outdoor Furniture — the founding category, encompassing Balinese-style teak and rattan outdoor sets, poolside loungers, dining collections, and daybeds, serving both residential and hotel clients across Mauritius and the Indian Ocean region; (2) Indoor Furniture & Accessories — a broad lifestyle range covering sofas, dining tables, bedroom furniture, decorative accessories, and textiles, sold through the Calebasses showroom; (3) Exclusive Brand Representation — Yuni is the sole authorised distributor in Mauritius for Manutti (Belgium, high-end outdoor), Cane-line (Denmark, design outdoor/indoor), Maiori (France, luxury outdoor lighting and furniture), and Jati & Kebon (premium teak collections); and (4) B2B / Hospitality Project Supply — bespoke furniture packages for hotel and resort developments, including design advisory, technical specification, production scheduling, quality control in Indonesia, and logistics to site. The company has manufacturing workshops both in Indonesia (Bali) and in Mauritius (employing fewer than 10 persons under CBRIS classification).

Geographic Presence

Primary retail presence is through two showrooms in Mauritius: the flagship location at Pinpoint Business Park (Cnr M2–M3 Motorway, Calebasses, Pamplemousses) — a purpose-built 1,000 m² showroom — and the original Terre Rouge location (MDC Building, St Joseph Road). B2B and hospitality supply extends across the Indian Ocean region, with documented sales reach into Seychelles and the Maldives. Manufacturing and sourcing is anchored in Bali, Indonesia, via a dedicated sourcing office and production partnerships that have been in place since at least 2008. The company's website (yuni.mu) serves as a product catalogue and brand presence; e-commerce capability is limited — the site does not appear to offer full online transactional purchasing.

The 2 Numbers That Matter Most
1. Administration Costs (MUR 33.97M / 32.7% of revenue): This single line is eating 89% of gross profit. If administration costs were brought to a more typical 20–22% of revenue — still generous for a two-location lifestyle retailer — net profit would increase from MUR 1.0M to approximately MUR 12–14M. That is a 12–14× improvement in profitability from cost discipline alone, without adding a single rupee of revenue. This is where the business wins or loses.

2. Inventory Days (251 days / MUR 45.3M): Reducing inventory to a more reasonable 150 days would free approximately MUR 18.5M in cash. This alone would transform the liquidity position and eliminate the dangerous DPO of 201 days — allowing the company to negotiate better supplier terms, protect its exclusive brand relationships, and rebuild a genuine cash buffer.

Structural Competitive Advantages

1. Exclusive Distribution Rights: The sole Mauritius distribution agreements for Manutti, Cane-line, Maiori, and Jati & Kebon represent genuine barriers to competitive entry. A competitor cannot simply import and sell these products — they must displace Yuni in the brand's view, which requires demonstrated market capability. These relationships, built over 10–20 years, are the single most strategically valuable asset in the business and must be protected above all else, including the payment terms behaviour noted in the DPO analysis.

2. Integrated Indonesian Manufacturing Capability: Yuni's Bali sourcing office and production partnerships give it cost and customisation advantages over pure importers. The ability to specify custom furniture, manage production quality, and control lead times from the source provides a genuine B2B differentiation that no pure-play local retailer can replicate quickly. This is particularly relevant for hospitality project work where bespoke specification is the norm.

3. Brand Equity and Physical Showroom Footprint: The 1,000 m² Calebasses showroom at Pinpoint Business Park (a premium commercial address on the M2–M3 motorway) functions as a marketing asset as much as a retail space. In the premium furniture segment, the physical experience of sitting in the product, feeling the quality, and receiving professional design advice cannot be replicated online. This creates switching friction for loyal customers and a discovery pipeline for new ones.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Mauritius real estate and resort development pipeline remains active — ongoing hotel refurbishments and new resort developments (particularly in the north and west of the island) continue to generate FF&E project demand for premium suppliers like Yuni.
  • Indian Ocean luxury hospitality growth — Seychelles, Maldives, and Réunion are experiencing sustained growth in upscale resort development, expanding Yuni's addressable B2B market beyond Mauritius.
  • Premium outdoor living trend accelerated post-COVID — residential demand for high-quality outdoor furniture has grown structurally as households invest in outdoor living spaces. Yuni's core outdoor product range is well-positioned to capture this.
  • Headwind: Indonesian Rupiah and EUR/MUR FX volatility — imported product costs from Bali and European brands are denominated in USD/EUR, creating FX exposure that compresses margins when the Mauritius Rupee weakens. No hedging strategy is evident from public data.
  • Headwind: Rising showroom and logistics costs in Mauritius — commercial lease rates at premium locations (Calebasses) have increased materially since 2022. This likely contributes to the elevated administration cost base.
  • Risk: Online furniture retail disruption — while premium furniture has so far resisted full online commoditisation, the growth of global e-commerce platforms reaching Mauritius (Wayfair, made.com alumni, international luxury platforms) creates a medium-term channel threat, particularly for the residential retail segment.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 8 / 10

MUR 103.8M revenue with two physical showrooms, a Bali sourcing and manufacturing operation, B2B hospitality project work across multiple islands, and three distinct business segments (retail, project, distribution) represents substantial operational complexity for an owner-managed company. The company registers under two separate business activity codes and has multiple registered places of business — all indicators of a business that has outgrown informal coordination. Size and complexity score is high; EOS is designed for exactly this profile.

02 — Leadership Openness 7 / 10

Kevin and Bryce Bauwens represent second-generation leadership — they did not found the company, which typically means they are more open to external frameworks than first-generation founders who built the business through intuition. Both are in their early-to-mid career stage (directors since 2000–2002, so approximately 40–50 years old). The fact that the business has reached MUR 100M with a 1% net margin suggests they are likely feeling the strain of running an increasingly complex business without formal management infrastructure. This pain receptivity is the key openness indicator. No public statements suggest resistance to change or entrenched fixed views on management philosophy.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 8 / 10

Classic "hitting the ceiling" profile. Revenue has grown to MUR 100M+ but net profitability has flatlined at 1%. The business is more complex than it has ever been — multiple locations, international manufacturing, B2B and B2C revenue streams, three owner-directors — without the management systems to support that complexity. The DPO of 201 days and cash position of MUR 1.5M suggest cash pressure that leadership will be experiencing viscerally. These are precisely the conditions EOS was designed to address: growth that has outpaced the informal structures that got the business here.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

No evidence of formal documented systems from public sources. The business has been operating across two locations, an Indonesian supply chain, and a B2B project pipeline without any visible process documentation or published operational playbooks. Annual returns and financial statements are filed on time (a positive compliance discipline indicator), and the auditor change in 2023 suggests a willingness to upgrade financial governance. However, the inventory management crisis (251 DIH) and supplier payment patterns (201 DPO) are consistent with informal, reactive procurement and cash management rather than system-driven processes. Score reflects a probable mix of some financial controls but limited operational process documentation.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

Business Magazine coverage describes Yuni as a company with strong identity, heritage, and aesthetic values — precisely the kind of "we know who we are" cultural foundation that EOS can build on. The brand story (Balinese authenticity, Mauritian lifestyle elevation, trusted family business) suggests a culture with genuine values that have never been formally articulated. Family-led businesses with strong brand identity tend to respond very well to the V/TO exercise and core values definition. The three-way ownership between two brothers and a long-term trusted partner (20 years together) also suggests a cohesive leadership culture if managed carefully.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 6 / 10

Three shareholders with near-parity ownership (33%/33%/34%) and no declared shareholder agreement creates a decision-making environment that can be paralysed by the need for consensus. With no formal Accountability Chart, decisions about purchasing, hiring, pricing, and capital allocation are likely made informally — either by the MD alone (Kevin) or by committee discussion. The absence of a formal governance structure is a moderate risk factor for EOS adoption (alignment meetings will be essential) but also represents the clearest immediate value EOS can deliver: decisional clarity through the Accountability Chart and L10 cadence.

07 — Vision Clarity 5 / 10

The brand vision ("premium Balinese-inspired lifestyle furniture for Mauritius and the Indian Ocean") is clear at the product and marketing level. But at the business strategy level — where is Yuni in five years? Are they expanding to a third showroom? Building an e-commerce capability? Growing the Indonesian manufacturing side? Pursuing further exclusive brand agreements? None of this is publicly articulated. Business Magazine articles discuss history and products, not strategic direction. This is a mid-score: the company knows what it is but has not clearly defined where it is going or what trade-offs it is prepared to make.

08 — Meeting Culture 5 / 10

No evidence of formal meeting cadence or structured management reviews from public sources. In a three-director, family-run business of this type, "meetings" tend to happen informally — in the showroom, during family interactions, on WhatsApp. The operational complexity (two locations, Indonesia sourcing, B2B projects, retail) demands a structured weekly review discipline that almost certainly does not yet exist in formal form. Accountability Chart

Three directors, two brothers, a trusted non-family partner — and no publicly declared role clarity. Who owns purchasing decisions? Who owns the Indonesian operation? Scorecard & Measurables

The V/TO exercise will create the shared strategic clarity that currently does not exist in documented form.

Rocks — Quarterly Priorities

Specific to Yuni's situation: the first quarter's Rocks should be (1) reduce inventory below MUR 30M, (2) reduce DPO below 90 days, (3) map all administration costs to owner. These three Rocks alone, if achieved, would transform both the balance sheet and the P&L. Rocks create the focused execution cadence that prevents leadership from working in the business instead of on it.

Issues List & IDS

The DPO of 201 days is a company-threatening issue that has persisted long enough to be structural. The administration cost bleed is another. In a family business, issues like these often go unnamed because raising them feels like criticism of a person rather than a system. The Issues List makes it safe to name and solve problems systematically — this is culturally critical for Yuni's three-director structure.

Level 10 Meeting

Replacing ad-hoc showroom conversations and WhatsApp discussions with a structured weekly 90-minute L10 meeting will create the accountability rhythm Yuni currently lacks. With Kevin in operations, Bryce in commercial/brand, and Sharon in her functional role, the L10 will be the first time all three are reviewing the same scorecard numbers at the same time in a structured format — a transformative shift for a business of this complexity.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Conduct a Full Administration Cost Audit and Set a 20% Cap

Administration costs of MUR 33.97M (32.7% of revenue) are destroying profitability. Kevin Bauwens should commission a line-by-line breakdown of this cost block — personnel by function, occupancy, depreciation, management fees, marketing, and other overheads — within 30 days. The target: reduce admin costs to 20% of revenue (MUR 20.8M), freeing approximately MUR 13M of profit. Success looks like: net profit of MUR 12–14M on current revenue, representing a 12× improvement without a single additional rupee of sales.

Recommendation 02

Inventory at MUR 45.3M (251 days) is the largest single drag on both liquidity and asset efficiency. Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

Yuni is a genuinely excellent business masquerading as a mediocre one. The brand, the exclusive distribution rights, the manufacturing capability, the showroom, and the 30-year heritage are all real, defensible, and valuable. But the company is earning only MUR 1M net profit on MUR 103M revenue because it has grown past the management capacity of its founders without building replacement infrastructure. The leadership is willing and capable — they simply have not yet been given the tools to run a MUR 100M business like one. That is the opportunity. EOS, entered through the profitability conversation, is a high-probability engagement for a Qualified conversation outcome.
