Executive Summary

A 16-year-old Italian gourmet import and retail business, acquired and built from 4 employees to 24 by founder-MD Daniela Quaranta, now entering a franchise-driven expansion phase with Aptis Holdings as a 50% strategic shareholder — a genuine founder-led growth business with strong commercial character and real EOS potential.

L'artigiano Ltd (formerly Bokia Ltd) was incorporated on 18 March 2009 and has operated from Royal Road, Triolet, Mauritius since its 2013 relocation and relaunch under Marchetto Silvano and Daniela Quaranta. The business curates and distributes premium Italian food products — artisanal cheeses, cured meats, fine wines, and gourmet pantry staples — sourced directly from hand-picked Italian producers. With approximately 1,300 product lines and 24 employees, it is one of the most established Italian specialty importers in Mauritius. Physical presence includes the Triolet warehouse/headquarters and a retail outlet at Mont Choisy Le Mall, opened in 2018.

Ownership is shared 50/50 between M&D Gestion and Investment Ltd (the vehicle of founders Marchetto Silvano and Daniela Quaranta, holding 100 shares) and APTIS Holdings Ltd (100 shares, invested October 2022). Jean-Pierre Mamet joined as Director on 1 November 2022, representing Aptis's strategic interest. This dual-ownership structure — a founder-led MD alongside a strategic group partner — is the defining governance dynamic. SIMO Corporate Services Ltd remains as company secretary (appointed 15 March 2013), with no change to secretarial arrangements post-Aptis investment.

Financially, FY2024 (year ended 31 December 2024) shows the company in profit: revenue of MUR 170,406 (or MUR 170M depending on unit interpretation — see Tab 03), net profit of MUR 109,192 (or MUR 109M). Retained earnings of MUR 116,223 (or MUR 116M) represent robust accumulated profitability. The balance sheet is healthy: no long-term borrowings, positive equity of MUR 116,243 (or MUR 116M), and total assets of MUR 144,392 (or MUR 144M). The franchise launch in January 2025 signals the company's entry into a new growth phase. EOS suitability scores 6.4/10 — a strong candidate for EOS engagement, with Daniela Quaranta as the natural leadership conversation partner.

Important — Financial Unit Interpretation
The CBRIS summary for L'Artigiano reports figures that appear in small absolute numbers: Turnover MUR 170,406; Net Profit MUR 109,192; Total Assets MUR 144,392. In the context of a 24-employee retail/import business with 1,300 product lines, two physical locations, and an active franchise launch, these figures almost certainly represent MUR thousands — i.e., actual revenue of approximately MUR 170 million, net profit of MUR 109 million, and total assets of MUR 144 million. However, since the CBRIS format does not explicitly state the unit, this analysis presents figures as filed and notes that ratios should be interpreted with this context. If figures are indeed in MUR thousands, the financial performance is exceptional. If they are literal MUR amounts (c.USD 3,800 revenue), the company's CBRIS filing is materially incomplete. We proceed on the MUR '000 interpretation as the only commercially logical reading.

Structural Competitive Advantages

Depth and curation of the Italian product range: 1,300 SKUs of authentic Italian gourmet products, sourced directly from Italian producers, is an almost impossible-to-replicate position for a Mauritius newcomer. The relationships with Italian producers built over 16 years constitute a genuine competitive moat. No supermarket chain or general importer can match this specialisation.

Brand authenticity and community: L'Artigiano has built a loyal following among the Mauritian Italian diaspora, expatriate community, and premium-seeking local consumers. The brand stands for Italian authenticity in a market where Italian cuisine is both popular and aspirational. This brand equity cannot be purchased — it is accumulated over years of consistent product quality and service.

Aptis Group resources: Since October 2022, L'Artigiano has access to Aptis Group's logistics infrastructure, commercial expertise, capital, and management support — without surrendering operational control (Daniela Quaranta remains MD with 50% ownership). This is a strategically advantageous arrangement for a growing SME.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Premiumisation of food consumption in Mauritius — rising disposable incomes among upper-middle-class Mauritians is driving sustained demand for imported European gourmet products, directly benefiting L'Artigiano's positioning.
  • Tourism growth — Mauritius's strong luxury tourism sector (European visitors, particularly Italian, French, and British) creates consistent demand for authentic Italian food products in hotel and restaurant channels.
  • Italian cuisine popularity — Italian food consistently ranks among the most popular global cuisines; the Mauritius restaurant scene features numerous Italian restaurants providing B2B wholesale demand.
  • Franchise model scalability — the January 2025 Moka franchise represents a capital-light growth model that allows L'Artigiano to expand geographic reach without proportional management bandwidth or capex investment.
  • Perishable product supply chain risk — cold chain management, customs delays, and import duty changes create ongoing operational risk for a business dependent on fresh Italian dairy and meat products.
  • Supermarket imported food competition — Winner's, Jumbo, and other chains have expanded their imported food sections; while these cannot match L'Artigiano's depth or authenticity, they compete for the casual gourmet shopper.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 7 / 10

24 employees across 2 owned locations (Triolet, Mont Choisy Le Mall), plus a franchise partner in Moka, plus wholesale distribution to hotels and restaurants — this is squarely in the EOS sweet spot of 10–250 with genuine operational complexity. Multiple business lines (retail, wholesale, import, food processing, franchise) create coordination challenges that EOS systems directly address. With the franchise network potentially growing, complexity will increase materially over the next 2–3 years.

02 — Leadership Openness 7 / 10

Daniela Quaranta's decision to bring Aptis Holdings in as a 50% strategic shareholder in October 2022 — giving up half the equity in exchange for resources and partnerships — is a strong signal of leadership openness to external expertise and collaboration. This is not a founder who clings to 100% control for control's sake. The franchise launch (January 2025) further demonstrates willingness to adapt the business model. Quaranta appears to be a growth-oriented, externally curious leader — exactly the profile for EOS receptivity.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 7 / 10

L'Artigiano is in active, managed growth: franchise launch, Aptis partnership, expanded product range to 1,300 SKUs. Classic EOS growth pain points are almost certainly present: Quaranta likely holds significant institutional knowledge personally (supplier relationships, product curation expertise, customer relationships); replicating this knowledge for franchise partners requires documented processes; coordinating 24 employees across multiple sites requires structured accountability. The franchise launch is the inflection point that makes EOS timing ideal — now, before the franchise network scales to 5–10 outlets.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

L'Artigiano has been operating for 16 years and has developed operational systems for Italian import, cold chain management, and retail. However, the franchise model demands a new level of process documentation — franchisees need replicable playbooks for product selection, ordering, display, pricing, and customer service. No evidence of documented EOS-style operating rhythms (weekly L10s, quarterly Rocks, formal Scorecard). The appointment of an Operations Manager (Jean-Yves Allas) and Financial Accountant (Shivesh Seepal) suggests functional role clarity, but inter-functional coordination systems are unknown.

05 — Company Culture 7 / 10

A business that has grown from 4 to 24 employees over 13 years under one founder-MD, with consistent profitability and zero staff turnover signal (inferred from company's stable public presence), projects a culture of quality, discipline, and customer service. The curated Italian product ethos — authenticity, craft, care — likely permeates the internal culture. This is a business where employees understand the brand mission. High scores on culture correlate with strong EOS adoption success.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 5 / 10

With Daniela Quaranta as both Director and MD, decision-making is likely highly centralised — which is appropriate for a 24-person business but will become a bottleneck as the franchise network grows. The 50/50 board structure with Jean-Pierre Mamet (Aptis) could slow major strategic decisions if alignment takes time. No evidence of delegated decision-making frameworks, documented authority levels, or formal leadership team structure below Quaranta. This is the area EOS would most immediately improve.

07 — Vision Clarity 7 / 10

The franchise launch and Aptis partnership suggest a clearly articulated growth vision: become the definitive Italian gourmet brand across Mauritius through a franchise network. The founding story, brand identity, and product curation philosophy are coherent and consistent. What is less clear — and where V/TO work would add value — is the precise 10-year target (how many franchise outlets? what revenue? export to Africa via Navitas channels?). The vision exists but needs the rigour that V/TO provides to become a fully operational roadmap.

08 — Meeting Culture 4 / 10

No evidence of structured meeting cadences. A 24-person business with the founder as MD likely operates with informal communication norms — ad hoc conversations, reactive problem-solving, founder-centric decision relays. As the franchise network grows and remote locations require management without physical presence, this model will break. Weekly L10 Meetings at Triolet HQ and a quarterly planning rhythm are precisely what the next growth phase demands.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for L'Artigiano

V/TO (Vision/Traction Organizer)

Quaranta has a growth vision — but it needs to be written down and shared with the entire leadership team, including the Aptis board representative. How many franchise outlets by 2030? What revenue? Which markets? V/TO would make the implicit vision explicit, create alignment between founders and Aptis, and give every team member a North Star to execute against.

Accountability Chart

As the company grows from 24 to 40+ employees across franchise and company-owned locations, role clarity becomes critical. Who owns the franchise relationship? Who owns product procurement from Italy? Who owns the hotel/restaurant wholesale channel? An Accountability Chart would clarify these ownership questions before the franchise network creates coordination chaos.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

The franchise launch is the company's most important current initiative. A first Rock should be: "Franchise #1 (Tutto Bene Ltd, Moka) achieving MUR [X] monthly revenue by end of Q3 2026." Rocks would focus the leadership team's energy on the 3–5 things that matter most each quarter, preventing dilution of attention across 1,300 SKUs and multiple channels.

Process Documentation (Franchise Playbook)

Scaling a franchise network requires that L'Artigiano's operational model — product selection, ordering, display, customer service standards, cold chain protocols — be fully documented in a Franchise Operations Manual. The EOS emphasis on "documenting and optimising" processes directly addresses this need. Without documented processes, each new franchisee will operate inconsistently, diluting the brand.

Level 10 Meeting Cadence

A weekly 90-minute L10 Meeting for L'Artigiano's leadership team (Quaranta, Operations Manager, Financial Accountant, Sales Supervisor) would create the rhythmic review that a multi-site, franchising business demands. The meeting would track the Scorecard, review Rock progress, and surface and resolve the Issues preventing franchise and wholesale growth.

Scorecard (Weekly Numbers)

Key metrics for L'Artigiano's Scorecard: weekly revenue by channel (Triolet retail, Mont Choisy retail, wholesale, franchise), gross margin by SKU category, inventory days (perishable risk), DSO (hotel/restaurant receivables), franchise outlet sales vs. target, and new SKU additions from Italian producer relationships. Weekly visibility on these numbers is the foundation for disciplined growth management.
