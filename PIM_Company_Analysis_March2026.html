Executive Summary

A 55-year-old listed plastics manufacturer with real scale, credible governance and a sustainability story, but still carrying the classic mid-market tension of thin profits, heavy inventory and a balance sheet that does not leave much room for sloppy execution.

PIM Limited is a long-established Mauritian plastics manufacturer incorporated on 1 June 1970 and listed on the Stock Exchange of Mauritius since 1993. It manufactures a broad range of industrial and household plastic products serving local and regional markets, and it now pairs the legacy manufacturing platform with a newer circular-economy proposition through PIM Recycling Ltd.

Strategically, the company sits in a useful place: established factory base, recognised certifications, large product breadth, and a controlling relationship with Mauritius Oil Refineries in practice. That gives it more institutional depth than the average SME. It also creates the usual group-dynamics question: how much of the business is run with crisp operating accountability versus board-level oversight and legacy habits.

Financially, FY2025 was profitable but not comfortably so. Revenue was Rs 340,931,196 and net profit after tax just Rs 5,994,298, a net margin of 1.8%. Liquidity is the pressure point: current ratio is only 1.23×, quick ratio 0.47×, and inventory alone absorbs Rs 76,313,049. That is exactly the kind of operational drag EOS can expose and attack fast, provided leadership is willing to translate broad intent into measurable weekly discipline.

Key Finding — Operationally Serious, But Margin-Thin
PIM does not look like a broken company. It looks like a serious mid-market operator that has already built the basics — manufacturing footprint, governance scaffolding, product range, certifications, export capability and a sustainability narrative. The real issue is that the economics are still tighter than the strategic ambition. That mismatch is where an EOS conversation gets interesting: not “do you need management 101?” but “how do you convert scale, complexity and initiatives into cleaner accountability, faster decisions, tighter inventory control and more profit?”

At a Glance

Commercial Strengths

Broad manufacturing capability, long market presence, local-plus-regional reach, recognised certifications, Coca-Cola supplier status, and a credible recycling angle that can differentiate the company in tenders and strategic accounts.

Financial Vulnerabilities

Liquidity is the weak spot. Quick assets are thin, cash is low, working capital is tied up in stock, and FY2025 profit is positive but too modest relative to the asset base and operating complexity.

Company Overview

An established Mauritian plastics platform evolving from legacy manufacturing into a broader packaging and circular-economy operator.

Founding and Corporate Record

PIM Limited was incorporated on 1 June 1970 under the former name Plastic Industry (Mauritius) Ltd. CBRIS shows file number C1808, BRN C06001808, status Live, and registered office at Level 3, Alexander House, 35 Cybercity, Ebene. Stated capital is Rs 20,000,000. Secretary: OneLink Ltd. Auditor: Deloitte Solutions Ltd.

The head office and factory are at Quay Road, Port Louis, with additional warehouse activity at Muslim Cemetery Road, Riche Terre. The business has long-standing export and industrial development certificates and has maintained annual return filings for the last three years. The company rebranded to PIM Limited in 2020, shifting from a purely industrial identity to a more modern, sustainability-aware positioning.

Products and Services Portfolio

PIM manufactures across injection, blow moulding and extrusion. The product catalogue is broad: household items, jars, bottles, jerrycans, caps, containers, buckets, crates, dustbins, flower pots, stools and chairs. End-markets include chemical, detergent, paint, beverages, food, pharmaceutical, cosmetics, industrial and environmental applications. Sales analysis in the 2025 annual report splits revenue mainly between industrial products and a smaller household consumables segment.

Geographic Presence

The operational footprint is clearly Mauritian at plant level, but the commercial footprint is wider. PIM serves the local market and exports into regional Indian Ocean markets. FY2025 export sales were much smaller than local sales, however, which suggests the export story remains strategically important but not yet dominant. The newer recycling entity at Riche Terre adds a second operational node and extends the footprint from manufacturing into waste collection and valorisation.

Digital Presence Note
PIM’s website is active, informative and more polished than the average Mauritian industrial SME. That is good. The weak spot is not presence but conversion: there is limited public evidence of a sharp commercial message around sector priorities, account focus or a clear “why choose us” narrative beyond heritage, breadth and sustainability.

Structural Competitive Advantages

The first real advantage is manufacturing breadth. PIM is not a one-trick moulding shop; it covers multiple product families and process types, which matters in a small island market where customers like consolidation. The second is institutional credibility: listed status, board structure, ISO/HACCP/Coca-Cola credentials and long operating history reduce perceived supplier risk. The third is that the recycling activity gives PIM a narrative many competitors cannot yet match credibly — not just “we sell plastic,” but “we can help you use, recover and re-enter plastic more responsibly.”

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Demand for reliable local packaging remains resilient because import substitution and supply-chain risk still matter in island economies.
  • Environmental scrutiny of plastics is rising, which hurts undifferentiated players but can favour manufacturers with recycling capability and certification.
  • Export markets are attractive, but they are lumpy and harder to win consistently; PIM’s own FY2025 export sales dipped sharply.
  • Customers increasingly expect both compliance and sustainability, which plays to PIM’s strengths if commercial teams monetise it properly.
  • Commodity, resin and energy cost volatility can savage already-thin margins, especially when pricing discipline is weak.
  • Operational complexity grows fast when a manufacturer adds sustainability projects, public visibility and a new subsidiary without an equally strong management system.
Competitive Gap
PIM’s public message says “broad range” and “quality” very well. It says “why a serious B2B account should choose us over the next capable local plastics supplier” less sharply. That is a positioning problem, not a product problem.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity8 / 10

At about 165 employees, with a listed-company governance overlay, a factory, a warehouse, a recycling subsidiary and both local and export activity, PIM is squarely in the zone where informal management becomes expensive.

02 — Leadership Openness6 / 10

There are positive signs: governance committees, quarterly board meetings, public reporting and strategic initiatives such as recycling. But listed-company governance is not the same thing as genuine operating-system openness. The gating question is whether leaders want more transparency once the numbers become uncomfortable.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points8 / 10

This is classic EOS territory: legacy business, newer strategic initiative, margin pressure, working-capital drag, export ambitions and the need to convert “many moving parts” into fewer real priorities.

04 — Current Systems and Processes6 / 10

PIM clearly has systems; this is not chaos. But the financials suggest that existing systems are not yet producing enough speed, simplicity or margin protection. In EOS terms, structure probably exists, traction probably leaks.

05 — Company Culture6 / 10

There are good cultural signals: longevity, safety language, certifications, unionised employees and sustainability messaging. But none of that proves execution accountability. The real test is whether underperformance gets named and solved cleanly.

06 — Decision-Making Patterns6 / 10

The governance structure is clear at board level, but operational ownership is less visible publicly. A managing director exists, yet group ties and board presence may still blur where final operating accountability truly sits.

07 — Vision Clarity7 / 10

Publicly, the company has a coherent strategic arc: heritage manufacturing plus circularity, recycling and smarter plastics. That is more vision than most. The missing piece is ruthless narrowing into a few company priorities that hit profit and cash, not just reputation.

08 — Meeting Culture7 / 10

Board meetings occur quarterly with agendas and committee structures. That is a better starting point than most SMEs. Still, board rhythm is not the same as an operational Level 10 cadence. EOS would need to build that layer beneath governance.

EOS Suitability

Overall Rating: Moderate-High — Qualified Positive

PIM looks like a very plausible EOS candidate. It has enough complexity, enough management drag and enough strategic ambition to benefit meaningfully. The gating factor is not need; it is willingness. If leadership wants a sharper operating machine, EOS fits. If they only want better-looking governance language, it won’t.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for PIM Limited

Scorecard & Measurables

PIM needs a weekly leadership scorecard around stock days, production efficiency, scrap, contribution by segment, overdue receivables, export pipeline conversion and cash. Right now the financials tell the story too late.

Accountability Chart

The business needs clarity between board governance, MD control, plant leadership, supply chain, sales/export and recycling. This is exactly where role fog quietly kills profit.

Rocks

PIM should force the strategy into 90-day priorities: inventory reduction, export recovery, recycling commercialisation and overhead clean-up. Not 19 initiatives. A handful.

V/TO

The company already has a public narrative. EOS would force leadership to turn that narrative into a real target market, uniques, 3-year picture and one-year plan that can actually be executed.

Issues List & IDS

PIM likely has hidden operational friction buried under board papers and departmental conversations. IDS would drag those issues into the open and stop them breeding in the dark.

Level 10 Meeting

This is the missing bridge between quarterly governance and weekly execution. Without it, the company risks managing through after-the-fact financials and fragmented departmental updates.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations: four financial/operational first, then four EOS-specific. Sequence matters. Fix immediate cash visibility and execution control first, then install the wider operating system.

Financial and Operational Recommendations

Recommendation 01

Launch a 13-week cash and working-capital war room

The MD, finance manager and operations lead should start a rolling 13-week cash forecast immediately. Success means weekly visibility on collections, supplier payments, inventory purchases and production cash burn. The aim is simple: no surprises and no lazy stock build.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 02

Attack inventory by category, not in aggregate

Inventory at Rs 76,313,049 is too heavy to leave as one giant number. Break it into fast-moving, strategic buffer, obsolete and low-rotation stock. Assign one accountable owner. Success means a measurable cash release within 90 days without service collapse.

Immediate Priority — Before EOS
Recommendation 03

Rebuild margin visibility by customer / product family

PIM should stop judging the business at total-company gross margin only. Finance and sales need a simple contribution view by segment, account and product family. Success means knowing which products and customers are carrying the business and which are freeloading.

Within 30–60 Days
Recommendation 04

Decode the “other current liabilities” line fully

The Rs 42,889,968 sitting in other current liabilities is too large to ignore. Finance should unpack it into operationally meaningful categories and link each to an owner and a due-date profile. Success means no mystery liabilities sitting outside leadership attention.

Within 30–60 Days

EOS Implementation Recommendations

Recommendation 05

Run a Focus Day with the real operating leadership team

Do not make this a board-seminar vanity exercise. Bring the MD, finance, operations, supply chain, sales/export and recycling leadership together. The output should be an honest diagnosis of the three biggest constraints hurting profit and cash right now.

Within 30–60 Days
Recommendation 06

Build an Accountability Chart hypothesis before touching organisation design

Map the seats first: Visionary/board role, Integrator/MD role, Sales, Operations, Supply Chain, Finance, Quality, Recycling. Success means each major result has a clear seat, not a committee blur.

Within 90 Days / During EOS
Recommendation 07

Make the first Rock a working-capital Rock, not a fluffy strategic one

The first 90-day Rock should be something like “Release Rs 10m–15m of working capital through stock and collections discipline while maintaining service levels.” That creates immediate proof EOS is not just management theatre.

Within 90 Days / During EOS
Recommendation 08

Use the V/TO to answer one brutal strategic question

What business is PIM really trying to become over the next three years: broad plastics manufacturer, higher-value packaging specialist, or circular solutions platform with manufacturing attached? Until leadership answers that cleanly, priorities will keep multiplying.

Within 90 Days / During EOS

Overall Assessment — Conclusion

The Bottom Line

PIM is a credible, serious industrial company — not a basket case and not a miracle either. The opportunity is obvious: a decent commercial platform, real assets, credible governance and a sustainability lever. What stands in the way is also obvious: thin conversion of effort into profit, too much cash parked in stock, and likely more operational ambiguity than the public narrative admits.

The Recommended Sequence

  1. 01Within 2 weeks: establish weekly cash, stock and collections visibility.
  2. 02Within 30 days: unpack inventory and other current liabilities into real management categories.
  3. 03Within 45 days: run a Focus Day with the actual operating leadership team.
  4. 04Within 60 days: finalise the Accountability Chart and leadership scorecard.
  5. 05Within 90 days: execute the first working-capital Rock and install Level 10 meetings.
