Executive Summary

A decade-old luxury real estate agency that generated MUR 23.7M in revenue and MUR 11.25M net profit in FY2024 — delivered by 35 professionals across six Mauritius locations — now navigating its most consequential transition: acquisition by APTIS Group in May 2025, with its founder stepping back and a new General Manager taking the helm.

BARNES Mauritius is the operating brand of OLOA (Holidays) Ltd (File No. C152041, BRN: C17152041), a private domestic company incorporated on 28 November 2017 in Mahébourg. Founded in 2015 by Outi de Falbaire — who introduced the BARNES International brand licence to the island after 20 years in Mauritian real estate — the company operates as one of Mauritius's top three luxury real estate agencies, with six offices covering Grand Baie, Tamarin, Moka, Belle Mare, Floréal, and Mahébourg. The company holds an exclusive licence to trade under the BARNES International brand, itself a Paris-founded luxury property group (est. 1997) with 150+ offices and 1,500+ employees worldwide.

In May 2025, APTIS Holdings Ltd acquired 100% of OLOA (Holidays) Ltd, replacing the original shareholders with APTIS Group ownership. This is confirmed by the CBRIS filing: as of the annual return filed 12 May 2025, APTIS Holdings Ltd holds 1,000 ordinary shares (100% of issued capital). Simultaneously, four new APTIS directors were appointed on 15 May 2025: Oscar Mamet (CEO), Sabbi Appanna (CFO), Anne Régine de Ravel de l'Argentière (HR Director), and François Colin (new General Manager). The founder, Outi de Falbaire, retained a role as Head of Sales — a deliberate continuity decision to preserve client relationships during the transition.

Financially, FY2024 (the last full year under founder ownership) shows a well-run business: MUR 23.7M in agency fee revenue, MUR 23.7M gross profit (100% margin, as expected for a pure agency), MUR 22.2M in administration costs producing PBT of MUR 11.8M and net profit of MUR 11.25M — a 47.4% net margin. The balance sheet is clean: MUR 8.99M equity (positive and growing), MUR 16.8M current assets (mostly cash and receivables), and only MUR 10.2M in current liabilities. No charges, no long-term debt, no winding-up proceedings. This is a financially healthy, asset-light luxury services business.

Key Finding — Financially Healthy Business in a Critical Cultural Transition
BARNES Mauritius is the most financially healthy entity in this three-company review — positive equity, strong margins, clean balance sheet, no debt, and a profitable operating model. The strategic risk is entirely human: a founder-dependent luxury brand, built on Outi de Falbaire's personal network and brand stewardship over a decade, is being transitioned to a new general manager (François Colin) under APTIS Group corporate governance. The EOS opportunity — rated 7.5/10 — lies in helping the new GM build a structured, people-independent operating system before the luxury client relationships that constitute BARNES Mauritius's primary asset are put at risk by the transition turbulence.

Products and Services Portfolio

BARNES Mauritius operates as a luxury real estate estate agency providing: residential property sales — villas, apartments, beach houses, IRS/PDS/Smart City scheme properties targeting both local and international investors; long-term residential rentals of premium properties; property management services for owners; new property development marketing — acting as sales agent for developers of high-end residential projects; and investment advisory for international buyers seeking Mauritius real estate as a wealth management asset. The MUR 78M turnover cited in the APTIS acquisition announcement (versus the MUR 23.7M in the CBRIS filing) likely reflects gross transaction values channelled through the agency rather than net commission income — a common distinction in estate agency reporting.

The BARNES International brand provides access to a global referral network linking Mauritius luxury buyers to the same brand in Paris, London, Miami, Geneva, Lisbon, Monaco, New York, Los Angeles, and St. Barts — providing a material competitive advantage for cross-border transactions involving international buyers acquiring Mauritius IRS/PDS properties.

Geographic Presence

BARNES Mauritius operates from six island locations, covering all major luxury real estate markets: BARNES Grand Baie (B45 Royal Road, Grand Baie 30501 — North); BARNES Tamarin (Ruisseau Créole, Rivière Noire 90625 — West/South-West); BARNES Moka (Les Allées d'Helvétia, Moka 80840 — Central); BARNES Mahébourg (Coastal Road, Barachois, Mahébourg 50815 — South-East, original headquarters); BARNES Floréal (Floréal — Central Plateau); and BARNES Belle Mare (Coastal Road, Poste de Flacq 41602 — East). A seventh BARNES HQ office appears to be listed at Grand Baie. This geographic coverage represents one of the most comprehensive luxury agency footprints on the island, competing directly with JLL, Knight Frank, and local agencies.

The 2 Numbers That Matter Most
1. Other Income MUR 10.9M = 45.9% of Revenue (Amber — Investigate) — The MUR 10.9M in "other income" is what transforms a thin 3.8% commission margin into a 47.4% net profit margin. Structural Competitive Advantages

1. BARNES International Licence — Global Referral Network: The BARNES International brand connects Mauritius buyers and sellers to a network of 150+ offices and 1,500+ professionals across 20+ countries. For a French or European investor considering a Mauritius IRS property, the ability to deal with the same BARNES brand they know from Paris, London, or Geneva is a decisive trust factor. This licence is the single most important competitive asset BARNES Mauritius holds — and APTIS Holdings must protect the licence relationship with BARNES International head office.

2. Six-Office Island-Wide Presence: BARNES Mauritius covers all six major luxury property zones (North, West, East, South-East, Central) — giving it geographic reach that smaller boutique agencies cannot match. This coverage allows the agency to serve clients buying in multiple zones (e.g., comparing Grand Baie with Belle Mare) within a single agency relationship.

3. 10 Years of Outi de Falbaire's Relationship Capital: The founder's two decades of Mauritius real estate experience and decade of BARNES brand stewardship have built a client book that no new entrant can replicate quickly. Retaining her as Head of Sales through the transition is a strategically correct decision — but the risk of her eventually leaving post-transition (and potentially taking clients with her) remains real.

Industry Trends and Tailwinds

  • Record IRS/PDS/Smart City foreign investment: Mauritius recorded strong foreign direct investment in real estate through 2024–2025, driven by European and South African HNW buyers seeking Mauritius residency via property investment. This directly drives BARNES's primary sales market.
  • BARNES International global network expansion: BARNES International's global growth (described as the #1 luxury real estate brand internationally) increases the referral pipeline from international offices to Mauritius — a structural tailwind that strengthens with every new BARNES International office opened globally.
  • Mauritius economic resilience and fiscal attractiveness: Mauritius's low personal tax rates, absence of capital gains tax, and residency-by-investment scheme continue to attract HNW individuals from France, South Africa, and the broader francophone world — expanding the premium property buyer universe.
  • Luxury rental market growth from Digital Nomad visa: The Mauritius Premium Visa and related schemes attract high-earning international residents needing quality long-term rental accommodation — directly supporting BARNES's rental management portfolio.
  • Integration risk from APTIS acquisition (headwind): The transition from a founder-led boutique to a corporate-owned subsidiary carries retention risk for both agents and clients. If key agents follow Outi de Falbaire out of a corporate structure they find uncongenial, BARNES Mauritius's revenue could erode faster than APTIS can rebuild it.
  • Rising office overhead costs (moderate headwind): Six premium retail locations across Mauritius at luxury property zones carry significant rent and fit-out costs — particularly in Grand Baie and Belle Mare where commercial rents have risen. Post-acquisition cost review is warranted.

EOS Suitability Assessment

EOS is most effective in private companies of 10–250 people experiencing growth complexity — disconnect between vision, execution, and accountability. Each dimension rated 1–10 with evidence from public data and financial analysis.

01 — Organisational Size and Complexity 7 / 10

35 staff across six offices — confirmed in the APTIS acquisition announcement. This places BARNES Mauritius comfortably within the EOS sweet spot (10–250 people). Six separate office locations create genuine management complexity: each office needs day-to-day leadership, performance accountability, and cultural alignment. Post-APTIS acquisition, the headcount and structural complexity are increasing — the new GM, François Colin, is managing a multi-site team he inherited, with a founder stepping back, four new corporate board directors, and 35 individuals whose loyalty and morale are uncertain.

02 — Leadership Openness 7 / 10

The APTIS Group's acquisition of BARNES Mauritius signals strategic ambition and openness to professionalisation — the group appointed BDO as auditor group-wide, installed structured corporate governance (Navitas secretary), and brought in an external GM (François Colin) rather than promoting internally. Oscar Mamet's leadership approach (structured CEO succession, willingness to acquire and integrate) signals openness to framework-based management. The new GM has no legacy culture to defend — he is almost certainly open to installing an operating system as his own leadership mark. Score: 7/10 — high readiness.

03 — Growth Stage and Pain Points 9 / 10

This dimension scores the highest across all three entities reviewed. BARNES Mauritius has just experienced the most disruptive possible event: a full ownership change. The pain points are immediate and severe: Who makes decisions now — the board or the GM? What happens when agents disagree with the new GM's direction? How do Outi de Falbaire and François Colin divide authority in front of clients? What is the new vision — is BARNES Mauritius going to expand, optimise, or simply be integrated? These are textbook EOS-addressable questions that EOS's Accountability Chart, V/TO, and Rocks would answer within a Focus Day.

04 — Current Systems and Processes 5 / 10

Under the founder, systems would have been personalised and relationship-driven — appropriate for a boutique but fragile at scale. The BARNES International brand provides some process standardisation (brand guidelines, client service protocols), but internal management processes (performance management, meeting rhythms, pipeline tracking) are likely founder-dependent. The new GM inherits these systems without the institutional knowledge to know which ones work and which ones only worked because Outi de Falbaire was running them. Score: 5/10 — mid-range, with post-acquisition systems gap emerging.

05 — Company Culture 6 / 10

The BARNES Mauritius culture under Outi de Falbaire was built around luxury service, personal relationships, and French/international aesthetic sensibility — consistent with the BARNES International brand identity. This is a premium, client-first culture. The risk is that corporate APTIS Group culture (distribution, logistics, tobacco heritage) feels alien to a team that identifies with a global luxury brand. The key cultural integration question: can François Colin honour and preserve the BARNES luxury service culture while introducing APTIS Group accountability structures?

06 — Decision-Making Patterns 5 / 10

Under the founder, decisions were likely made centrally by Outi de Falbaire with high consistency. Under the new structure, decision authority is unclear: François Colin as GM has day-to-day authority, but four APTIS board directors can override. Outi de Falbaire as Head of Sales has direct client-facing authority. Six office leaders may have de facto autonomy on local decisions. This ambiguity is the post-acquisition decision-making trap — EOS's Accountability Chart resolves it within the first session.

07 — Vision Clarity 4 / 10

The acquisition announcement articulated a high-level vision ("APTIS Group enters the luxury real estate market") but says nothing about the specific 3-year plan for BARNES Mauritius. What does it look like in 2028 under APTIS ownership? Expand to 10 offices? Reach MUR 150M turnover? Add a property development arm? None of this is documented. The 35 BARNES staff need to know where the company is going, and that question has no answer yet. EOS V/TO would create it in two sessions. Score: 4/10 — critical gap.

08 — Meeting Culture 4 / 10

Under the founder, meeting culture in a six-office luxury agency would have been ad hoc and relationship-driven — appropriate for that phase. Under new corporate ownership, there is no evidence of structured weekly meeting rhythms. The new GM needs a cadence to manage six office leaders without micromanaging — the EOS L10 and Scorecard combination is precisely designed for this. Without it, François Colin will spend his first year firefighting rather than building.

Highest-Priority EOS Tools for BARNES Mauritius

Accountability Chart

The post-acquisition leadership structure (new GM, founder as Head of Sales, six office leaders, four corporate directors) has no documented Accountability Chart. Who owns the P&L for each office? Who manages agent performance? An Accountability Chart built in the first Focus Day would answer these questions and establish François Colin's authority clearly.

Vision/Traction Organizer (V/TO)

35 staff do not know what BARNES Mauritius looks like in 3 years under APTIS ownership. A V/TO session with François Colin, Oscar Mamet, and Outi de Falbaire would crystallise the 10-Year Target (e.g., #1 luxury agency in Mauritius), 3-Year Picture (e.g., 8 offices, MUR 40M in commission), and 1-Year Plan — giving the entire team a destination to work towards.

Scorecard

Six offices generating different transaction volumes and commission rates need a weekly Scorecard tracking: listings added, properties under offer, completions, commission earned, and rental enquiries by office. This gives the new GM real-time visibility without requiring six separate management meetings, and surfaces underperforming offices quickly.

Rocks (90-Day Priorities)

The first three Rocks write themselves: (1) complete Accountability Chart and office-level seat assignments; (2) understand and protect the MUR 10.9M "other income" revenue stream; (3) complete HR integration of 35 staff into APTIS Group framework. 90-day focus prevents the new GM from trying to fix everything simultaneously.

Level 10 Meeting (L10)

A weekly 90-minute L10 with the six office leaders, using the Scorecard, Rocks, and Issues List, would give François Colin the management cadence to run a six-office network without being in six different locations. This is the single most impactful EOS tool for a multi-site GM who needs to build a unified leadership team.

People Analyser

With 35 inherited staff and a founder stepping back into a narrower role, the People Analyser (Core Values + GWC) would identify which agents and office leaders are right seats under the new vision — and which relationships need to be managed carefully to retain top performers and prevent talent exodus in the first 12 months.

The Bottom Line

BARNES Mauritius is the most valuable and most precarious entity in the APTIS group simultaneously. Valuable because it generates extraordinary returns (ROA 58.7%, ROE 125.2%, 47.4% net margin) from a globally recognised brand in a booming market. Precarious because its value resides primarily in human capital — Outi de Falbaire's relationships and the team she built — not in hard assets. The acquisition was well-timed and well-structured (founder retention as Head of Sales is the right call). The next 12 months will determine whether APTIS successfully transitions this boutique to a scalable luxury agency platform or loses the human capital that made the acquisition valuable. EOS, deployed through the new GM, is the operating system that makes the former outcome more likely.
